Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 16th September 2026
Order Books Tell Divergent Stories as Foreign Flows Hit Record Two-Way Levels
The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange delivered a session of remarkable contrasts on Wednesday. Equity turnover moderated to TZS 10.18 billion, the indices pulled back from recent highs, yet foreign participation surged to levels not seen in months — with buying at 51.39% and selling at 61.90% of turnover. Behind these headline numbers lies a market where different stocks are telling very different stories through their order books. Some are offer-heavy, suggesting further consolidation; others are balanced, suggesting stability. Understanding these divergences is essential for any investor navigating this market.

1. Market Snapshot
The session remained in the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 10.18 billion, though this represents a 40% decline from Tuesday's block-heavy TZS 16.86 billion. The deal count held steady at 3,240, confirming that everyday investors remained engaged.
Foreign flows hit unprecedented two-way levels: Foreign buying jumped to 51.39% of turnover (TZS 5.23 billion), while foreign selling rose to 61.90% (TZS 6.30 billion). This is the highest level of two-way foreign participation in recent memory. The net outflow was approximately TZS 1.07 billion. This pattern — heavy buying alongside heavy selling — indicates active portfolio rebalancing rather than a one-way exit. Foreign institutions are repositioning across counters, not abandoning the market.
The DSEI and TSI both pulled back from recent highs. The DSEI closed at 4,663.24, down 32.87 points, while the TSI slipped to 10,380.73. This is a normal consolidation after several sessions of gains.
Bond market activity remained elevated at TZS 17.83 billion, with the 10-year 11.25% bond trading TZS 12.41 billion at yields of 10.67% to 10.77% tax-free.

2. Top Movers
Gainers: VODA (+3.5%), PAL (+3.3%), MCB (+2.6%), DCB (+2.3%), TCC (+0.7%).
Losers: TCCL (–5.1%), TOL (–4.2%), NMB (–1.4%), AFRIPRISE (–0.8%), CRDB (–0.7%).
VODA was the standout performer among the large caps, rising 3.5% to 1,190 after absorbing a 1.55-million-share block trade. The order book is now balanced: 5,634 bids versus 5,247 offers. Supply and demand are in equilibrium.
TCCL fell 5.1% to 3,700, giving back recent gains. TOL dropped 4.2% to 1,820, continuing its volatile pattern.
CRDB eased 0.7% to 2,920. The order book is extremely offer-heavy: 588,938 offers versus just 30,868 bids — a ratio of 19 to 1. This suggests the market is still digesting supply from recent block trades.
NMB fell 1.4% to 2,150 after absorbing a 900,000-share block trade. The order book is even more offer-heavy: 450,110 offers versus 14,196 bids — a ratio of 32 to 1. The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending.

3. In Focus: Reading Order Books and the 5% Daily Price Band
Wednesday's session provided a powerful lesson in reading order books — the lists of outstanding buy and sell orders that sit beneath every stock's price.

What does this mean for everyday investors?
When a stock closes with a balanced order book, it means buyers and sellers are roughly equal in number. The price is likely to be stable in the near term.
When a stock closes with offers far exceeding bids, it means there is more supply than demand. Sellers are competing to exit, and buyers can afford to be patient. This suggests the stock may continue to drift lower until the excess supply is absorbed.
The lesson is simple: pay attention to the order book, not just the price. A stock that falls on heavy volume with a balanced book may be near a bottom. A stock that falls with an offer-heavy book may have further to go.

4. Bond Market: Institutions Still Locking in Duration
Government bond turnover was TZS 17.83 billion on Wednesday, down 33% from Tuesday's TZS 26.61 billion but still elevated. The most liquid instruments were:
10-year 11.25% bond (Jul 2026): TZS 12.41 billion at 106.54% to 107.17%, yielding 10.67% to 10.77%
10-year 11.44% bond (Sep 2021): TZS 5.00 billion at 114.58%, yielding 9.11%
20-year 15.49% bond (Sep 2020): TZS 0.20 billion at 100.00% to 137.23%, yielding 11.02% to 15.68%
The 10-year 11.44% bond traded at 9.11% — the tightest yield on record for that maturity. The 10-year 11.25% benchmark, which is the current issue, traded at yields of 10.67% to 10.77% tax-free.
What does this mean for everyday investors?
The bond market continues to attract significant institutional demand. Pension funds and insurance companies are locking in long-dated, tax-free yields before they compress further. The 9.11% yield on the 10-year bond is the lowest on record, confirming that the era of double-digit yields on shorter maturities is ending.
However, the 20-year 15.49% bond still offers over 11% tax-free at the higher end of its range. For those seeking predictable, long-term income, this remains an attractive option.
The simultaneous strength of both the bond and equity markets tells us that this is not a one-way rotation. Institutions are deploying capital across both asset classes. A balanced approach — some capital in bonds for stability, some in quality equities for growth — can work well in this environment.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate
Near-Term (Mid-September):
CRDB closed at 2,920 with an extremely offer-heavy order book. The 588,938-share offer wall will likely cap any rally in the near term. A pullback to 2,870 or below would represent a natural pause.
VODA closed at 1,190 with a balanced order book after absorbing a 1.55-million-share block. The stock appears to have found equilibrium. The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst.
NMB closed at 2,150 with the most offer-heavy book on the board (32:1 offers to bids). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted.
TCC eased 0.7% to 13,450. The stock continues to trade in its pre-dividend window.
TCCL fell 5.1% to 3,700. The stock remains volatile; avoid chasing.
Medium-Term (Q3 2026):
Foreign participation hit record two-way levels on Wednesday. If this continues, it could provide additional liquidity and support for the market.
The bond market continues to attract TZS 17-26 billion in duration demand at 9% to 11% tax-free. Watch for a contraction in bond turnover below TZS 10 billion for two consecutive sessions while equity turnover remains elevated — that would signal a clearer rotation.
Long-Term:
The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, the foreign flow reversal, and now the record two-way foreign participation — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.
