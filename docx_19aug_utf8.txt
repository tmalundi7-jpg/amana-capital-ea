3. In Focus: Block Trades and the 5% Daily Price Band
Wednesday’s block trades — including 150,000 CRDB shares and 253,019 KCB shares — were absorbed quietly, with the broader market rising. This is a testament to the depth of local institutional liquidity. A block trade is a privately negotiated transaction between two large institutions, reported on the prearranged board and executed away from the open market. It allows a seller to exit a large position without flooding the order book, and it gives the buyer a stable entry point. For the everyday investor, a block trade is not a signal to buy or sell; it is simply part of the market’s daily mechanics.
The 5% daily price band also plays a crucial role in keeping trading orderly. On Wednesday, CRDB closed at 2,690. On Thursday, 20 August, the stock cannot trade at any price outside a band of ±5% around that level. The limits are:
Upper limit: 2,690 × 1.05 = 2,824.50
Lower limit: 2,690 × 0.95 = 2,555.50
Any order to buy above 2,824.50 or to sell below 2,555.50 will be automatically rejected by the DSE’s trading system. This mechanism ensures that even the largest trades do not produce panicdriven price swings.

4. Bond Market: Steady Demand for LongTerm Paper
Government bond turnover was a healthy TZS 8.94 billion, with the 25year 13.25% bond continuing to attract the most interest. Institutions bought this paper at prices ranging from about 114% to 125% of face value, locking in yields of roughly 10.5% to 13.4%, all free of withholding tax. The 20year 15.49% highcoupon bond also saw notable activity. The bond market remains a deep, liquid alternative for pension funds and insurance companies seeking predictable, taxfree income, and its continued strength alongside a rising equity market underscores the depth of local capital.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate
NearTerm (August):
The market has built a solid base above 4,250. With local buying absorbing foreign sales comfortably, further gradual gains are possible, though the pace may remain uneven.
NMB is at an alltime high. The exdividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 dividend is overdue and could be announced at any time. When it is, the share price may adjust downward by an amount roughly equal to the dividend — a normal, mechanical process.
CRDB held the 2,690 level after several sessions of consolidation, confirming that buying interest remains at these prices. A sustained move above 2,720 would signal a stronger recovery.
VODA continues to trade near 1,030, with support visible around 1,010 and resistance near 1,040. The stock appears to be stabilising after its lateJuly surge.
MediumTerm (Q3 2026):
The Bank of Tanzania’s steady policy stance remains supportive of both equities and bonds. Any change in the benchmark interest rate would be the most significant macroeconomic event.
The implementation of the government’s infrastructure spending will directly benefit cement, construction, and logistics companies, including TPCC and TCCL.
LongTerm:The DSE’s ability to advance despite foreign selling in most recent sessions is a powerful indicator of structural change. A market that can rely on its own domestic capital is far more resilient than one that must wait for international investors to return. For the patient, longterm investor, this is the foundation on which sustainable wealth is built.

7. Considerations for a MultiYear Framework
A market that rises on a day when foreign investors are net sellers, and that handles three block trades without disruption, is a market that has come of age. The handover of ownership from foreign to local hands — completed over the past two months — has created a more stable, more predictable exchange. For the multiyear investor, this transformation is the real story: the DSE is no longer a market that panics when international capital leaves; it is a market that stands on its own two feet.
