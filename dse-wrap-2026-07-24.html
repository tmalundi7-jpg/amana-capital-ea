Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 24 July 2026

VODA Rally Caps a Week of Recovery as Foreign Sellers Stay Silent

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange closed the week on a strong note, with the All-Share Index rising 37 points to 4,150.14 — its best level since early June. A late-week surge in VODA and continued buying in TBL lifted the market, while foreign investors, who once dominated daily selling, recorded zero sales for the second time this week. The session provided a clear signal that the market has absorbed the historic July handover and is now being driven by local confidence and selective international buying.

Turnover returned to a more normal, unhurried pace, free from the enormous block trades that characterised the middle of the month. The jump in the number of deals to 2,639 is a healthy sign: while large institutional activity has eased, smaller investors and local funds remain active, providing a broad base for the market.

Foreign participation was once again encouraging. International buyers accounted for 18.5% of turnover, while foreign selling registered zero for the second time in four sessions. The persistent foreign exit that dominated the first half of 2026 is now firmly behind us. International capital is not yet flooding back, but it is quietly and steadily returning.

3. In Focus: VODA’s Rally and the Discipline of Taking a Profit

VODA’s climb to 830 — a gain of 30 shillings from its recent base — highlights an important principle for every investor: a profit that is not locked in is only a number on a screen. The stock has risen on the expectation of a positive dividend announcement. When that announcement finally comes, there is no guarantee the price will continue to rise. Often, markets “buy the rumour and sell the fact,” meaning the price may already reflect the good news. Taking a profit into strength, and then considering a re-entry at a lower level, is a disciplined approach that protects gains and keeps capital working.

This does not mean investors should sell everything the moment a stock goes up. It means having a plan — a pre-determined price at which you will exit, based on your own analysis and goals. The 5% daily price band (explained below) provides a framework for setting realistic, executable targets.

4. Today’s Concept: The 5% Daily Price Band — Your Tool for Realistic Orders

On Friday, CRDB closed at 2,690. That means on Monday, 27 July, the stock cannot be traded at any price outside a band of ±5% around that closing level. The limits are:

  • Upper limit: 2,690 × 1.05 = 2,824.50
  • Lower limit: 2,690 × 0.95 = 2,555.50

Any order to buy above 2,824.50 or to sell below 2,555.50 will be automatically rejected by the DSE’s trading system. This rule protects the market from extreme, panic-driven swings and forces prices to move in an orderly fashion, even after large block trades.
