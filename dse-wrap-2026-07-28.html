Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 28 July 2026

NMB Scales New Peak as VODA Rally Continues and Market Consolidates

Tuesday’s session on the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange saw the market settle into a calmer rhythm after the block-trade surge of the previous day. The All-Share Index rose 21 points to 4,163.44, driven by another strong advance in VODA and a fresh all-time high for NMB. With no new block trades recorded, the day provided a clear picture of steady, broad-based participation and offered an important lesson in how markets absorb large institutional moves.

1. Market Snapshot

Turnover returned to a normal, sustainable level, free from the exceptional block trades that had inflated Monday’s figures. The number of deals, while lower, remained healthy at over 4,300, indicating that small and mid-sized investors continued to trade actively.

Foreign participation also normalised. The sharp drop in foreign buying and the presence of modest selling — accounting for 16.64% of turnover — largely reflects profit-taking after the previous day’s strong rally in VODA and other counters. This is a normal, two-way flow, and a far cry from the sustained one-sided foreign exodus that characterised the first half of 2026.

NMB powered to yet another record high, touching 17,220 during the day before settling at 17,130. The stock has now gained 14.6% in recent weeks as the market positions ahead of the company’s expected ex-dividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 per share dividend. VODA extended its powerful rally, adding another 8.3% after Monday’s 3.5-million-share block trade, bringing its two-day gain to nearly 22%. The stock closed at 980, a level last seen before the June correction. CRDB, meanwhile, eased slightly to 2,650, continuing its prolonged post-handover consolidation.

3. In Focus: Understanding VODA’s Surge and the 5% Price Band

VODA’s extraordinary two-day surge — up 22% from 805 to 980 — is a textbook example of how large institutional activity can drive a stock while a key exchange rule keeps trading orderly.

On Monday, a single pre-arranged block trade of 3.5 million VODA shares, worth roughly TZS 3.2 billion, changed hands between two large institutions. By executing the trade away from the public order book, the parties avoided disrupting the normal market, but the size of the transaction signalled strong demand and sparked a wave of follow-on buying. On Tuesday, the stock continued to climb as other investors reacted to the news, closing at 980.

Throughout this rapid move, the DSE’s daily price band — a rule that limits how far most stocks can move in a single session — ensured that the price did not spike uncontrollably. For most actively traded equities, the daily band is ±5% of the previous day’s closing price. Because Monday’s close was 905, the maximum allowed move on Tuesday was to 950.25. The stock opened higher but stayed within this band.

Any order to buy above the upper limit or sell below the lower limit will be automatically rejected by the exchange’s trading system. This safety net protects investors and ensures that even the most dramatic moves are contained within a predictable framework. Before placing any order, it is good practice to check the previous day’s close and calculate the band — a small habit that prevents placing unexecutable orders.

4. Bond Market: Institutions Lock in Attractive Yields

Government bond turnover doubled to TZS 24.9 billion, led by the 20-year 12.25% bond and a large 20-year 12.00% bond. Yields on these long-dated instruments remain attractive at around 10.1% to 11.7%, entirely free of withholding tax. The simultaneous strength in bonds and equities confirms that local institutional liquidity is deep enough to support both asset classes comfortably.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

  • Near-Term (Late July / Early August):
    VODA’s sharp rally has been driven by a large block trade and the market’s expectation of a strong dividend announcement. When that news eventually arrives, the stock could see further movement, but it is worth remembering that much of the good news may already be reflected in the price.
    NMB has reached a fresh all-time high. The ex-dividend date for its approved TZS 610.15 dividend has not yet been published but is expected very soon. Historically, share prices can adjust downward after the books close for a dividend.
    CRDB remains in a tight consolidation range. With the historic ownership transfer now behind it, the stock is likely to trade on its underlying business fundamentals.
  • Medium-Term (Q3 2026):
    The Bank of Tanzania’s steady policy stance continues to provide a supportive backdrop for both equities and bonds. Any change in the benchmark interest rate would be the most significant macroeconomic event.
    The implementation of the government’s infrastructure spending will directly affect cement, construction, and logistics companies.
  • Long-Term:
    Long-Term: The market’s ability to absorb a 3.5-million-share block trade and then continue rising the following day is a testament to its deepening liquidity and local ownership base. This structural resilience is the foundation on which patient, long-term investors build wealth.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

A block trade of 3.5 million VODA shares, a new all-time high in NMB, and a market that continues to rise even as foreign selling normalises — these are the signs of a maturing exchange. The overhang of the first half of 2026 has been absorbed, and what remains is a market anchored by patient local institutions and selective international participation. For the multi-year investor, this stability is not a headline; it is the quiet, structural improvement that turns a volatile market into a reliable engine of long-term returns.
