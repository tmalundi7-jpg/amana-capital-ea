Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 29 July 2026

VODA Breakout Continues as Profit-Takers Emerge and NMB Notches Another Record

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange extended its winning streak to a sixth consecutive session, with the All-Share Index reaching a fresh post-correction high of 4,173.13. A heavy block trade in NMB and another surge in VODA dominated the day’s activity, while a notable increase in foreign selling provided a timely lesson in how markets absorb sharp rallies.

Turnover climbed back above TZS 8 billion, powered by a 162,900-share NMB block and heavy normal-board activity in VODA, which saw over 303,000 shares change hands. The number of deals held comfortably above 4,000, confirming strong participation from retail and local institutional investors.

The sharp rise in foreign selling — to 40.15% of turnover — was not a sign of a renewed exodus. It was concentrated in the stocks that have rallied the most over the past week, particularly VODA. When a share price climbs rapidly, it is entirely normal for institutional holders to lock in profits. Local buyers comfortably absorbed the flow, as shown by the rising index.

3. In Focus: Profit-Taking and the 5% Price Band

The increase in foreign selling on Wednesday provides a valuable illustration of how markets work. After VODA surged by nearly 30% in just three trading days, some of the large institutional holders who bought at lower prices naturally chose to sell and lock in their profits. This is neither a cause for alarm nor a signal that the stock is about to collapse. It is simply the normal functioning of a liquid market.

For the everyday investor, the key takeaway is that sharp rallies attract sellers, and the price cannot rise indefinitely. A disciplined approach means having a plan — knowing at what price you would consider taking a profit, rather than hoping for an unlimited gain.

This is where the 5% daily price band becomes a practical tool. It limits how far a stock can move in a single session, providing a natural framework for setting realistic orders. Because VODA closed at 1,035 on Wednesday, the maximum it can rise or fall on Thursday is ±5% around that closing price.

Any order to buy above the upper limit or sell below the lower limit will be automatically rejected by the DSE’s trading system. This rule ensures that even during periods of intense activity, price movements remain orderly and investors are protected from extreme, panic-driven swings.

4. Bond Market: Steady Institutional Demand

Government bond turnover was steady at TZS 26.9 billion, led by the 20-year 12.25% bond and the 15-year 10.75% bond. Yields on these long-dated instruments remain at 10.6–11.7%, entirely free of withholding tax. The continued appetite for bonds alongside a rising equity market confirms that local institutional liquidity is deep enough to support both asset classes comfortably.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

  • Near-Term (Early August):
    VODA has experienced a remarkable rally, but the surge in foreign selling suggests that some of the buying pressure is now being met with profit-taking. The upcoming dividend announcement remains the key catalyst, but it is worth remembering that much of the good news is often reflected in the price before the formal statement.
    NMB is at an all-time high. The ex-dividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 dividend is still pending and could be announced at any time. Historical patterns suggest the share price may adjust downward once the books close, as the entitlement to the dividend is removed.
    CRDB continues to consolidate, digesting the enormous ownership transfer of July. The underlying business remains solid, and the stock is likely to trade on fundamentals rather than block-driven events.
  • Medium-Term (Q3 2026):
    The Bank of Tanzania’s stable policy stance remains supportive. Any shift in the benchmark interest rate would be the most significant macroeconomic event for both equities and bonds.
    The implementation of the government’s infrastructure spending will directly affect cement, construction, and logistics companies.
  • Long-Term:
    The market’s ability to absorb a 30% rally in VODA without disruption, and to continue rising even as foreign investors take profits, is a powerful signal of its growing maturity. A market anchored by patient domestic institutions is a more reliable foundation for long-term wealth creation than one that depends on international capital flows.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

The sight of foreign institutions taking profits on a 30% rally, while local buyers comfortably absorb every share, is a defining image of the DSE’s new era. The handover of CRDB ownership in July marked the structural low point of foreign exit; today’s tape marks the point at which international capital is once again actively trading, not fleeing. For the multi-year investor, these are the quiet, structural improvements that turn a volatile frontier market into a reliable engine of long-term returns.
