Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 12th August 2026

Market Regains Footing as CRDB Rises and Local Investors Dominate Trading

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange recovered some of the previous day’s losses on Wednesday, with the All-Share Index climbing back above 4,177. Equity turnover rose to TZS 4.83 billion, while foreign participation remained almost entirely absent. The market’s direction continues to be set by local pension funds, institutions, and retail investors — a defining feature of August’s trading.

1. Market Snapshot

Turnover improved despite a dip in the number of shares traded, indicating that a few larger orders — rather than many small ones — drove the session. A pre-arranged block trade of 19,185 NMB shares on the pre-arranged board contributed to the day’s activity, while normal-board trading remained steady across the banking and industrial counters.

Foreign participation stayed minimal. International buyers accounted for just 0.67% of equity turnover, and sellers 1.22%. The market is now almost entirely locally driven, with domestic institutions providing both the floor and the buying interest that has kept the DSE stable through August.

2. Top Movers

Gainers: SWIS (+10.7%), NMG (+3.7%), DSE (+2.1%), KCB (+1.6%), NICO (+1.4%). Losers: MCB (–4.2%), PAL (–1.6%), TCCL (–1.4%), MKCB (–1.3%).

CRDB edged up to 2,620, recovering some of the ground lost in recent sessions. The stock has now spent several days building a base in the 2,600–2,620 range, suggesting that selling pressure has been absorbed and that patient buyers are willing to defend these levels. VODA was unchanged at 1,010, consolidating after its late-July surge. NMB held firm at 17,600, just below its record high, supported by the block trade and steady institutional demand.

Among smaller stocks, SWIS jumped more than 10% on light volume — a reminder that such sharp moves in less liquid names can be caused by a single transaction and should be interpreted with care. On the downside, MCB eased further after the previous day’s heavy trading, while PAL and TCCL posted modest declines.

3. In Focus: CRDB’s Quiet Recovery and the Importance of Daily Price Limits

CRDB’s rise to 2,620 on Wednesday may appear modest, but it is significant. For several sessions, the market’s largest bank has been testing the 2,600 level, and each time it has found support. The heavy institutional selling that marked July and early August appears to have run its course, and the stock is now trading in a narrow range that suggests a new equilibrium is being established.

One of the reasons this process has been orderly is the 5% daily price band. On Wednesday, CRDB closed at 2,620. That means on Thursday, the stock cannot trade at any price outside a band of ±5% around that closing level. The limits are:

Upper limit: 2,620 × 1.05 = 2,751

Lower limit: 2,620 × 0.95 = 2,489

Any order to buy above 2,751 or to sell below 2,489 will be automatically rejected by the DSE’s trading system. This rule applies to all actively traded stocks, with some smaller counters having a wider 15% band.

By limiting how far a price can move in a single day, the exchange prevents the kind of panic selling or speculative spikes that can occur in less regulated markets. For everyday investors, this is a valuable safeguard: it keeps trading orderly, gives everyone time to digest new information, and ensures that limit orders are set within realistic boundaries.

4. Bond Market: Activity Cools After Tuesday’s Surge

Government bond turnover fell back to TZS 17.99 billion, a sharp decline from Tuesday’s exceptional TZS 41.55 billion. The busiest instrument was again the 25-year 13.25% bond, which traded at prices around 114–122% of face value, giving yields of roughly 10.7% to 11.5%. Long-dated paper remains popular with pension funds and insurance companies seeking tax-free income that can match their long-term liabilities.

Corporate bond activity was light, with a 5-year 12.00% bond being the only notable trade. Overall, the bond market remains a deep and reliable alternative for investors seeking predictable income, even as equity trading continues to dominate daily headlines.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (August):

CRDB is stabilising near the 2,620 level. The heavy supply that weighed on the stock appears to have been absorbed, and a gradual recovery may develop if local buying remains consistent.

NMB is at its all-time high. The ex-dividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 dividend has still not been announced. When it is, the share price may adjust downward by an amount roughly equal to the dividend — a normal mechanical process.

VODA has settled near the 1,010 area. The stock may continue to trade sideways as investors assess the company’s full-year results and the longer-term growth of its mobile money business.

Medium-Term (Q3 2026):

The Bank of Tanzania’s steady policy stance remains supportive. Any change in the benchmark interest rate would be the most significant macroeconomic event for both equities and bonds.

The implementation of the government’s infrastructure spending will directly benefit cement, construction, and logistics companies.

Long-Term: The quiet, locally driven sessions of August are a sign of a market that has absorbed its historic ownership transfers and found a new equilibrium. For the patient investor, this stability is the foundation on which long-term wealth is built.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

A market that can absorb a large block trade without disruption, and where local investors account for virtually all activity, is a market that has reached a new stage of maturity. The ownership transfers of July are complete; the drama has passed. What remains is a steady, domestically anchored exchange where patient capital can compound quietly — a far stronger foundation for long-term wealth than a market dependent on the moods of foreign money.
