Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 13th August 2026

Market Rebounds Sharply as CRDB Surges and Local Investors Absorb Foreign Selling

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange delivered one of its strongest daily performances in weeks on Thursday, with the AllShare Index jumping more than 50 points to 4,228.80. The advance was led by a broadbased recovery in the banking sector, headlined by CRDB, which climbed over 3% to close at 2,700. The session was particularly notable because the market rose despite a return of significant foreign selling — a clear sign that domestic demand is now deep enough to absorb institutional outflows without undermining prices.

Equity turnover eased slightly, but the number of shares traded and the number of deals both rose, indicating broad participation across a wider range of counters. The index’s strong gain on lower total turnover suggests that a few large institutional moves, combined with steady retail interest, were enough to lift the market.

Foreign selling spiked to 37.68% of turnover — the highest in weeks — yet the market rose. This is the signature of a locally anchored exchange: when international investors reduce positions, Tanzanian pension funds, insurance companies, and individual investors step in to absorb the supply. The result is a market that can withstand external pressure without panicking.

CRDB was the day’s most significant mover among the large caps, rallying 80 shillings to 2,700. This marked the stock’s strongest singleday gain in weeks and lifted it well clear of the 2,600 support level that had been tested repeatedly. KCB also rose strongly, climbing to 1,970 on heavy volume, while NMB edged up to 17,650 — just below its alltime high.

Among smaller stocks, TCCL jumped 8% on moderate volume, and PAL posted a doubledigit percentage gain on very light trading, a reminder that such moves in less liquid counters can be driven by a single transaction and should be treated with caution.

3. In Focus: The 5% Daily Price Band — A Safety Net That Works Even During a Rally

Thursday’s sharp rise in CRDB — from 2,620 to 2,700 — occurred within the boundaries of a rule that protects every DSE investor: the 5% daily price band. This mechanism limits how far a stock can move in a single trading session, ensuring that even the most powerful rallies or selloffs occur in an orderly manner.

Because CRDB closed at 2,700 on Thursday, it cannot trade on Friday at any price outside a band of ±5% around that level. The limits are:

Upper limit:  2,700 × 1.05 = 2,835

Lower limit:  2,700 × 0.95 = 2,565

Any order to buy above 2,835 or to sell below 2,565 will be automatically rejected by the DSE’s trading system. The same rule applies to all actively traded stocks, with a handful of smaller counters operating under a wider 15% band.

Understanding this band is essential for setting realistic limit orders. It helps you avoid placing orders that the exchange will automatically reject, and it provides a natural framework for thinking about where prices are likely to move next. On a day like Thursday, the band also demonstrates how a market can rise sharply without spinning out of control.

4. Bond Market: Steady Demand for LongTerm Paper

Government bond turnover was a healthy TZS 14.47 billion, with the 25year 13.25% bond continuing to attract the most interest. Institutions bought this paper at prices ranging from about 111% to 124% of face value, locking in yields of roughly 10.6% to 12.0% — all free of withholding tax. The 20year 12.25% bond also traded actively, with yields around 11.0–11.5%. The bond market remains a deep, liquid alternative for pension funds and insurance companies seeking predictable income, and its continued strength alongside a rising equity market underscores the depth of local capital.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (August):

  • The strong rebound in CRDB, KCB, and NMB suggests that the market has absorbed the supply overhang that weighed on the banking sector in recent weeks. If local buying remains consistent, further gradual gains are possible.
  • NMB is now just below its all-time high. The ex-dividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 dividend has not yet been published, but it is expected soon. When it is announced, the share price may adjust downward by an amount roughly equal to the dividend — a normal, mechanical process that investors should understand in advance.
  • VODA continues to trade near the 1,010 level, with buying interest visible on dips. The stock appears to have found a stable range as investors digest the company’s full-year results and the longer-term potential of its mobile money business.

Medium-Term (Q3 2026):

  • The Bank of Tanzania’s steady policy stance remains supportive of both equities and bonds. Any change in the benchmark interest rate would be the most significant macroeconomic event for the market.
  • The implementation of the government’s infrastructure spending will directly benefit cement, construction, and logistics companies.

Long-Term:
The market’s ability to rise by more than 50 points on a day when foreign selling accounted for over a third of turnover is a powerful signal. It tells us that the DSE has truly come of age: it is now a market anchored by patient domestic capital, capable of weathering international flows without losing its footing. For the long-term investor, that resilience is the foundation on which lasting wealth is built.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

A 50point rise on a day of heavy foreign selling is the clearest evidence yet that the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange has achieved a new level of maturity. The ownership transfers of July are complete, and the market is now driven by local institutions and retail investors who are committed for the long term. For the multiyear investor, this quiet transformation is the real story — a market that can absorb external shocks and continue to compound wealth for those with patience and discipline.
