Daily DSE Wrap | Monday, 17th August 2026

NMB Touches 18,000 as Local Investors Absorb Continued Foreign Selling

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange opened the new week on a positive note, with the All-Share Index advancing to 4,248.98 — its highest level in over a month. The session was highlighted by a large pre-arranged trade in DCB, a fresh all-time intraday high for NMB, and continued evidence that local investors are fully absorbing whatever foreign institutions choose to sell. The market's resilience tells a clear story: the ownership of Tanzania's listed companies is now firmly anchored at home.

Equity turnover eased from Friday's block-driven surge, but the number of deals jumped by more than a third — a clear indication that retail and local institutional investors were active across a broad range of counters. A single pre-arranged block trade in DCB, covering 760,000 shares, accounted for a large part of the day's volume and pushed the stock up by 14%.

Foreign selling picked up again, reaching 32.5% of equity turnover, yet the index rose. This is the signature of a market that has moved beyond its reliance on international capital: local buyers are absorbing every foreign share without panic, and prices are grinding steadily higher.

Among the larger stocks, NMB touched 18,000 during the session — a fresh all-time high — before easing back to close at 17,710. VODA regained the 1,030 level, while CRDB held near 2,690. The broader market's advance, despite the foreign selling, was supported by steady gains in NICO and other mid-sized financial counters.

3. In Focus: DCB’s Block Trade and the Power of Local Demand

The 760,000-share DCB block trade is the largest single transaction in the stock in recent memory. Block trades like this are privately negotiated between large institutions and reported on the pre-arranged board, away from the open market. Their purpose is to allow a seller to offload a large position without flooding the order book and causing a sharp price collapse.

On Monday, the buyer — almost certainly a local institution — absorbed the entire block at a price that pushed DCB up 14%. That is a powerful statement: even in a smaller bank, there is enough domestic capital to absorb institutional supply and move the price higher. For the everyday investor, the lesson is not to chase the spike, but to understand that the market's foundation is now built on patient, local hands. When foreign sellers appear, they are no longer met with panic; they are met with buyers who are in no hurry to sell again.

This same dynamic played out in the broader market. Foreign selling accounted for nearly a third of equity turnover, yet the All-Share Index rose. The DSE has reached a stage where international flows are just one part of the daily mix, not the dominant force they once were.

4. Bond Market: A Quiet Day After a Busy August

Government bond turnover fell sharply to just TZS 1.27 billion — the lowest in weeks. Only the 25-year 13.25% bond saw meaningful activity, trading at yields of roughly 10.7% to 11.6%, entirely free of withholding tax. After a period of heavy institutional accumulation in the bond market, institutions have temporarily stepped back. This is a natural lull rather than a change in the underlying demand for long-term, tax-free income. The bond market remains a deep and reliable alternative for investors seeking predictable returns.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (August):

The market's upward drift continues, supported by domestic buying. The large block trades and foreign selling are being absorbed without difficulty, which suggests that any pullbacks are likely to be shallow.

NMB has reached a new all-time high. The ex-dividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 dividend is still pending and could be announced at any time. When it is, the share price may adjust downward by an amount roughly equal to the dividend — a normal, mechanical process.

VODA appears to have settled into a stable range between roughly 1,010 and 1,060, providing a predictable trading band.

CRDB continues to consolidate near the 2,690 level, with support around 2,670 and resistance near 2,710.

Medium-Term (Q3 2026):

The Bank of Tanzania's steady policy stance remains supportive of both equities and bonds. Any change in the benchmark interest rate would be the most significant macroeconomic event.

The implementation of the government's infrastructure spending will directly benefit cement, construction, and logistics companies.

Long-Term: The DSE's ability to advance while foreign investors are net sellers in four of the last five sessions is a powerful signal of structural change. A market that can rely on its own domestic capital is far more resilient than one that must wait for international investors to return. For the patient, long-term investor, this is the foundation on which sustainable wealth is built.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

A market that rises while foreign investors are net sellers is no longer a market dependent on the moods of international capital. The handover of ownership to local institutions, completed over the past two months, has created a more stable, more predictable exchange. For the multi-year investor, this transformation is the real story — a market that can stand on its own two feet and compound wealth for those with patience and discipline.
