Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 28th August 2026

NMB Dominates as Foreign Sellers Hand the Market to Local Investors

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange closed the week with a session that told a powerful story about the changing face of Tanzania's capital markets. Equity turnover reached TZS 10.20 billion, still elevated by recent standards, but the real headline was NMB. The bank's post-split shares traded more than 4 million times on the normal board alone, accounting for over 83% of the day's equity turnover. Foreign investors sold heavily — 65% of all turnover came from foreign selling — yet the All-Share Index still climbed to a new high. This is the clearest sign yet that local capital has taken firm control of the market.

1. Market Snapshot

Turnover remained firmly in the HIGH regime at TZS 10.20 billion, though it halved from Thursday's exceptional TZS 21.59 billion — a level driven largely by a single block trade in NMB. What makes Friday's session remarkable is that the deal count actually rose by 18.5% to 6,752, the highest in recent memory. This tells us that while the total value traded was lower, participation was broader. More investors were actively buying and selling, just in smaller amounts.

Foreign selling surged to 65.01% of turnover — a dramatic shift from Thursday's 21.68% and a level not seen in years. The concentration is almost entirely in NMB, where a foreign institution or institutions appear to be exiting their post-split positions. Yet the DSEI rose 59 points to 4,444.48, a new post-split high. The Tanzania Share Index also climbed to 9,924.23. Local investors are absorbing the foreign supply with confidence, and the market is rewarding that resilience.

3. In Focus: The NMB Phenomenon and the 5% Daily Price Band

Friday's session was dominated by NMB. The bank's post-split shares closed at 2,130, up from 1,850 on Monday — a gain of 15.1% in just five trading sessions. The volume tells the story: 4,010,704 shares traded on the normal board, plus 224,383 shares of the iTrust ETF (which holds a significant NMB position). This is what a successful share split looks like: a stock that was once out of reach for many retail investors at TZS 17,700 per share is now accessible at TZS 2,130, and the market is responding with enthusiasm.

However, the foreign selling figures tell another side of the story. Foreign investors sold 65.01% of the day's turnover — approximately TZS 6.63 billion — yet the market still rose. This is a remarkable display of local buying power. Tanzanian individuals and institutions are stepping forward to absorb every share that foreign sellers offer. The market is no longer dependent on external capital; it is standing on its own two feet.

For everyday investors, the key lesson is about the daily price band. Because NMB closed at 2,130 on Friday, it cannot trade on Monday, 31 August, at any price outside a band of ±5% around that level. The limits are:

Upper limit: 2,130 × 1.05 = 2,236.50

Lower limit: 2,130 × 0.95 = 2,023.50

Any order to buy above 2,236.50 or to sell below 2,023.50 will be automatically rejected by the DSE's trading system. This mechanism keeps trading orderly and prevents panic-driven moves, even when volumes are high.

4. Bond Market: Steady Demand for Long-Term Tax-Free Income

Government bond turnover cooled to TZS 6.02 billion on Friday, down from Thursday's TZS 12.55 billion, but the session still saw meaningful activity. The most liquid instruments were:

25-year 12.56% bond: TZS 3.49 billion traded at 131.33% to 136.71% of face value, yielding approximately 8.8% to 9.3% tax-free

20-year 15.49% bond: TZS 1.80 billion traded at 100.00%, yielding 15.6% tax-free

20-year 12.25% bond: TZS 0.15 billion at 115.00%, yielding 10.6%

Corporate bond activity picked up, with TZS 230 million of the iTrust 13% bond traded at prices between 100% and 103.74%.

The bond market continues to attract institutional money seeking predictable, tax-free income. The 0% withholding tax on government bonds with maturities of three years or more makes these instruments particularly attractive. A 25-year bond yielding 8.8% tax-free may seem lower than the equity market's potential, but it offers certainty — a guaranteed income stream for decades with no daily price volatility.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Early September):

NMB has risen 15.1% since the split and is now trading at 2,130. The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend (split-adjusted from TZS 610.15) is still pending. When it is announced, the share price will mechanically adjust downward by the dividend amount. This is a normal process, not a sign of weakness.

CRDB continues to consolidate at 2,640. Support at 2,630–2,650 remains intact. Any dip toward 2,620 represents an opportunity for patient buyers.

VODA has broken out of its range, closing at 1,050. The stock appears to be building momentum, and the expected FY2025 dividend announcement in the coming weeks could provide further support.

TOL pulled back sharply but may find support near 1,800–1,830 based on recent trading patterns.

Medium-Term (Q3 2026):

The market is entering a period rich in corporate actions. NMB's ex-dividend date is pending, VODA's dividend announcement is expected within weeks, and the Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive.

The government's infrastructure spending programme will continue to benefit cement, construction, and logistics companies.

Long-Term: The NMB share split has been a defining moment for the DSE. It has made the exchange's second-largest bank accessible to everyday investors, and the market has responded with record volumes. The heavy foreign selling being absorbed by local capital confirms that the market has matured into a stable, self-reliant exchange. For the patient, long-term investor, this is exactly the kind of environment that supports consistent, compounding wealth creation.

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the price-band or share-split concepts are new to you, read Understanding Market Data and Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs.

Understand the mechanics of share splits. A split does not change the value of your investment — it simply divides the same pie into more, smaller slices. What changes is accessibility and liquidity.

Respect the daily price bands. They are designed to keep trading orderly and prevent panic-driven moves. Always calculate the allowed range before placing an order.

Think in years, not days. The NMB rally has been exciting, but chasing stocks after sharp rises can be risky. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.

Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Friday's session — with foreign investors selling 65% of turnover and the market still rising to a new high — is the clearest evidence yet that the DSE has entered a new era. The local investor base has the depth, confidence, and capital to absorb significant foreign exits without distress. This structural resilience is the foundation for long-term, sustainable growth. The NMB share split has democratised access to one of the market's premier banking counters, and the broader market is benefiting from the increased participation. For the multi-year investor, this is not a moment to chase short-term rallies; it is a confirmation that the patient, disciplined approach — accumulating quality assets, respecting price bands, and letting compounding work — will be rewarded over time.
