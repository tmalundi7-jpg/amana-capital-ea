Daily DSE Wrap | Thursday, 10th September 2026

Bond Market Finally Blinks as Equity Rotation Accelerates

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange delivered its clearest signal yet on Thursday that the long-awaited rotation from bonds to equities is underway. Government bond turnover collapsed by 78.5% to TZS 7.72 billion, while equity turnover rose 33.8% to TZS 8.33 billion. The All-Share Index surged 42 points to 4,623.20 and the Tanzania Share Index broke through 10,335.82 — both new post-split highs. For everyday investors, this is a moment that demands attention. The institutions that spent weeks locking in double-digit tax-free bond yields have finally turned their attention to the stock market.

The session was firmly in the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 8.33 billion. While this is below Monday's block-trade-inflated TZS 17.31 billion, it is more than triple the levels seen in early August. The deal count of 3,601 confirms that everyday investors remain engaged.

The rotation signal is confirmed: Bond turnover collapsed from TZS 35.95 billion on Wednesday to just TZS 7.72 billion on Thursday — a 78.5% contraction in a single session. Simultaneously, equity turnover rose 33.8%. This is the classic pattern that experienced investors watch for: institutions stepping away from fixed income and moving into equities.

Foreign selling faded to a trickle: Foreign selling fell to 7.69% of turnover (TZS 640 million) — the lowest level in weeks. The foreign exit that dominated August is now a minor footnote. Local capital has fully taken control.

The DSEI and TSI both hit new post-split highs. The DSEI closed at 4,623.20, up 42 points, while the TSI broke through 10,335.82. This is not just a large-bank story anymore; the entire market is participating.

3. In Focus: The Bond-to-Equity Rotation and the 5% Daily Price Band

Thursday's session delivered the clearest confirmation yet that the bond-to-equity rotation is real. Here's what happened, in plain terms.

For weeks, pension funds and insurance companies have been aggressively buying government bonds, locking in tax-free yields of 10-12%. On Wednesday, they bought TZS 35.95 billion worth — the largest single-day figure in a week. But on Thursday, bond turnover collapsed to just TZS 7.72 billion — a 78.5% drop.

At the same time, equity turnover rose 33.8% to TZS 8.33 billion. The DSEI surged 42 points. The TSI broke through 10,335.

What does this mean? The institutions that were parking cash in bonds have finished their duration-locking phase. They have secured the guaranteed income they need. Now they are turning to equities for growth. And they are not being subtle about it — the bid walls in CRDB, VODA, and NMB tell the story.

For everyday investors, the lesson is clear: when bond turnover collapses while equity turnover rises, institutional money is rotating into stocks. This is a signal to be ready with your equity orders, not to chase, but to position patiently at support levels.

For the daily price band, Thursday's closing prices determine Friday's limits. Because CRDB closed at 2,840, it cannot trade on Friday, 11 September, outside a band of ±5% around that level:

  • Upper limit: 2,840 × 1.05 = 2,982.00
  • Lower limit: 2,840 × 0.95 = 2,698.00

4. Bond Market: The Rotation Signal is Confirmed

Government bond turnover collapsed to TZS 7.72 billion on Thursday, down 78.5% from Wednesday's TZS 35.95 billion. The dominant trades were:

  • 25-year 15.00% bond: TZS 3.50 billion at 127.02% to 142.40%, yielding 10.33% to 11.76%
  • 20-year 12.25% bond: TZS 2.65 billion at 105.00% to 125.43%, yielding 9.59% to 11.86%
  • 25-year 13.25% bond: TZS 1.00 billion at 122.41%, yielding 10.83%

Yields remain attractive at 9.59% to 11.86% tax-free, but the directional flow has clearly shifted. The institutions that were buying bonds aggressively on Wednesday stepped back on Thursday. Equity turnover rose simultaneously.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The window for double-digit tax-free bond yields is still open, but it is narrowing. The 25-year 15.00% bond still yields over 10%, and the 20-year 12.25% issue yields close to 12% at the higher end. For those seeking predictable income, bonds remain a viable option. But for those seeking growth, the equity market is where the institutional money is now heading.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Mid-September):

  • CRDB closed at 2,840 with a 2.5x bid/offer ratio. A pullback to 2,800 would represent an opportunity for patient buyers before the next leg up.
  • NMB is at a new post-split high of 2,190. The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. When announced, the stock will mechanically adjust downward.
  • VODA closed at 1,110 with zero offers on the book. The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst.
  • TOL fell 11.6% after its speculative surge. This is a warning: do not chase volatile counters.

Medium-Term (Q3 2026):

  • The bond-to-equity rotation is now confirmed. If bond turnover remains below TZS 10 billion while equity turnover stays above TZS 8 billion, the rotation will continue.
  • The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any rate cuts would further compress bond yields and support equity valuations.

Long-Term:
The events of the past eight weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, the foreign flow reversal, and now the confirmed rotation — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.
