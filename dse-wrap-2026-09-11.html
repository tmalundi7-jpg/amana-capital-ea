Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 11th September 2026

Record Block Supply Absorbed Without a Flinch as Indices Reach New Peaks

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange closed the week with a session that demonstrated the remarkable depth of local institutional demand. Equity turnover nearly doubled to TZS 16.64 billion, driven by three major block trades — a 4.9-million-share NMB block, a 600,000-share VODA block, and a 114,000-share TCC block. Yet the All-Share Index rose to a new post-split high of 4,658.31, and the Tanzania Share Index crossed 10,400. The message is clear: local capital is not just absorbing supply — it is actively bidding for it. At the same time, the bond market attracted TZS 19.43 billion in institutional money, proving that both asset classes can draw liquidity simultaneously.

The session was firmly in the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 16.64 billion. The deal count rose modestly to 3,747, confirming that this was not just institutional activity but broad-based participation. The volume of 7.53 million shares was nearly double Thursday's level.

The block trades tell the story: NMB absorbed a 4.9-million-share block, VODA a 600,000-share block, and TCC a 114,000-share block. In total, more than 5.6 million shares changed hands in pre-arranged transactions. Yet the market closed higher across the board. This is what a market looks like when local institutions are the dominant buyers.

Foreign selling faded further: Foreign selling fell to 5.27% of turnover (TZS 877 million) — the lowest level in months. The foreign exit that dominated August is now a minor footnote. Local capital has fully taken control.

The DSEI and TSI both hit new post-split highs. The DSEI closed at 4,658.31, up 35 points, while the TSI broke through 10,400.36. Market capitalisation rose to TZS 40.61 trillion, a new record.

3. In Focus: Record Block Absorption and the 5% Daily Price Band

Friday's session provided a textbook example of how institutional block trades work in a deep, locally-driven market. Three major blocks changed hands:

  • NMB: 4,904,282 shares
  • VODA: 600,000 shares
  • TCC: 114,000 shares

In total, more than 5.6 million shares — worth approximately TZS 15 billion — were transferred in pre-arranged transactions. Yet the market closed higher. The DSEI rose 35 points. The TSI crossed 10,400.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

Block trades are negotiated directly between institutions, not through the normal order book. They don't reflect retail demand. But they do signal institutional confidence. When local institutions step forward to absorb millions of shares without demanding a discount, it tells you they believe the stock is worth buying at current prices.

The most remarkable aspect of Friday's session was the price action. Despite absorbing 5.6 million shares in blocks, the market did not decline. CRDB rose 2.1%. TCC rose 1.4%. NMB and VODA closed flat. This is what a market looks like when the buyer base is deep and confident.

For the daily price band, Friday's closing prices determine Monday's limits. Because CRDB closed at 2,900, it cannot trade on Monday, 14 September, outside a band of ±5% around that level:

  • Upper limit: 2,900 × 1.05 = 3,045.00
  • Lower limit: 2,900 × 0.95 = 2,755.00

4. Bond Market: Two-Way Liquidity in Action

Government bond turnover rebounded to TZS 19.43 billion on Friday, up 151.7% from Thursday's TZS 7.72 billion. The dominant trades were:

  • 20-year 12.25% bond (Jul 2026): TZS 15.96 billion at 105.00% to 112.65%, yielding 11.04% to 11.87%
  • 25-year 13.25% bond (Feb 2026): TZS 2.22 billion at 126.997% to 137.4337%, yielding 9.50% to 10.40%
  • 25-year 15.00% bond: TZS 0.60 billion at 138.3874%, yielding approximately 10.4%

The most striking development is that both the equity and bond markets attracted significant institutional money on the same day. Equity turnover reached TZS 16.64 billion, while bond turnover reached TZS 19.43 billion. This is not a one-way rotation; it is a two-way liquidity environment where institutions are deploying capital across both asset classes.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The window for double-digit tax-free bond yields remains open. The 20-year 12.25% bond still yields 11.04% to 11.87%, and the 25-year 13.25% bond yields 9.50% to 10.40%. These are attractive returns for those seeking predictable income.

But the equity market is also offering compelling opportunities. CRDB closed at 2,900 with a 3.5x bid/offer ratio. VODA closed at 1,110 with a 4.5x bid/offer ratio. These are deep, liquid counters with strong institutional demand.

The lesson for everyday investors is that you don't have to choose between bonds and equities. A balanced approach — some capital in tax-free bonds for stability, some in quality equities for growth — can work well in this environment.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Mid-September):

  • CRDB closed at 2,900, a new post-split high, with a 3.5x bid/offer ratio. The stock touched 2,980 intraday; a pullback to 2,840–2,880 would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • NMB closed flat at 2,190 after absorbing a 4.9-million-share block. The order book has flipped — offers now exceed bids. The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending.
  • VODA closed at 1,110 with a 4.5x bid/offer ratio. The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst.
  • TCC continues to rally into its ex-dividend window. The stock closed at 13,440 and is approaching its 5% upper limit.
  • TOL fell 3.8% — the speculative surge has fully reversed. Avoid chasing.

Medium-Term (Q3 2026):

  • The bond-to-equity rotation is not a one-way street. Institutions are active in both markets. The key signal to watch is whether bond turnover contracts below TZS 10 billion for two consecutive sessions while equity turnover remains above TZS 10 billion.
  • The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any rate cuts would further compress bond yields and support equity valuations.

Long-Term:
The events of the past nine weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, the foreign flow reversal, and now the record block absorption — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the block-trade or price-band concepts are new to you, read Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs and Understanding Market Data.
  • Understand block trades. When institutions negotiate large transactions, it doesn't mean you should chase the stock. It means institutional confidence exists at that price. Use it as a reference point, not a signal to buy at any cost.
  • Respect the daily price bands. They are designed to keep trading orderly and prevent panic-driven moves. Always calculate the allowed range before placing an order.
  • Watch both markets. The bond market and equity market can attract liquidity simultaneously. A balanced approach across both asset classes can work well.
  • Don't chase volatile counters. TOL's 3.8% drop after its speculative surge is a reminder that not every rally is sustainable. The patient investor waits for stable prices and clear entry points.
  • Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

Foreign selling was just 5.27% of turnover. Local institutions absorbed every block. Both the DSEI and TSI closed at new post-split highs. For the multi-year investor, the message is clear: this is a market that rewards patience, education, and the willingness to adapt.
