Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 15th September 2026

Foreign Buyers Return as VODA Supply Evaporates — But Not Every Stock Tells the Same Story

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange delivered a session of stark contrasts on Tuesday. Equity turnover surged 166.5% to TZS 16.86 billion, driven by four major block trades. The All-Share Index closed at a new post-split high of 4,696.11. Yet beneath these headline numbers lies a more nuanced picture: foreign buyers returned in their largest numbers in weeks, VODA closed with zero offers on the order book after absorbing a 1.4-million-share block, while NMB and CRDB both turned offer-heavy. This is not a uniform market — it is a market where different stocks are telling very different stories.

The session returned to the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 16.86 billion, after Monday's quieter TZS 6.33 billion. The surge was driven almost entirely by block trades: NMB (4,768,685 shares), VODA (1,400,000 shares), CRDB (140,000 shares), and KCB (116,280 shares). The deal count fell 31% to 3,516, confirming that this was an institution-dominated session.

Foreign buyers returned in force: Foreign buying jumped to 11.82% of turnover (TZS 1.99 billion) — the highest level in weeks. Foreign selling was 15.36% (TZS 2.59 billion), leaving a net outflow of approximately TZS 597 million. This is the first time in recent memory that foreign buying has exceeded 10% of turnover. The foreign exit that dominated August is not just fading — it is reversing.

The DSEI closed at a new post-split high of 4,696.11, up 6.13 points. The TSI eased marginally to 10,438.46, down 8.02 points. Market capitalisation reached a record TZS 40.94 trillion.

Bond market activity remained strong at TZS 26.61 billion, with the 20-year 12.25% bond trading TZS 10.42 billion at yields of 10.72% to 11.35% tax-free. The 10-year 11.44% bond traded at 9.16% — the tightest yield on record.

The most remarkable aspect of VODA's session was the order book: at the close, there were 39,504 bids and zero offers. Supply has completely evaporated. NMB closed flat at 2,180 after absorbing a 4.77-million-share block trade. The order book is extremely offer-heavy: 447,196 offers versus 73,938 bids — a ratio of 6.0 to 1. The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending.

DCB fell 3.3% to 435, while MCB dropped 2.5% to 385.

3. In Focus: Divergent Order Books and the 5% Daily Price Band

Tuesday's session provided a masterclass in reading order books. Three large-cap stocks — VODA, CRDB, and NMB — all absorbed significant block trades. But their order books told very different stories at the close:

What does this mean for everyday investors?

When a stock closes with zero offers, it means there are literally no shares available for sale at the closing price. Every seller has been matched with a buyer. This is an extremely bullish signal — it suggests that anyone who wanted to sell has already done so, and the remaining holders are not willing to part with their shares at current prices.

When a stock closes with offers far exceeding bids, it means there is more supply than demand. Sellers are competing to exit, and buyers can afford to be patient. This is a bearish signal in the short term — it suggests the stock may pull back until the excess supply is absorbed.

The lesson for everyday investors is simple: pay attention to the order book, not just the price. A stock that rises on strong volume but closes with an offer-heavy book may be due for a pause. A stock that absorbs a massive block and closes with zero offers may be poised for further gains.

For the daily price band, Tuesday's closing prices determine Wednesday's limits. Because CRDB closed at 2,940, it cannot trade on Wednesday, 16 September, outside a band of ±5% around that level:

  • Upper limit: 2,940 × 1.05 = 3,087.00
  • Lower limit: 2,940 × 0.95 = 2,793.00

4. Bond Market: Institutions Still Locking in Duration

Government bond turnover rose to TZS 26.61 billion on Tuesday, up 52% from Monday's TZS 17.47 billion. The most liquid instruments were:

  • 20-year 12.25% bond (Jul 2026): TZS 10.42 billion at 109.21% to 114.70%, yielding 10.72% to 11.35%
  • 20-year 15.49% bond (Sep 2020): TZS 5.20 billion at 100.00% to 145.64%, yielding 10.31% to 15.70%
  • 25-year 15.95% bond (Oct 2021): TZS 5.05 billion at 100.00% to 140.00%, yielding 11.13% to 16.24%
  • 10-year 11.44% bond (Sep 2021): TZS 5.00 billion at 114.34%, yielding 9.16%

The 10-year 11.44% bond traded at 9.16% — the tightest yield on record for that maturity. The 20-year 12.25% bond, which is the current benchmark, traded at yields of 10.72% to 11.35% tax-free.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to attract significant institutional demand. Pension funds and insurance companies are locking in long-dated, tax-free yields before they compress further. The 9.16% yield on the 10-year bond is the lowest on record, confirming that the era of double-digit yields on shorter maturities is ending.

However, the 20-year 12.25% bond still offers over 11% tax-free at the higher end of its range. For those seeking predictable, long-term income, this remains an attractive option.

The simultaneous strength of both the bond and equity markets tells us that this is not a one-way rotation. Institutions are deploying capital across both asset classes. A balanced approach — some capital in bonds for stability, some in quality equities for growth — can work well in this environment.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Mid-September):

  • CRDB closed at 2,940 with an offer-heavy order book. A pullback to 2,900 or below would represent a natural pause after the stock's recent run toward 3,000.
  • VODA closed at 1,150 with zero offers on the book. The stock has absorbed a 1.4-million-share block and shows no sign of supply. The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst.
  • NMB closed flat at 2,180 with an extremely offer-heavy book (447,196 offers vs. 73,938 bids). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted.
  • TCC eased 1.0% to 13,360. The stock continues to trade in its pre-dividend window.
  • TOL rose 2.2% to 1,900 but remains volatile. Avoid chasing.

Medium-Term (Q3 2026):

  • Foreign buying returned to 11.82% of turnover — the highest in weeks. If this trend continues, it could provide additional support for the market.
  • The bond market continues to attract TZS 26 billion in duration demand at 9% to 11% tax-free. Watch for a contraction in bond turnover below TZS 10 billion for two consecutive sessions while equity turnover remains elevated — that would signal a clearer rotation.

Long-Term:
The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, the foreign flow reversal, and now the return of foreign buyers — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the order-book or price-band concepts are new to you, read Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs and Understanding Market Data.

  • Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.
