VODA Closes With Zero Offers as Local Institutions Tighten Their Grip on the Market

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange opened the new week with a session that confirmed a pattern we have seen repeatedly in recent weeks: local institutions are in firm control, foreign participation is negligible, and order books are revealing where the real demand lies. Equity turnover surged 173% to TZS 11.76 billion, driven by block trades across five counters. The DSEI closed at 4,633.82, up 31 points, while the TSI climbed 134 points to 10,300.66. But the most striking development was VODA, which closed with zero offers on the order book — a signal that supply has completely evaporated.

1. Market Snapshot

The session returned to the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 11.76 billion, after Friday's quieter TZS 4.30 billion. The surge was driven by block trades in five counters: KCB (1,800,000 shares), VODA (1,000,000 shares), TOL (500,000 shares), TCCL (400,000 shares), and NMB (364,304 shares). Normal-board activity was led by CRDB (668,525 shares), NMB (479,238 shares), and MCB (137,000 shares).

Foreign participation fell back to minimal levels after Friday's block-driven selling. Foreign buying was just 0.01% of turnover, while selling was 7.24%. The net outflow was approximately TZS 851 million. Local investors bought 99.99% and sold 92.76% of turnover — the market is entirely domestically driven.

The DSEI closed at 4,633.82, up 31.56 points, while the TSI climbed 134 points to 10,300.66. The banking sector led the gains, rising 1.60%, with NMB up 2.9% and KCB up 2.0%.

Bond market turnover rose to TZS 5.74 billion from Friday's TZS 0.63 billion. The 5-year 10.25% bond traded TZS 4.37 billion at a yield of 10.18%, while the 25-year 13.25% bond traded TZS 1.06 billion at 11.64%.

2. Top Movers

Gainers: VODA (+3.3%), PAL (+3.3%), NMB (+2.9%), MUCOBA (+2.5%), TTP (+2.4%).
Losers: MCB (−3.8%), DSE (−2.7%), SWIS (−1.5%), TOL (−1.1%), TBL (−0.7%).

VODA was the standout performer, rising 3.3% to 1,260 after absorbing a 1-million-share block trade. The most remarkable aspect of VODA's session was the order book: at the close, there were 100,177 bids and zero offers. Supply has completely evaporated. This is what happens when demand overwhelms available shares.

NMB rose 2.9% to 2,130 on volume of 843,542 shares. The order book shows 92,349 bids against 50,195 offers — a ratio of 1.8 to 1 in favour of buyers. The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending.

PAL added 3.3% to 310, while MUCOBA rose 2.5% to 415. These gains were achieved on relatively light volume.

MCB was the session's worst performer, falling 3.8% to 375. The order book is heavily offer-weighted: 136,609 offers versus just 11,240 bids.

CRDB closed flat at 2,810 after touching 2,670 intraday. The order book is bid-heavy: 146,315 bids versus 52,401 offers — a ratio of 2.8 to 1 in favour of buyers. This suggests demand is building beneath the surface.

3. In Focus: Reading Order Books — A Key Skill for Investors

Monday's session provided another powerful lesson in reading order books — the lists of outstanding buy and sell orders that sit beneath every stock's price. Several stocks told very different stories through their order books at the close:

What does this mean for everyday investors?

  • When a stock closes with zero offers — as VODA did — it means there are literally no shares available for sale at the closing price. Every seller has been matched with a buyer. This is an extremely bullish signal in the short term. It suggests that anyone who wanted to sell has already done so, and the remaining holders are not willing to part with their shares at current prices.
  • When a stock closes with offers far exceeding bids — as MCB did — it means there is more supply than demand. Sellers are competing to exit, and buyers can afford to be patient. This suggests the stock may continue to drift lower until the excess supply is absorbed.

The lesson is simple: pay attention to the order book, not just the price. A stock that rises on strong volume with a bid-heavy book may have further to run. A stock that falls with an offer-heavy book may have further to go.

4. Bond Market: Institutions Return to Duration

Government bond turnover rose to TZS 5.74 billion on Monday, up from Friday's TZS 0.63 billion. The most active instruments were:

  • 5-year 10.25% bond (Apr 2026): TZS 4.37 billion at 107.55%, yielding 10.18%
  • 25-year 13.25% bond (Feb 2026): TZS 1.06 billion at 115.00%, yielding 11.64%
  • 25-year 15.00% bond (Aug 2025): TZS 0.10 billion at 135.40% to 140.00%, yielding 10.58% to 11.00%

Corporate bond activity was minimal, with a single trade in the 5-year 12.00% bond at 99.90% to 100.00% of face value.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to attract institutional demand, with the 5-year bond offering over 10% tax-free and the 25-year bond offering over 11.6% tax-free. These are attractive yields for those seeking predictable, long-term income.

However, the simultaneous strength of both the bond and equity markets tells us that this is not a one-way rotation. Institutions are deploying capital across both asset classes. A balanced approach — some capital in bonds for stability, some in quality equities for growth — can work well in this environment.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Late September):

  • CRDB closed flat at 2,810 with a bid-heavy order book. The stock touched 2,670 intraday before recovering, suggesting strong support at lower levels. A pullback to 2,750 or below would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • VODA closed at 1,260 with zero offers on the book. The stock has absorbed a 1-million-share block trade and shows no sign of supply. The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst.
  • NMB closed at 2,130 with a bid-heavy order book. The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted until the ex-date is announced.
  • TCC remains in its pre-dividend window, trading at 13,400. The net dividend yield remains attractive at over 7%.
  • MCB fell 3.8% with an extremely offer-heavy book. The stock may continue to drift lower until supply is absorbed.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026):

  • The bond market continues to attract significant institutional demand, with yields compressing. Watch for a pickup in equity turnover alongside a slowdown in bond buying as a potential signal of rotation.
  • The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any change in interest rates would be a major event for both bonds and equities.

Long-Term:
The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and the foreign flow reversal — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the order-book or price-band concepts are new to you, read Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs and Understanding Market Data.
  • Read the order book, not just the price. A stock that closes with zero offers is very different from one that closes with offers far exceeding bids. The order book tells you what the price alone cannot.
  • Respect the daily price bands. They are designed to keep trading orderly and prevent panic-driven moves. Always calculate the allowed range before placing an order.
  • Watch for turnover patterns. When both bond and equity turnover rise together, institutions are active across asset classes. When one rises while the other falls, a rotation may be underway.
  • Don't chase volatile counters. Sharp moves, like MCB's 3.8% drop, can reverse just as quickly. Patience and discipline are your best tools.
  • Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.
