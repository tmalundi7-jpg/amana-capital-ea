Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 25th September 2026

Equities Reclaim High Ground as Bond Turnover Contracts for Third Straight Session

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange closed the week on a strong note, with both indices reaching new post-split highs and equity turnover recovering above the TZS 3 billion mark. Yet the most significant development was not in the equity market at all — it was in the bond market, where turnover contracted for the third consecutive session. This is the pattern that experienced investors watch for: when institutional money steps away from fixed income while equities attract fresh capital, a rotation may be quietly underway. For everyday investors, this is a moment to pay close attention.

1. Market Snapshot

The session moved the market back into the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 3.78 billion, after Thursday's brief dip into the NORMAL range. The deal count rose 7% to 3,719, confirming that everyday investors remained engaged. The NMB block trade of 429,000 shares was absorbed without difficulty.

The DSEI closed at a new post-split high of 4,682.13, up 24 points, while the TSI climbed 57 points to 10,419.49. Both indices have now risen for three consecutive sessions.

Foreign participation remains negligible. Foreign buying was zero for the second consecutive session, while foreign selling was 11.31% of turnover (TZS 428 million), concentrated in NMB and CRDB profit-taking. Local investors bought 100% and sold 88.69% of turnover. The market is entirely domestically driven.

3. In Focus: The Bond-to-Equity Rotation Signal — What It Means for Everyday Investors

In Thursday's wrap, we noted that bond turnover was contracting (from TZS 39.48 bn on Wednesday to TZS 20.87 bn on Thursday) while equity turnover was rising. On Friday, that pattern intensified: bond turnover fell again to TZS 18.91 bn, while equity turnover rose to TZS 3.78 bn.

Why does this matter?

Tanzanian capital markets are heavily dominated by a few large local institutions (pension funds and insurance companies). When they move capital, they move the market. For the past year, they have been pouring money into long-term government bonds to lock in double-digit tax-free yields. This created massive bond turnover and suppressed equity prices.

Now, we are seeing the first consistent signs that this money may be pausing its bond purchases and looking back at the equity market. When bond volume drops and equity volume rises for three consecutive sessions, it suggests institutions have filled their bond quotas and are deploying cash into stocks.

The signal is not yet fully confirmed. For it to be confirmed, we would need to see bond turnover remain below TZS 10 billion for at least two more sessions while equity turnover stays above TZS 5 billion. But the direction of travel is clear.

What should everyday investors do?

The lesson is not to rush into the market. It is to pay attention to the signals and position accordingly. If the rotation is real, quality stocks like CRDB, TBL, and TCC may benefit. If it is a false start, the daily price bands and disciplined limit orders will protect you from overpaying.

The most important thing is to avoid chasing sharp moves. Instead, identify the stocks you want to own, set your limit prices, and wait for the market to come to you.

4. Bond Market: Distressed Seller Exits at 19% Yield

Government bond turnover fell 9.4% to TZS 18.91 billion on Friday, the third consecutive contraction. The most active instruments were:

  • 10-year 11.44% bond (Nov 2019): TZS 16.03 billion at 90.38% to 111.65%, yielding 8.17% to 19.08%
  • 25-year 15.75% bond (Jan 2025): TZS 0.78 billion at 140.00% to 147.52%, yielding 10.60% to 11.27%
  • 20-year 12.25% bond (Jul 2026): TZS 0.82 billion at 105.00%, yielding 11.93%
  • 25-year 15.00% bond (Aug 2025): TZS 0.40 billion at 136.72%, yielding 10.89%

The most striking development was the 10-year 11.44% bond trading at 90.38%, yielding 19.08%. This is a distressed sale — an institution exiting at a significant loss. Such trades often occur when an institution needs to raise cash quickly, and they can create opportunities for patient buyers.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to offer attractive yields for those seeking predictable, tax-free income. The 25-year 15.75% bond at 10.60% to 11.27% tax-free remains an attractive option for long-term savers. However, the distressed sale in the 10-year bond suggests that some institutions are under pressure. This is not a reason to panic — it is a reason to be selective.

For those considering bonds, the key is to focus on quality and yield. The 25-year bonds at over 10% tax-free are attractive. The 10-year bond at 19% is a distressed sale and should be treated with caution.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Late September into October):

  • CRDB closed at 2,940, a new post-split high, with a balanced order book. After rising 70 shillings in one session, a pullback to 2,880–2,920 is possible. This would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • VODA closed at 1,280 with an offer-heavy order book. The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst. A pullback to 1,240 would represent an opportunity.
  • NMB closed flat at 2,130 after absorbing a 429,000-share block trade. The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted until the ex-date is announced.
  • TCC closed at 13,200, remaining in its pre-dividend window. The net dividend yield remains attractive at over 7.5%.
  • TBL closed at 9,800; the net dividend yield is 7.93% — the highest in the market.
  • DCB fell 4.5% with an extremely offer-heavy book. The stock is in a downtrend; avoid.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026):

  • The bond-to-equity rotation signal is building. Watch for bond turnover to remain below TZS 10 billion for two more sessions while equity turnover stays above TZS 5 billion — that would confirm the rotation.
  • The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any change in interest rates would be a major event for both bonds and equities.

Long-Term:

The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and the foreign flow reversal — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the rotation or price-band concepts are new to you, read Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs and Understanding Market Data.
  • Watch the bond market for signals. When bond turnover contracts for multiple sessions while equity turnover rises, institutional money may be rotating into stocks. This is a signal to be ready with your equity orders.
  • Respect the daily price bands. They are designed to keep trading orderly and prevent panic-driven moves. Always calculate the allowed range before placing an order.
  • Read the order book, not just the price. A stock that closes with bids far exceeding offers is very different from one that closes with offers far exceeding bids. The order book tells you what the price alone cannot.
  • Don't chase volatile counters. MUCOBA's 11.1% gain and DCB's 4.5% drop are reminders that sharp moves can reverse quickly.
  • Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Friday's session — with CRDB hitting a new post-split high of 2,940, the DSEI and TSI reaching new records, and bond turnover contracting for the third consecutive session — is a reminder that markets move in cycles. The three-session bond contraction (TZS 39.48 bn → TZS 20.87 bn → TZS 18.91 bn) alongside the equity market's recovery above TZS 3 billion is the pattern that precedes a rotation from fixed income to equities. The distressed sale in the 10-year bond at 19% yield suggests some institutions are under pressure to raise cash. Meanwhile, CRDB's balanced order book at 2,940 after a 70-shilling rally suggests the market is consolidating at the top of its range. For the multi-year investor, the message is clear: this is a market that rewards patience, education, and the willingness to adapt. The opportunities are there — in CRDB's consolidation, in VODA's pre-dividend accumulation, in the 10.60% government bond yield that may not last — but they require discipline to capture. The crowd chases sharp moves; the wise investor reads order books, respects the price bands, and lets the market's natural rhythm do the heavy lifting.
