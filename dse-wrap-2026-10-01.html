Daily DSE Wrap | Thursday, 1st October 2026

Foreign Flows Find Equilibrium as Market Enters a New Phase of Two-Way Trading

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange opened the new month with a session that delivered a quiet but significant milestone. Foreign buying and selling were almost exactly equal — 23.81% versus 23.64% of turnover — marking the first time this quarter that international capital has been in balance. This is not a trivial detail. After months of one-way foreign selling, the market has matured into a genuinely two-way marketplace where local and foreign investors trade on equal terms. For everyday investors, understanding what this means is essential.

3. In Focus: What Balanced Foreign Flows Mean for Everyday Investors

Thursday's session delivered a milestone that most observers will miss: foreign buying (23.81%) and foreign selling (23.64%) were essentially equal — the first balanced foreign flow of the quarter. The net inflow of TZS 5.63 million is trivial in absolute terms, but its significance is structural.

Why does this matter?

For months, foreign investors were net sellers on the DSE. They were exiting positions, creating supply that local institutions had to absorb. This dynamic kept a lid on prices and created opportunities for patient buyers. Then, in late September, foreign capital began to return. On Wednesday, foreign buying surged to 35.26% of turnover, producing a net inflow of TZS 1.29 billion.

Thursday's session completed the transition. Foreign buying and selling are now in balance. This tells us that the market has matured into a genuinely two-way marketplace where local and foreign investors trade on equal terms. Local capital is no longer absorbing foreign supply — it is trading against foreigners on equal footing.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

A balanced market is a healthy market. When both buyers and sellers are active, prices are more likely to reflect genuine supply and demand rather than one-sided pressure. This creates opportunities for investors who understand how to read order books and identify where the real demand lies.

The lesson is to pay attention to foreign flows — not because they determine the market's direction, but because they tell you whether the market is one-sided or two-way. A two-way market is one where disciplined, patient investors can find opportunities on both sides of the trade.

4. Bond Market: Institutions Still Locking in Duration

Government bond turnover rose 32.4% to TZS 15.86 billion on Thursday from Wednesday's TZS 11.98 billion. The most active instruments were:

  • 20-year 15.49% bond (Sep 2020): TZS 15.16 billion at 106.00% to 141.21%, yielding 10.67% to 14.82%
  • 25-year 15.00% bond (Aug 2025): TZS 0.54 billion at 100.00% to 135.75%, yielding 10.99% to 15.34%
  • 15-year 14.50% bond (Feb 2025): TZS 0.05 billion at 126.89%, yielding 10.84%
  • 25-year 15.95% bond (Oct 2021): TZS 0.04 billion at 110.00%, yielding 14.57%

Corporate bond activity was nil.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to attract significant institutional demand. The 20-year 15.49% bond, which traded TZS 15.16 billion in a single session, offers a yield of 10.67% to 14.82% tax-free. This is an attractive return for those seeking predictable, long-term income.

The simultaneous strength of both the bond and equity markets tells us that this is not a one-way rotation. Institutions are deploying capital across both asset classes. A balanced approach — some capital in bonds for stability, some in quality equities for growth — can work well in this environment.

For those considering bonds, the key is to focus on quality and yield. The 20-year bonds at over 10% tax-free are attractive. However, with turnover concentrated in a few large trades, liquidity can be limited. Investors should be patient and wait for opportunities that suit their goals.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Early October):

  • CRDB closed at 2,970 after absorbing a 140,000-share block. The order book is balanced. A pullback to 2,940 or below would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • VODA closed at 1,260 with a strongly bid-weighted order book (3.5x ratio). The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst. A pullback to 1,240 would represent an opportunity.
  • NMB closed at 2,040 with an extremely offer-heavy order book (10.7x ratio). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted until the ex-date is announced.

  • TOL absorbed a 178,600-share block but closed down 1.1%. The stock remains volatile; avoid chasing.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026):

  • Foreign flows have found equilibrium. Watch whether this two-way dynamic continues — a sustained balance would signal a mature, resilient market.
  • The bond market continues to attract institutional demand, with yields compressing. Watch for a pickup in equity turnover alongside a slowdown in bond buying as a potential signal of rotation.
  • The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any change in interest rates would be a major event for both bonds and equities.

Long-Term:

The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and now the equilibrium in foreign flows — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the foreign flow or order-book concepts are new to you, read Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs and Understanding Market Data.
  • Watch foreign flows. When foreign buying and selling are balanced, the market is two-way. When one side dominates, the market is one-sided. A two-way market is healthier and more resilient.
  • Respect the daily price bands. They are designed to keep trading orderly and prevent panic-driven moves. Always calculate the allowed range before placing an order.
  • Read the order book, not just the price. A stock that closes with bids far exceeding offers is very different from one that closes with offers far exceeding bids. The order book tells you what the price alone cannot.
  • Don't chase volatile counters. TOL's 1.1% decline after absorbing a block trade is a reminder that sharp moves can reverse quickly.
  • Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Thursday's session — with foreign buying and selling in near-perfect balance for the first time this quarter, CRDB consolidating at 2,970 with a balanced order book, and VODA showing a 3.5-to-1 bid-to-offer ratio — is a reminder that markets evolve. The DSE has transitioned from a market dominated by foreign selling to one where local and international capital trade on equal terms. This is a sign of maturity. It means the market is no longer dependent on any single group of investors and can absorb shocks from any direction. For the multi-year investor, the message is clear: this is a market that rewards patience, education, and the willingness to adapt. The opportunities are there — in CRDB's consolidation, in VODA's bid-heavy book, in the 10.67% government bond yield that may not last — but they require discipline to capture. The crowd chases sharp moves; the wise investor reads order books, respects the price bands, and lets the market's natural rhythm do the heavy lifting.
