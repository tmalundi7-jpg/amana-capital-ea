Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 2nd October 2026

Bond Market Absorbs TZS 57.8 Billion as Equity Order Books Turn Heavily Offer-Weighted

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange closed the week with a session that revealed a striking divergence between its two main markets. Government bond turnover exploded to TZS 41.95 billion in face value and TZS 57.78 billion in transaction value — more than thirteen times the equity market's turnover of TZS 4.27 billion. Meanwhile, equity order books in the largest counters turned heavily offer-weighted, with CRDB showing just 6,494 bids against 301,625 offers. This is not a market in retreat; it is a market where fixed income is draining liquidity while equity sellers distribute into any available demand. For everyday investors, understanding this dynamic is essential.

3. In Focus: Reading the Divergence Between Bonds and Equities

Friday's session provided a powerful lesson in how different markets can tell very different stories. Government bond turnover exploded to TZS 57.78 billion in transaction value — the largest of the quarter. Meanwhile, equity turnover was just TZS 4.27 billion. This is a ratio of more than 13 to 1 in favour of fixed income.

What does this mean in plain terms?

When institutions buy government bonds, they are parking cash in a safe, predictable asset. When they sell bonds or stop buying, that cash has to go somewhere. On Friday, the opposite happened: institutions aggressively bought bonds, locking in tax-free yields of 9% to 14.8%. This drained liquidity from the equity market.

The equity order books reflect this. CRDB closed with 301,625 offers against just 6,494 bids — a ratio of 46 to 1 in favour of sellers. NMB closed with 312,131 offers against 15,622 bids — a ratio of 20 to 1. These are extreme imbalances. They tell us that sellers are distributing into any available demand, and buyers are stepping back.

What should everyday investors do?

The lesson is not to panic. It is to understand that markets move in cycles. When one asset class attracts massive institutional flows, the other may temporarily lose liquidity. This creates opportunities for patient investors who understand where the real demand lies.

The key is to focus on order books, not just prices. A stock that closes with bids far exceeding offers is very different from one that closes with offers far exceeding bids. CRDB's offer-heavy book suggests near-term weakness; VODA's bid-heavy book suggests demand remains strong. Understanding these dynamics is essential for making informed decisions.

4. Bond Market: Institutions Lock In Duration at Scale

Government bond turnover exploded to TZS 41.95 billion in face value and TZS 57.78 billion in transaction value on Friday — the largest of the quarter. The most active instruments were:

  • 15-year 12.25% bond (Feb 2026): TZS 25.20 billion at 114.26% to 114.58%, yielding 10.46% to 10.50%
  • 20-year 15.49% bond (Sep 2020): TZS 15.48 billion at 100.00% to 141.30%, yielding 10.67% to 14.82%
  • 25-year 15.00% bond (Aug 2025): TZS 0.40 billion at 160.72%, yielding 9.00%
  • 20-year 14.00% bond (Sep 2025): TZS 0.30 billion at 116.67%, yielding 11.91%
  • 15-year 13.50% bond (Feb 2024): TZS 0.13 billion at 113.50% to 119.20%, yielding 10.99% to 11.35%

Corporate bond activity was modest, with trades in the 4-year 13.00% bond (TZS 3.00 million) and the 5-year 12.00% bond (TZS 21.50 million).

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to attract massive institutional demand. The 15-year 12.25% bond, which traded TZS 25.20 billion in a single session, offers a yield of 10.46% to 10.50% tax-free. The 20-year 15.49% bond offers yields of up to 14.82% tax-free at the higher end of its range.

For those seeking predictable, long-term income, these are attractive returns. However, the concentration of turnover in a few large trades means liquidity can be limited. Investors should be patient and wait for opportunities that suit their goals.

The key takeaway is that institutions are aggressively locking in duration. They are willing to accept lower yields on shorter maturities to secure long-term, tax-free income. This tells us that they expect interest rates to remain low or fall further. For everyday investors, this is a signal that the window for double-digit tax-free yields may be closing.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Early October):

  • CRDB closed at 2,950 with an extremely offer-heavy order book (46:1 offers to bids). The stock may face near-term weakness as sellers distribute. A pullback to 2,900 or below would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • VODA closed at 1,260 with a bid-heavy order book (2.2:1 bids to offers). The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst. A pullback to 1,240 would represent an opportunity.
  • NMB closed at 2,040 with an extremely offer-heavy order book (20:1 offers to bids). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted until the ex-date is announced.
  • TCC closed at 13,350, remaining in its pre-dividend window. The net dividend yield remains attractive at over 7.4%.
  • TBL closed at 9,780; the net dividend yield is 7.95% — the highest in the market.
  • DCB fell 2.4% and remains in a downtrend; avoid.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026):

  • The bond market is absorbing massive institutional liquidity. Watch for a slowdown in bond buying and a pickup in equity turnover as a potential signal of rotation.
  • Foreign selling resumed on Friday. Watch whether this continues — sustained foreign selling would add supply to the equity market and could keep prices under pressure.
  • The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any change in interest rates would be a major event for both bonds and equities.

Long-Term:

The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and the rotation between asset classes — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the order-book or rotation concepts are new to you, read Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs and Understanding Market Data.
  • Read the order book, not just the price. A stock that closes with bids far exceeding offers is very different from one that closes with offers far exceeding bids. The order book tells you what the price alone cannot.
  • Watch the bond market for signals. When bond turnover spikes, institutions are locking in duration. When it contracts, they may be rotating into equities. Both signals are useful.
  • Respect the daily price bands. They are designed to keep trading orderly and prevent panic-driven moves. Always calculate the allowed range before placing an order.
  • Don't chase volatile counters. DSE's 4.1% drop and DCB's 2.4% decline are reminders that sharp moves can reverse quickly.
  • Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Friday's session — with bond turnover exploding to TZS 57.78 billion, CRDB's order book showing 46 times more offers than bids, and NMB's showing 20 times more offers than bids — is a reminder that markets are interconnected. When institutions aggressively buy bonds, they drain liquidity from equities. When they sell bonds, that cash can flow back into stocks. Understanding these flows is essential for navigating the market. For the multi-year investor, the message is clear: this is a market that rewards patience, education, and the willingness to adapt. The opportunities are there — in CRDB's offer-heavy pause, in VODA's bid-heavy book, in the 10.46% government bond yield that may not last — but they require discipline to capture. The crowd chases sharp moves; the wise investor reads order books, respects the price bands, and lets the market's natural rhythm do the heavy lifting.
