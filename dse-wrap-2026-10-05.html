Daily DSE Wrap | Monday, 5th October 2026

Offer-Heavy Bank Books Meet Bid-Heavy VODA as Bond Turnover Contracts 63.5%

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange opened the new week with a session that revealed a market of stark contrasts. Equity turnover held steady at TZS 4.27 billion, and the deal count surged 42.8% to 4,735 — confirming broad participation. Yet beneath the surface, the order books told very different stories. CRDB and NMB closed with extreme offer-heavy books, while VODA remained firmly bid-heavy. Meanwhile, government bond turnover contracted 63.5% from Friday's record TZS 41.95 billion, though institutions continued to lock in tax-free yields above 10%. For everyday investors, understanding these divergences is essential.

The session remained firmly in the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 4.27 billion, unchanged from Friday. The deal count surged 42.8% to 4,735 — the highest in weeks — confirming that everyday investors are actively participating across the market.

Foreign selling continued. Foreign buying fell to just 0.03% of turnover (TZS 1.18 million), while selling was 22.47% (TZS 959 million). Bond market turnover contracted 63.5% to TZS 15.29 billion from Friday's record TZS 41.95 billion. The 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 15.15 billion at a yield of 10.12% tax-free.

3. In Focus: Reading Divergent Order Books — A Key Skill for Investors

Monday's session provided a powerful lesson in how different stocks can tell very different stories through their order books. Three large-cap counters absorbed significant block trades, but their order books at the close pointed in different directions:

MCB13,945178,0530.08xOffers exceed bids by 12.8x

What does this mean in plain terms?

When a stock closes with bids far exceeding offers — as VODA did — it means there are many more people wanting to buy than to sell. This is a strong signal of demand. The price is likely to find support and may continue to rise until supply increases.

When a stock closes with offers far exceeding bids — as CRDB and MCB did — it means there is more supply than demand. Sellers are competing to exit, and buyers can afford to be patient. This suggests the stock may continue to drift lower until the excess supply is absorbed.

What should everyday investors do?

The lesson is not to panic. It is to understand that markets are not uniform. Different stocks tell different stories, and the order book reveals what the price alone cannot. A stock that falls on heavy volume with a balanced book may be near a bottom. A stock that falls with an offer-heavy book may have further to go. A stock that rises with a bid-heavy book may have further to run.

The key is to focus on the order book, not just the price. Understanding these dynamics is essential for making informed decisions.

4. Bond Market: Turnover Contracts but Yields Remain Attractive

Government bond turnover contracted 63.5% to TZS 15.29 billion on Monday from Friday's record TZS 41.95 billion. The most active instruments were:

  • 20-year 15.49% bond (Sep 2020): TZS 15.15 billion at 146.35%, yielding 10.12%
  • 25-year 15.95% bond (Aug 2021): TZS 0.02 billion at 145.00%, yielding 10.74%
  • 25-year 12.56% bond (May 2022): TZS 0.02 billion at 120.00%, yielding 10.73%
  • 25-year 13.25% bond (Feb 2026): TZS 0.02 billion at 106.71%, yielding 12.69%

Corporate bond activity was modest, with trades in the 4-year 13.00% bond (TZS 6.00 million) and the 5-year 12.00% bond (TZS 71.00 million).

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to offer attractive yields for those seeking predictable, tax-free income. The 20-year 15.49% bond, which traded TZS 15.15 billion in a single session, offers a yield of 10.12% tax-free. This is an attractive return for long-term savers.

However, the contraction in turnover from Friday's record level suggests that institutions may be pausing their duration-buying spree. This could be a signal that the rotation from bonds to equities is beginning, or it could simply be a pause before the next wave of buying.

For those considering bonds, the key is to focus on quality and yield. The 20-year bonds at over 10% tax-free are attractive. However, with turnover concentrated in a few large trades, liquidity can be limited. Investors should be patient and wait for opportunities that suit their goals.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Early October):

  • CRDB closed at 2,900 with an extremely offer-heavy order book (12.6:1 offers to bids). The stock may face near-term weakness as sellers distribute. A pullback to 2,850 or below would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • VODA closed at 1,290 with a strongly bid-weighted order book (2.4:1 bids to offers). The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst. A pullback to 1,260 would represent an opportunity.
  • NMB closed at 2,030 with an offer-heavy order book (6.7:1 offers to bids). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted until the ex-date is announced.
  • TCC fell 1.7% to 13,120, remaining in its pre-dividend window. The net dividend yield remains attractive at over 7.6%.
  • TBL closed at 9,790; the net dividend yield is 7.94% — the highest in the market.
  • MCB fell 4.1% and remains volatile; avoid.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026):

  • The bond market is absorbing institutional liquidity at attractive yields. Watch for a slowdown in bond buying and a pickup in equity turnover as a potential signal of rotation.
  • Foreign selling resumed on Monday. Watch whether this continues — sustained foreign selling would add supply to the equity market and could keep prices under pressure.
  • The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any change in interest rates would be a major event for both bonds and equities.

Long-Term:

The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and the rotation between asset classes — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

  • Don't chase volatile counters. MCB's 4.1% drop and DCB's 1.3% decline are reminders that sharp moves can reverse quickly.
  • Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.
