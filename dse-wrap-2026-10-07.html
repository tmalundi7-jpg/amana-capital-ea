Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 7th October 2026

Block Trades Dominate as Order Books Reveal a Market of Two Halves

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange delivered its most active session of the week on Wednesday, with equity turnover surging 154.8% to TZS 5.68 billion. Two large block trades — TBL (200,000 shares) and TCCL (240,000 shares) — crossed the pre-arranged board, while foreign selling accounted for 57.88% of turnover. Yet the All-Share Index fell only 12.56 points. The story beneath the surface is one of stark divergence: some counters are extremely offer-heavy, while others are extremely bid-heavy. For everyday investors, understanding these differences is essential.

The session returned to the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 5.68 billion, driven almost entirely by the two block trades. The deal count fell 8.3% to 3,070, confirming that the surge was institutional rather than retail-driven.

3. In Focus: When Order Books Tell Opposite Stories

Wednesday's session provided a powerful lesson in how different stocks can tell very different stories through their order books. Two large-cap counters — CRDB and VODA — closed with opposite imbalances:

What does this mean in plain terms?

When a stock closes with bids far exceeding offers — as VODA and MCB did — it means there are many more people wanting to buy than to sell. This is a strong signal of demand. The price is likely to find support and may continue to rise until supply increases.

When a stock closes with offers far exceeding bids — as CRDB and DCB did — it means there is more supply than demand. Sellers are competing to exit, and buyers can afford to be patient. This suggests the stock may continue to drift lower until the excess supply is absorbed.

What should everyday investors do?

The lesson is not to panic. It is to understand that markets are not uniform. Different stocks tell different stories, and the order book reveals what the price alone cannot. A stock that falls on heavy volume with a balanced book may be near a bottom. A stock that falls with an offer-heavy book may have further to go. A stock that rises with a bid-heavy book may have further to run.

The key is to focus on the order book, not just the price. Understanding these dynamics is essential for making informed decisions.

4. Bond Market: Institutions Lock In Duration at Scale

Government bond turnover exploded to TZS 20.09 billion in face value on Wednesday — the largest in over a week. The most active instruments were:

  • 20-year 15.25% bond (Feb 2025): TZS 15.03 billion at 108.80% to 115.00%, yielding 13.36% to 14.23%
  • 7-year 10.08% bond (Oct 2020): TZS 4.54 billion at 108.35%, yielding 6.47%
  • 20-year 15.49% bond (Nov 2019): TZS 0.28 billion at 137.22%, yielding 10.99%
  • 20-year 13.00% bond (Dec 2025): TZS 0.10 billion at 111.03%, yielding 12.13%
  • 25-year 12.56% bond (Jun 2023): TZS 0.01 billion at 100.73%, yielding 12.69%

Corporate bond activity was modest, with trades in the 3-year 11.00% bond (TZS 0.60 million), 3-year 12.50% bond (TZS 36.00 million), and 5-year 12.00% bond (TZS 6.50 million).

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to attract massive institutional demand. The 20-year 15.25% bond, which traded TZS 15.03 billion in a single session, offers a yield of 13.36% to 14.23% tax-free. This is an attractive return for those seeking predictable, long-term income.

The 7-year 10.08% bond at 6.47% tax-free offers a shorter duration option for those who prefer less interest rate risk.

The simultaneous strength of both the bond and equity markets tells us that this is not a one-way rotation. Institutions are deploying capital across both asset classes. A balanced approach — some capital in bonds for stability, some in quality equities for growth — can work well in this environment.

  • CRDB closed at 2,900 with an extremely offer-heavy order book (4:1 offers to bids). The stock may face near-term weakness as sellers wait for buyers. A pullback to 2,870 or below would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • VODA closed at 1,260 with a strongly bid-heavy order book (2.4:1 bids to offers). The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst. A pullback to 1,240 would represent an opportunity.
  • NMB closed at 2,030 with an offer-heavy order book (1.4:1 offers to bids). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted until the ex-date is announced.
  • TCC closed at 13,360, remaining in its pre-dividend window. The net dividend yield remains attractive at over 7.4%.
  • TBL closed at 9,750 after absorbing a 200,000-share block; the net dividend yield is 7.97% — the highest in the market.
  • MCB rose 2.7% with an extremely bid-heavy order book (29.7:1). The stock may continue to rise until supply increases.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026):

  • The bond market is absorbing massive institutional liquidity at attractive yields. Watch for a slowdown in bond buying and a pickup in equity turnover as a potential signal of rotation.
  • Foreign selling resumed on Wednesday. Watch whether this continues — sustained foreign selling would add supply to the equity market and could keep prices under pressure.
  • The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any change in interest rates would be a major event for both bonds and equities.

Long-Term:

The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and the rotation between asset classes — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.
