Daily DSE Wrap | Thursday, 8th October 2026

Equity Turnover Retreats to NORMAL Regime as Institutions Favour Duration Over Stocks

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange delivered a session that laid bare a structural shift in institutional preferences. Equity turnover fell 64.9% to TZS 1.99 billion — dropping below the TZS 3 billion threshold for only the second time this month — while government bond turnover rose 29% to TZS 25.96 billion. The 7-year 10.08% bond traded at a record-low yield of 6.37%. This is not a market in distress; it is a market where institutional capital has migrated decisively to fixed income, and equity liquidity has thinned accordingly. For everyday investors, understanding this divergence is essential.

3. In Focus: When Two Markets Diverge — What It Means for Everyday Investors

Thursday’s session delivered a clear divergence between the equity and bond markets. Equity turnover fell 64.9% to TZS 1.99 billion, while bond turnover rose 29% to TZS 25.96 billion. The 7-year 10.08% bond traded at a yield of just 6.37% — the tightest on record.

What does this mean in plain terms?

When institutions buy government bonds, they are parking cash in a safe, predictable asset. When they sell bonds or stop buying, that cash has to go somewhere. On Thursday, the opposite happened: institutions aggressively bought bonds, locking in tax-free yields of 6.37% to 12.13%. This drained liquidity from the equity market.

The equity order books reflect this. CRDB closed with 309,852 offers against 175,602 bids — a ratio of 1.8 to 1 in favour of sellers. NMB closed with 111,962 offers against 20,435 bids — a ratio of 5.5 to 1. VODA closed with 57,404 offers against 19,044 bids — a ratio of 3 to 1.

What should everyday investors do?

The lesson is not to panic. It is to understand that markets move in cycles. When one asset class attracts massive institutional flows, the other may temporarily lose liquidity. This creates opportunities for patient investors who understand where the real demand lies.

The key is to focus on order books, not just prices. A stock that closes with bids far exceeding offers is very different from one that closes with offers far exceeding bids. CRDB’s offer-heavy book suggests near-term weakness; MCB’s improving book suggests demand may be returning.

Understanding these dynamics is essential for making informed decisions.

4. Bond Market: Institutions Lock In Duration at Record-Low Yields

Government bond turnover rose 29% to TZS 25.96 billion on Thursday. The most active instruments were:

  • 20-year 15.25% bond (Feb 2025): TZS 15.03 billion at 125.00% to 134.74%, yielding 11.07% to 12.13%
  • 15-year 12.25% bond (Feb 2026): TZS 5.93 billion at 113.70% to 114.82%, yielding 10.45% to 10.59%
  • 7-year 10.08% bond (Oct 2020): TZS 4.54 billion at 108.47%, yielding 6.37%
  • 20-year 14.00% bond (Sep 2025): TZS 0.24 billion at 125.46%, yielding 10.94%
  • 25-year 12.56% bond (Dec 2022): TZS 0.17 billion at 111.93% to 117.30%, yielding 10.90% to 11.48%

Corporate bond activity was minimal, with a single trade in the 5-year 12.00% bond at 100.00% of face value.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to attract massive institutional demand. The 20-year 15.25% bond, which traded TZS 15.03 billion in a single session, offers a yield of 11.07% to 12.13% tax-free. The 7-year 10.08% bond at 6.37% tax-free offers a shorter duration option for those who prefer less interest rate risk.

The 6.37% yield on the 7-year bond is the tightest on record. This tells us that institutions are willing to accept lower yields to lock in long-term, tax-free income. The era of double-digit yields on shorter maturities is ending.

For those seeking predictable, long-term income, the 15-year and 20-year bonds at over 10% tax-free remain attractive. However, with turnover concentrated in a few large trades, liquidity can be limited. Investors should be patient and wait for opportunities that suit their goals.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Mid-October):

  • CRDB closed at 2,890 with an offer-heavy order book (1.8:1 offers to bids). The stock may face near-term weakness as sellers wait for buyers. A pullback to 2,860 or below would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • VODA closed at 1,260 with an offer-heavy order book (3:1 offers to bids). The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst. A pullback to 1,240 would represent an opportunity.
  • NMB closed at 2,010 with an extremely offer-heavy order book (5.5:1 offers to bids). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted until the ex-date is announced.
  • TCC closed at 13,360, remaining in its pre-dividend window. The net dividend yield remains attractive at over 7.4%.
  • TBL closed at 9,700; the net dividend yield is 8.01% — the highest in the market.
  • TCCL fell 7.3% and remains volatile; avoid chasing.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026):

  • The bond market is absorbing massive institutional liquidity at attractive yields. Watch for a slowdown in bond buying and a pickup in equity turnover as a potential signal of rotation.
  • Foreign selling continued on Thursday. Watch whether this persists — sustained foreign selling would add supply to the equity market and could keep prices under pressure.
  • The Bank of Tanzania’s monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any change in interest rates would be a major event for both bonds and equities.

Long-Term:

The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and the rotation between asset classes — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the order-book or rotation concepts are new to you, read Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs and Understanding Market Data.
  • Read the order book, not just the price. A stock that closes with bids far exceeding offers is very different from one that closes with offers far exceeding bids. The order book tells you what the price alone cannot.
  • Watch both markets for signals. When both bonds and equities attract liquidity, institutions are deploying capital across asset classes. When one rises while the other falls, a rotation may be underway.
  • Respect the daily price bands. They are designed to keep trading orderly and prevent panic-driven moves. Always calculate the allowed range before placing an order.
  • Don’t chase volatile counters. TCCL’s 7.3% drop and TOL’s 3.4% decline are reminders that sharp moves can reverse quickly.
  • Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Thursday’s session — with equity turnover falling into the NORMAL regime, bond turnover rising 29% to TZS 25.96 billion, and the 7-year bond trading at a record-low 6.37% yield — is a reminder that markets are interconnected. When institutions aggressively buy bonds, they drain liquidity from equities. When they sell bonds, that cash can flow back into stocks. Understanding these flows is essential for navigating the market. For the multi-year investor, the message is clear: this is a market that rewards patience, education, and the willingness to adapt. The opportunities are there — in CRDB’s offer-heavy pause, in VODA’s pre-dividend accumulation, in the 11.07% government bond yield that may not last — but they require discipline to capture. The crowd chases sharp moves; the wise investor reads order books, respects the price bands, and lets the market’s natural rhythm do the heavy lifting.
