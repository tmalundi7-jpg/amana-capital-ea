Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 9th October 2026

Foreign Selling Slows to a Trickle as Local Institutions Absorb Another Round of Block Supply

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange closed the week with a session that confirmed a quiet but important shift in market dynamics. Equity turnover rose 93.1% to TZS 3.85 billion, moving back above the TZS 3 billion threshold after Thursday’s brief dip. Two large block trades — CRDB (262,000 shares) and NMB (491,000 shares) — crossed the pre-arranged board. Yet foreign selling fell to just 7.15% of turnover, the lowest in weeks. Local institutions absorbed every share without price damage. This is a market where the balance of power has shifted decisively to domestic capital.

VODA rose 0.8% to 1,270. The order book is bid-heavy: 62,590 bids against 32,477 offers — a ratio of 1.9 to 1 in favour of buyers.

3. In Focus: The Slowdown in Foreign Selling — What It Means for Everyday Investors

Friday’s session delivered a notable development: foreign selling slowed to just 7.15% of turnover, the lowest in weeks. This is a significant shift from the 20-65% selling rates seen in recent weeks.

Why does this matter?

For months, foreign investors have been net sellers on the DSE. They were exiting positions, creating supply that local institutions had to absorb. This dynamic kept a lid on prices and created opportunities for patient buyers. Now, the selling has slowed to a trickle.

What does this tell us? It suggests that the foreign exit is largely complete. The institutions that wanted to reduce their Tanzanian exposure have done so. The remaining foreign holders are either long-term investors or are waiting for better prices.

What should everyday investors take from this?

The lesson is not to rush into the market. It is to understand that the balance of power has shifted. When foreign selling slows, it removes a source of supply from the market. This can support prices and create opportunities for local investors.

The key is to focus on order books, not just prices. A stock that closes with bids far exceeding offers — as NMB did on Friday — is very different from one that closes with offers far exceeding bids. The order book tells you where the real demand lies.

For everyday investors, this is a time to focus on fundamentals. Identify the stocks you want to own, set your limit prices, and wait for the market to come to you. The slowdown in foreign selling is a positive development, but it does not mean the market will rise in a straight line.

4. Bond Market: Turnover Contracts but Yields Remain Attractive

Government bond turnover fell 40.6% to TZS 15.41 billion on Friday from Thursday’s TZS 25.96 billion. The most active instruments were:

  • 5-year 10.25% bond (Apr 2026): TZS 7.12 billion at 108.08%, yielding 10.19%
  • 20-year 12.10% bond (Jul 2022): TZS 3.37 billion at 110.05% to 114.28%, yielding 10.81%
  • 25-year 15.95% bond (Apr 2021): TZS 2.96 billion at 100.00% to 143.67%, yielding 11.14% to 16.43%
  • 15-year 13.50% bond (Feb 2024): TZS 0.80 billion at 110.25%, yielding 11.43%
  • 25-year 13.25% bond (Feb 2026): TZS 0.27 billion at 115.00%, yielding 11.72%

Corporate bond activity was nil.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to offer attractive yields for those seeking predictable, tax-free income. The 5-year 10.25% bond at 10.19% tax-free is a particularly attractive option for those who prefer shorter duration. The 25-year 15.95% bond at 11.14% to 16.43% tax-free offers higher yields for those willing to accept longer duration.

The contraction in turnover from Thursday’s level suggests that institutions may be pausing their duration-buying spree. This could be a signal that the rotation from bonds to equities is beginning, or it could simply be a pause before the next wave of buying.

For those considering bonds, the key is to focus on quality and yield. The 5-year and 10-year bonds at over 10% tax-free are attractive. However, with turnover concentrated in a few large trades, liquidity can be limited. Investors should be patient and wait for opportunities that suit their goals.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Mid-October):

  • CRDB closed at 2,900 with an offer-heavy order book (2.3:1 offers to bids). The stock may face near-term weakness as sellers wait for buyers. A pullback to 2,880 or below would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • VODA closed at 1,270 with a bid-heavy order book (1.9:1 bids to offers). The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst. A pullback to 1,250 would represent an opportunity.
  • NMB closed at 2,010 with a bid-heavy order book (2.2:1 bids to offers). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted until the ex-date is announced.
  • TCC closed at 13,380, remaining in its pre-dividend window. The net dividend yield remains attractive at over 7.4%.
  • TBL closed at 9,680; the net dividend yield is 8.03% — the highest in the market.
  • MUCOBA fell 14.4% and remains volatile; avoid.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026):

  • Foreign selling has slowed to a trickle. Watch whether this continues — a sustained slowdown would remove a source of supply from the market and could support prices.
  • The bond market continues to attract institutional demand, with yields compressing. Watch for a pickup in equity turnover alongside a slowdown in bond buying as a potential signal of rotation.
  • The Bank of Tanzania’s monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any change in interest rates would be a major event for both bonds and equities.

Long-Term:

The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and now the slowdown in foreign selling — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.
