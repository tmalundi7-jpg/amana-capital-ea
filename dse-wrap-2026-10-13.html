Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 13th October 2026

Bond Market Surge Drains Equity Liquidity as Bank Order Books Turn Heavily Offer-Weighted

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange delivered a session of stark contrasts on Tuesday. Equity turnover eased to TZS 3.22 billion, still in high territory but down nearly 10% from Monday. Meanwhile, government bond turnover exploded to TZS 42.19 billion — the largest single-day figure this month — as institutions aggressively locked in tax-free yields above 10.5%. Behind these headline numbers, the order books told an even more important story: key bank counters like NMB and DCB closed with offers exceeding bids by more than 14 times, while a handful of others remained firmly bid-heavy. For everyday investors, understanding these divergences is essential.

3. In Focus: When the Bond Market Surges and Equity Order Books Diverge

Tuesday's session delivered two important signals that every investor should understand.

First, the bond market surge. Government bond turnover exploded to TZS 42.19 billion — the largest single-day figure this month. The 15-year 12.25% bond alone traded TZS 30.93 billion at yields of 10.55% to 10.60% tax-free. This tells us that institutions are aggressively locking in long-term, tax-free income. When institutions buy bonds, they are parking cash in a safe, predictable asset. When they do so in massive size, it can drain liquidity from the equity market.

Second, the order book divergence. Different stocks told very different stories through their order books:

What does this mean for everyday investors?

When a stock closes with offers far exceeding bids — as NMB and DCB did — it means there is more supply than demand. Sellers are competing to exit, and buyers can afford to be patient. This suggests the stock may continue to drift lower until the excess supply is absorbed.

When a stock closes with bids far exceeding offers — as TBL, TPCC, VODA, and TCCL did — it means demand is strong and supply is thin. This suggests the stock may find support and could rise if buying continues.

The lesson is simple: pay attention to the order book, not just the price. A stock that falls on heavy volume with a balanced book may be near a bottom. A stock that falls with an offer-heavy book may have further to go. A stock that rises with a bid-heavy book may have further to run.

Understanding these dynamics is essential for making informed decisions.

4. Bond Market: Institutions Lock In Duration at Scale

Government bond turnover exploded to TZS 42.19 billion on Tuesday — the largest of the month. The most active instruments were:

  • 15-year 12.25% bond (Feb 2026): TZS 30.93 billion at 113.84% to 114.24%, yielding 10.55% to 10.60%
  • 5-year 10.25% bond (Apr 2026): TZS 7.12 billion at 108.83%, yielding 10.01%
  • 25-year 12.56% bond (May 2023): TZS 2.51 billion at 100.00% to 117.88%, yielding 11.02% to 12.82%
  • 25-year 15.75% bond (Jan 2025): TZS 1.00 billion at 147.18%, yielding 10.69%
  • 20-year 15.49% bond (Sep 2020): TZS 0.25 billion at 100.00% to 138.44%, yielding 10.52% to 16.23%

Corporate bond activity was minimal, with a single trade in the 5-year 12.00% bond at 100.00% of face value.

What does this mean for everyday investors?

The bond market continues to attract massive institutional demand. The 15-year 12.25% bond, which traded TZS 30.93 billion in a single session, offers a yield of 10.55% to 10.60% tax-free. The 5-year 10.25% bond at 10.01% tax-free offers a shorter duration option.

The surge in bond turnover tells us that institutions are willing to accept lower yields on shorter maturities to lock in long-term, tax-free income. For those seeking predictable, long-term income, the 15-year and 20-year bonds at over 10.5% tax-free remain attractive. However, with turnover concentrated in a few large trades, liquidity can be limited. Investors should be patient and wait for opportunities that suit their goals.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (Mid-October):

  • CRDB closed at 2,840 with an offer-heavy order book. The stock may face near-term weakness as sellers wait for buyers. A pullback to 2,800 or below would represent an opportunity for patient buyers.
  • VODA closed at 1,280 with a bid-heavy order book (2.7:1 bids to offers). The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst. A pullback to 1,260 would represent an opportunity.
  • NMB closed at 2,000 with an extremely offer-heavy order book (14.5:1 offers to bids). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted until the ex-date is announced.
  • TCC closed at 13,350, remaining in its pre-dividend window. The net dividend yield remains attractive at over 7.4%.
  • TBL closed at 9,690 with a complete bid wall; the net dividend yield is 8.02% — the highest in the market.
  • DCB fell 1.3% with an extremely offer-heavy book; avoid.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026):

  • The bond market is absorbing massive institutional liquidity. Watch for a slowdown in bond buying and a pickup in equity turnover as a potential signal of rotation.
  • Foreign selling surged on Tuesday. Watch whether this continues — sustained foreign selling would add supply to the equity market and could keep prices under pressure.
  • The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any change in interest rates would be a major event for both bonds and equities.

Long-Term:

The events of the past several weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and the rotation between asset classes — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the order-book or rotation concepts are new to you, read Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs and Understanding Market Data.
  • Read the order book, not just the price. A stock that closes with bids far exceeding offers is very different from one that closes with offers far exceeding bids. The order book tells you what the price alone cannot.
  • Watch both markets for signals. When bond turnover surges, institutions are locking in duration. When it contracts, they may be rotating into equities. Both signals are useful.
  • Respect the daily price bands. They are designed to keep trading orderly and prevent panic-driven moves. Always calculate the allowed range before placing an order.
  • Don't chase volatile counters. MUCOBA's 6.5% gain and CRDB's 1.7% decline are reminders that sharp moves can reverse quickly.
  • Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Tuesday's session — with bond turnover exploding to TZS 42.19 billion, foreign selling surging to 58.52% of turnover, and order books in NMB and DCB showing extreme offer-heavy imbalances — is a reminder that markets are interconnected. When institutions aggressively buy bonds, they drain liquidity from equities. When foreign investors sell, local institutions absorb the supply. Understanding these flows is essential for navigating the market. For the multi-year investor, the message is clear: this is a market that rewards patience, education, and the willingness to adapt. The opportunities are there — in CRDB's offer-heavy pause, in VODA's bid-heavy book, in the 10.55% government bond yield that may not last — but they require discipline to capture. The crowd chases sharp moves; the wise investor reads order books, respects the price bands, and lets the market's natural rhythm do the heavy lifting.
