Daily DSE Wrap | Monday, 19th October 2026

A TZS 357 Million NMB Block and a New 25-Year Bond's First Day

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange opened the week with a quieter equity session and a small piece of history in the bond market. Equities cooled 22% to TZS 2.84 billion in turnover as institutions stepped back, though a 182,910-share block in NMB — worth about TZS 357 million — crossed at the prevailing price without moving it. The indices slipped gently, with the All-Share Index easing 8 points to 4,564.52. The real milestone: the brand-new 25-year government bond, issued just three days ago, traded for the first time — TZS 23.26 billion changing hands as investors set the price on a fresh quarter-century benchmark.

The pattern flipped from Friday: more deals, smaller tickets. That is retail participation rather than institutional flow — thousands of smaller trades replacing Friday's block-driven volume. Foreign investors were net sellers for a second straight session (roughly TZS 530 million out), yet the All-Share Index fell just 8 points. When heavy selling arrives two days running and prices barely flinch, domestic demand is doing the heavy lifting — one of the healthiest signals a frontier market can send.

3. In Focus: Block Trades and the Daily Price Bands

Monday's NMB block trade is worth understanding, because large pre-arranged trades are becoming a regular feature of the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange (DSE) — Friday saw one in CRDB, Monday's is in NMB. A block trade lets an institution buy or sell a large quantity of shares in a single negotiated transaction, rather than feeding small orders into the market over days. The lesson for the rest of us: when a block crosses at the prevailing price without moving it, the market is deep enough to absorb institutional flow. That is a sign of maturity, not of anything about the company's prospects.

The second lesson is about the daily price bands, which protect every investor on the DSE. Because NMB closed at 1,990 on Monday, it cannot trade on Tuesday, 20 October, outside a band of ±5% around that price:

  • Upper limit: 1,990 × 1.05 = 2,089.50
  • Lower limit: 1,990 × 0.95 = 1,890.50

4. Bond Market: A New 25-Year Benchmark Is Born

Government bond turnover settled to TZS 42.45 billion across 17 deals — down from Friday's 53.13 billion, but carrying a milestone. The 25-year bond at 12.75% coupon, issued just three days ago on 16 October, traded for the first time: TZS 23.26 billion in face value across 6 deals, priced between 100% and 125.2% of par. Watching a fresh long-dated benchmark find its price is a genuine market-development moment — it gives investors a reference point for valuing every other long-term asset in the country.

Elsewhere on the board: the 15-year 12.25% bond moved TZS 4 billion in face value at 118.77% of par (a yield of about 10%), the 2-year 9.5% bond moved TZS 10 billion at 105.3% (yield about 7.5%), and the 20-year 14% bond moved TZS 5 billion at 104.94% (yield about 13.5%). On the corporate side, two small issues traded: SAMIA's 5-year 12% bond (TZS 10 million face at 100%, yield 12.85%) and TCB-STAWI's 5-year 13.5% bond (TZS 20 million face at 105%, yield 12.68%).

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming days):

  • Back-to-back blocks (CRDB Friday, NMB Monday) suggest institutional repositioning is under way. Watch whether NMB holds 1,990 — a break above 2,000 with volume would signal the block's buyer accumulating further; a slip toward 1,950 suggests more supply to come.
  • AFRIPRISE (−2.07% on heavy volume) is the counter to watch on the downside: nearly 46,000 shares traded with the price falling, which suggests sellers are in control for now.
  • Foreign selling has now run two sessions at roughly TZS 530–630 million per day. A third day would test how deep the domestic bid really is.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • The new 25-year benchmark will be watched closely: if it settles at yields near 10–11%, it anchors expectations for everything from corporate borrowing costs to pension fund returns.
  • Dividend season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.9% net) and TCC (7.4% net) tend to attract attention.

Long-Term:

A market that takes two institutional blocks in its stride, absorbs two days of heavy foreign selling without a wobble, and prices a brand-new 25-year benchmark on day one is a market standing on its own feet. For the patient, long-term investor, that depth and self-reliance is exactly the foundation on which compounding wealth is built. ---

When a market can take two billion-shilling blocks in two days and still close essentially flat, it has acquired something more valuable than a rally: resilience. The DSE's growing institutional participation — visible in the equity block board and in a brand-new 25-year bond priced on its first day of trading — means prices increasingly reflect considered capital allocation rather than retail sentiment.
