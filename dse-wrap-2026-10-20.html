Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 20th October 2026

Two Institutional Blocks, TZS 776 Million of Foreign Selling — and the Index Barely Moved

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange delivered its loudest session of the week. Equity turnover jumped 48% to TZS 4.20 billion, driven by two institutional block trades totalling about TZS 1.17 billion — a 120,000-share block in CRDB and a 417,870-share block in NMB. Foreign investors sold a third straight session, a record TZS 776 million out, and the All-Share Index still gave up only 3 points to close at 4,561.14. In the bond market, the brand-new 25-year benchmark traded its second day, with TZS 8.53 billion changing hands as the long end settles near 10.5%.

Strip out the blocks and the session was still healthier than Monday: underlying turnover rose about 22% even before the big trades. The headline number, though, belongs to the institutions. And the most telling number of all may be the smallest: the index fell 3 points on a day when foreigners sold TZS 776 million of stock — the heaviest outflow of a three-day run totalling roughly TZS 1.9 billion, against which the index has shed just 12 points. When a market takes that much selling and barely flinches, domestic demand is doing the heavy lifting — one of the healthiest signals a frontier market can send.

3. In Focus: Reading a Block Trade From the Numbers

Tuesday's two block trades are worth understanding, because the official report never tells you the block price — yet it can be read anyway. The report gives, for each counter, the normal-board volume and its weighted average price, plus the day's total turnover. Subtract the normal board (volume × average price) from the total and divide by the block size, and the block price falls out:

  • NMB: the 417,870-share block implies a crossing price of about TZS 1,913 — roughly 4% below the 1,990 close. An institution exited at a discount, and the market price never wobbled.
  • CRDB: the 120,000-share block implies about TZS 2,827, essentially at the 2,830 close.

That is the anatomy of absorption: a large holder accepting a discount to exit, buyers stepping in at the prevailing price. This is a sign of market maturity, not a signal about either company's prospects.

The second lesson is the daily price bands, which protect every investor on the DSE. Because NMB closed at 1,990 on Tuesday, it cannot trade on Wednesday, 21 October, outside a band of ±5% around that price:

  • Upper limit: 1,990 × 1.05 = 2,089.50
  • Lower limit: 1,990 × 0.95 = 1,890.50

4. Bond Market: The New 25-Year Benchmark Finds Its Level

Government bond turnover fell to TZS 24.15 billion across 27 deals — quieter than Monday, but carrying the session's real information. The 25-year 12.75% bond issued four days ago traded its second day: TZS 8.53 billion in face value across 7 deals, priced between 100% and 120% of par (yields 10.49–12.76%), with the anchor 7.96-billion clip at 119.43% — a yield of about 10.5%. The long end of Tanzania's yield curve is finding its level, and it is landing near ten and a half percent.

Elsewhere: the 20-year 14% bond moved TZS 5 billion in face value at 146.71% of par (a yield of just 9.0% — long-dated paper pricing like money is cheap); the 5-year 10.25% bond moved TZS 5 billion at 108.32% (yield about 10.2%). On the corporate side, two small issues traded at par: FHF's 4-year 13% bond (yield 13.24%) and SAMIA's 5-year 12% bond (yield 12.86%) — corporate borrowers still paying roughly 200–300 basis points over the sovereign for five-year money.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming days):

  • Three blocks in three sessions (CRDB Friday, NMB Monday, CRDB and NMB Tuesday) confirm institutional repositioning is under way. Watch whether NMB holds 1,990 — the session-end order book is heavily offer-weighted (837,810 offered vs 43,057 bid), so more supply may be waiting above.
  • Foreign selling has now run three sessions totalling roughly TZS 1.9 billion. A fourth day would test whether the domestic bid has a limit.
  • AFRIPRISE (−0.70% on 28,000 shares) is the downside watch: two sessions of real-volume selling.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • The new 25-year benchmark settling near 10.5% will anchor expectations for everything from corporate borrowing costs to pension fund returns.
  • Dividend season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.8% net) and TCC (7.4% net) tend to attract attention.

Long-Term:

A market that intermediates institutional blocks at a discount, absorbs three days of foreign selling without a wobble, and prices a brand-new 25-year benchmark on day two is a market standing on its own feet. For the patient, long-term investor, that depth and self-reliance is exactly the foundation on which compounding wealth is built. ---

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

When a market can take TZS 1.9 billion of foreign selling in three days and close each day essentially flat, it has acquired something more valuable than a rally: resilience. The DSE's growing institutional participation — visible in the block board and in a brand-new 25-year bond finding its price on day two — means prices increasingly reflect considered capital allocation rather than retail sentiment.
