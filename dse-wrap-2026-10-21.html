Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 21st October 2026

TZS 31 Billion Flows Into Bonds as One Quiet Price Cut Moves the Headline Index

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange delivered a lesson in reading beneath the headline on Wednesday. The All-Share Index fell 25 points to 4,536.37 — yet the entire decline came from a single share that did not trade at all: the exchange adjusted EABL's reference price from 5,860 to 5,560 shillings, wiping roughly TZS 237 billion off the index's market capitalisation in one administrative move. Strip that out and the domestic market actually rose — the Tanzania Share Index gained 8 points to 10,072.66, its strongest level of the week. Meanwhile, the bond market absorbed TZS 31.13 billion in 28 deals as institutions continued to lock in long-term income. For everyday investors, Wednesday was a masterclass in a single principle: the index is a number, the market is what is actually trading.

Equity activity cooled sharply after Tuesday's block-driven surge, with turnover falling 42% and no large pre-arranged trades except a single NMB block of 186,630 shares. The day's real divide was between the two indices: the All-Share Index, which includes cross-listed foreign companies, fell on the EABL adjustment — while the Tanzania Share Index, which tracks only Tanzanian-incorporated companies, rose to its best level of the week. When the two indices move in opposite directions, the domestic one tells you what local investors are actually doing.

Foreign investors sold a net TZS 614 million of shares — the fourth consecutive session of foreign outflows, totalling roughly TZS 2.55 billion — and the domestic market still rose. When heavy selling fails to push prices down four days running, it tells you domestic demand is deep — one of the healthiest signals a frontier market can send.

3. In Focus: Why Two Indices Moved in Opposite Directions

Wednesday's split between the All-Share Index and the Tanzania Share Index is worth understanding, because it reveals what each index actually measures. The DSEI includes every company listed on the exchange — including large cross-listed firms from neighbouring markets such as EABL, JHL and KCB. The TSI includes only companies incorporated in Tanzania. On Wednesday, EABL's reference price was adjusted downward by the exchange (from 5,860 to 5,560 shillings) even though not a single EABL share traded. Because EABL is one of the largest companies by market value on the exchange, that one adjustment dragged the whole DSEI down 25 points.

The TSI, which excludes EABL entirely, rose 8 points — reflecting what Tanzanian companies actually did. The practical lesson: when you hear the market "fell" or "rose", check which index is being quoted. A move driven by one untraded foreign counter tells you nothing about the Tanzanian businesses you might actually own. The domestic market — the one that matters to local investors — had a good day.

4. Bond Market: The New 25-Year Benchmark Finds Its Level

Government bond turnover rose 29% to TZS 31.13 billion across 27 deals — and the story of the week continued to be the brand-new 25-year bond issued on 16 October, which traded its third session: TZS 3.47 billion changed hands in 22 separate deals at prices between 100% and 120% of face value. When a new bond trades actively for three straight days and the prices cluster tighter each time, it means the market is collectively agreeing on what long-term Tanzanian government debt is worth — currently, yields around 10.5%.

The session's largest single print was the 15-year bond at 12.25% interest: TZS 22 billion changed hands at 118.86% of face value, locking in a yield of about 10%. The 25-year bond at 15.75% interest moved TZS 5.5 billion at 148.65% of face value. On the corporate side, the SAMIA 5-year bond traded a small TZS 12.1 million at face value. The message for savers: high-quality, tax-free income of around 10–11% a year remains available for those willing to lend to the government for the long term.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (rest of the week):

  • Watch whether domestic strength broadens: the TSI at its weekly high needs the banking counters (CRDB, NMB) to keep their bid interest to sustain it.
  • CRDB closed at 2,860 with buyers queuing at the close — a move above 2,880 with volume would confirm the momentum; a slip below 2,830 would suggest the buying was a one-day event.
  • VODA has now fallen four straight sessions from 1,280 to 1,240. A stabilisation above 1,210 would be the first sign the slide is exhausting itself.
  • NMB at 1,980 carries a heavy overhang of sellers above the price — the pending dividend remains the key catalyst to watch.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • The new 25-year government bond is now a live, tradeable benchmark — its yield, settling near 10.5%, becomes the reference rate against which every long-term investment in Tanzania is measured.
  • Dividend season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.7% net) and TCC (7.4% net) tend to attract attention.

Long-Term:

A market that absorbs TZS 2.55 billion of foreign selling over four sessions while its domestic index climbs is a market standing on its own feet. Add a functioning long-term bond benchmark being priced in the open market, and you have the two pillars of a maturing capital market: equity depth and a real yield curve. For the patient, long-term investor, that is the most constructive backdrop possible. ---

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Wednesday's session will be remembered less for what the index did than for what it revealed: a market deep enough that TZS 614 million of foreign selling barely registers, sophisticated enough to price a brand-new 25-year bond in three sessions, and transparent enough that one can see exactly why the headline number moved. The domestic investor base — not foreign flows — now sets the tone.
