Daily DSE Wrap | Thursday, 22nd October 2026

Busiest Day of the Week: TZS 3.5 Billion in Equities, TZS 54 Billion in Bonds — and One Buyer Pays Up for Half a Million Vodacom Shares

Thursday was the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange's busiest session of the week. Equity turnover jumped 44% to TZS 3.49 billion from 1.99 million shares, the Tanzania Share Index climbed 13 points to 10,085.33 — its strongest level of the week — and the bond market absorbed a remarkable TZS 54.33 billion in 62 deals. But the most interesting story of the day sits in the block trade report: two large pre-arranged trades crossed, and the numbers tell you everything. A 90,000-share block of CRDB changed hands at roughly 2,875 shillings — close to the market price, a routine institutional transfer. A 516,000-share block of VODA, though, changed hands at an implied 1,280 shillings — about 3% above the day's average market price of 1,240. When an investor pays a premium to take half a million shares in one trade, that is deliberate accumulation, not an exit. Meanwhile, foreign investors sold a net TZS 827 million of shares — their fifth straight selling session, totalling about TZS 3.38 billion — and the domestic market still closed at its weekly high. For everyday investors, Thursday was confirmation of the week's theme: the domestic investor base is doing the buying, and it is doing it confidently.

When heavy selling fails to push prices down five days running, it tells you domestic demand is deep — one of the healthiest signals a frontier market can send.

3. In Focus: What a Block Trade Tells You

Most investors watch the prices on the normal trading board — but some of the market's most honest information arrives in the block trade report. A block trade is a large pre-arranged transaction between two investors, negotiated privately and then reported to the exchange. The exchange does not publish the block price, but the report gives you enough figures to work it out: the total value of the counter's trading, minus the normal board's volume multiplied by its average price, leaves the block's value. Divide by the block's shares, and you have the implied price.

Thursday's two blocks show why this exercise is worth doing. The CRDB block of 90,000 shares implies a price of roughly 2,875 shillings — within 1% of the normal-board average of 2,900. That is a routine institutional transfer: someone sold, someone bought, at a fair price. The VODA block of 516,000 shares implies roughly 1,280 shillings — about 3% above the normal-board average of 1,240. Paying a premium to take half a million shares in one trade is not a distressed seller; it is a buyer with conviction who wanted size quickly and was willing to pay for it. The practical lesson: when a block implies a price above the market, institutions are competing for the stock. That is demand you can read — no estimates required, just the exchange's own numbers.

4. Bond Market: TZS 54 Billion Day as the 25-Year Benchmark Matures

Government bond turnover surged 75% to TZS 54.33 billion across 62 deals — and the story of the month continued to be the brand-new 25-year bond issued on 16 October, which traded its fourth session: TZS 15.60 billion changed hands in 44 separate deals at prices between 100% and 122% of face value, with yields clustering around 10.5%. When a new bond trades actively for four straight days and becomes the session's volume engine, it means the market has accepted it as the reference for long-term Tanzanian government debt — currently, yields near 10.5%.

The session's largest single print was the 2-year bond at 9.50% interest: TZS 14.50 billion changed hands at 104.95% of face value, locking in a yield of about 7.8% — short-term money is cheap and deep. The 15-year bond at 12.25% interest moved TZS 3.88 billion at about 114% of face value (yield ~10.6%), and the 15-year bond at 13.50% interest moved TZS 2.15 billion (yields ~10.5–11.0%). On the corporate side, the ITRUST 4-year bond traded TZS 58 million at 95.26% of face value — a yield of about 15.7%, roughly 4.5 percentage points above the sovereign curve on 4-year money. The message for savers: high-quality, tax-free income of around 10–11% a year remains available for those willing to lend to the government for the long term.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (end of the week):

  • Watch whether the week's domestic strength carries into Friday: the TSI at its weekly high needs the banking counters (CRDB, NMB) to keep their bid interest to sustain it.
  • CRDB closed at 2,900 with buyers still queuing at the close — the bid book was roughly four times the offer side. A move above 2,930 with volume would confirm the momentum.
  • VODA held flat at 1,240 for a second session after four straight falls. With a block buyer paying a premium at 1,280, a push above the 1,270 daily high would be the first real sign the slide has exhausted itself.
  • NMB at 1,960 carries a heavy overhang of sellers above the price — the pending dividend remains the key catalyst to watch.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • The new 25-year government bond is now a live, heavily-traded benchmark — its yield, settling near 10.5%, becomes the reference rate against which every long-term investment in Tanzania is measured.
  • Dividend season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.7% net) and TCC (7.2% net) tend to attract attention.

Long-Term:

A market that absorbs TZS 3.38 billion of foreign selling over five sessions while its domestic index climbs is a market standing on its own feet. Add a functioning long-term bond benchmark being priced in the open market, and you have the two pillars of a maturing capital market: equity depth and a real yield curve. For the patient, long-term investor, that is the most constructive backdrop possible. ---
