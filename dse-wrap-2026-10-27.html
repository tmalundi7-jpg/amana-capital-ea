Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 27th October 2026

TZS 7.6 Billion Day — Foreign Investors Sold 76% of Everything Traded for a Second Straight Session, and the Market Barely Blinked Again

Tuesday was the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange's second straight session of heavy foreign selling — and its second straight session of absorbing it. Foreign investors sold TZS 5.77 billion of shares, 76% of the day's equity turnover, while buying just TZS 380 million. Combined with Monday's TZS 11.94 billion exit, that is about TZS 17.32 billion out in two sessions. Yet the two headline indices fell less than 1% each: the DSE All Share Index slipped 0.14% to 4,495.66 and the Tanzania Share Index eased 0.90% to 9,934.38. Turnover halved from Monday's block-swollen TZS 14.03 billion to TZS 7.57 billion — but three large pre-arranged block trades still supplied 56% of it: 2 million NMB shares, 300,000 VODA shares and 100,000 CRDB shares. The normal trading board, where everyday investors buy and sell, traded only about TZS 3.31 billion — almost exactly Monday's normal-board level. For the everyday investor, Tuesday's lesson is the same as Monday's, now confirmed twice: the market's real daily run-rate is about TZS 3.3 billion, the blocks sit on top of it, and domestic demand keeps meeting foreign supply without a rout.

The two headline indices fell together again, but modestly — DSEI by 0.14%, TSI by 0.90%. This time the gap between total market value (down TZS 295.81 billion) and domestic market value (down TZS 486.98 billion) runs the other way from Monday: the domestic market fell further than the headline. The reason is the cross-listed companies — KCB rose 3.30% on a TZS 6.51 trillion market value, cushioning the headline while Tanzanian companies did the falling. When the headline and domestic figures disagree this sharply, follow the domestic one — it tells you what Tanzanian companies actually did.

Foreign participation was again almost entirely one-way: foreign buying of TZS 380.36 million against foreign selling of TZS 5,769.65 million — a net outflow of about TZS 5.39 billion in a single session, roughly half Monday's record but still larger than any single session's outflow before this week. Yet the domestic index fell under 2%. When heavy selling fails to push prices down hard, it tells you domestic demand is deep — one of the healthiest signals a frontier market can send, even on a day when the selling itself deserves attention.

KCB led the market, rising 3.3% to 2,190 — the session's standout, and the reason the headline index fell less than the domestic one. The two banking heavyweights both eased: CRDB slipped 0.7% to 2,850 on the normal board's largest volume of 597,066 shares (plus a 100,000-share block), while NMB fell 2.1% to 1,880 — but its headline volume of 2,500,954 shares is mostly the 2-million-share block, with only 500,954 shares traded on the normal board. VODA held flat at 1,250 for a third straight session, this time with a 300,000-share block on top of its normal-board trading. On the downside, TTP gave back 6.2% to 380 — reversing most of Monday's 8.0% jump, on just 926 shares, so treat the size of the move with care — and MBP fell 4.9% to 1,930, its second straight down day after Monday's 4.3% fall.

3. In Focus: Two Days of Blocks, One Pattern — the Discount Is Narrowing

Monday's wrap explained how to work out a block trade's implied price: take the counter's total reported value, subtract the normal board's volume multiplied by its average price, and divide what is left by the block's shares. Tuesday gives us a second data point for the same exercise, and the comparison is worth making.

The NMB block of 2,000,000 shares implies a price of roughly 1,799 shillings — about 4% below the day's average market price of 1,880. Monday's NMB block implied roughly 1,800 shillings against an average price of 1,920 — a 6% discount. So the discount has narrowed from 6% to 4%, but it has not closed, and this is now 5.5 million NMB shares in two sessions crossing below the market price. A seller conceding price to move size, two days running, is a seller still working through a position — not a buyer accumulating one. The other two blocks tell the calmer story: the CRDB block of 100,000 shares implies roughly 2,824 shillings, within 1% of the day's average price of 2,850, and the VODA block of 300,000 shares implies roughly 1,252 shillings, essentially at the day's average price of 1,250. Those are routine transfers. The practical lesson, two days in: do the arithmetic on every block, because the same calculation produces a routine transfer twice and an exit at a concession twice — and telling those apart is the difference between reading the market and reading the headline.

4. Bond Market: TZS 23.5 Billion Day as the Benchmark Settles

Government bond turnover eased from Monday's TZS 37.09 billion to TZS 23.52 billion in face value across 49 deals (government bonds: face value TZS 23.52 billion, transaction value TZS 25.82 billion; corporate bonds: TZS 15.00 million). A quieter day than Monday — and still a heavy one by any normal standard. The session's weight was two prints: the 5-year bond at 13.00% interest moved TZS 10.03 billion in face value at prices between 100% and 113% of par (yields about 10.5–15.1%), and the 2-year bond at 9.50% interest moved TZS 5.50 billion at about 106% of par (yield about 7.3%) — short-term money is still deep and cheap. The brand-new 25-year bond issued on 16 October, now in its second week as the market's long-term benchmark, traded TZS 2.20 billion in 9 deals at prices between 100% and 120% of face value, with yields settling around 10.5–10.7%. When a new bond trades actively for a second straight week and its yield stops wandering, it means the market has accepted it as the reference for long-term Tanzanian government debt. On the corporate side, trading was a single print — the CRDB 5-year bond at 100% of face value (yield about 12.8%). The message for savers: high-quality, tax-free income of around 10–11% a year remains available for those willing to lend to the government for the long term, while short-term money earns around 7.3%.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (this week):

  • Watch whether the foreign selling continues into a third session or exhausts itself: two days, TZS 17.32 billion out, and the NMB blocks still crossing at a discount — the discount narrowing from 6% to 4% is the first sign the pressure may be easing.
  • NMB closed at 1,880 with buyers returning below the price (bids of 90,931 shares at the close, against none on Monday) — the return of waiting buyers is the counter's near-term story.
  • VODA held flat at 1,250 for a third straight session, with buyers queuing below the price at more than double Monday's level — a base forming, or a calm before the next move.
  • CRDB closed at 2,850 with sellers queuing heavily above the price — the offer queue more than doubled from Monday, worth watching.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • The new 25-year government bond is now a settled, heavily-traded benchmark — its yield, near 10.5%, becomes the reference rate against which every long-term investment in Tanzania is measured.
  • Dividend season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.69% net) and TCC (6.99% net) tend to attract attention.

Long-Term:

A market that absorbs TZS 17.32 billion of foreign selling in two sessions while its domestic index falls under 2% on the second day is a market standing on its own feet. Add a functioning long-term bond benchmark being priced in the open market, and you have the two pillars of a maturing capital market: equity depth and a real yield curve. For the patient, long-term investor, that self-reliance — tested twice now, not assumed — is the most constructive backdrop possible, even when the exit itself is the headline. ---

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Tuesday's session will be remembered as the day the exit repeated itself — and the market repeated its answer. The numbers on the normal board were ordinary again, the headline turnover was extraordinary again, and the gap between them was three pre-arranged trades worth more than half the market. A market where domestic buyers absorb a second day of heavy foreign selling with barely a 1% fall in the headline index, where a brand-new 25-year bond settles into its role as the long-term benchmark, and where one block still concedes 4% while two others cross at the market price, is a market in the middle of a genuine deepening.
