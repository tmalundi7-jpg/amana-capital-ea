Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 28th October 2026

TZS 9.6 Billion Day — Foreign Selling Halved for a Second Session, and the Big Blocks Crossed at a Premium Instead of a Discount

Wednesday was the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange's third straight session of foreign selling — and the first in which the selling looked like it might be running out of steam. Foreign investors sold TZS 5.42 billion of shares, 57% of the day's equity turnover, while buying TZS 2.52 billion — the strongest buying share of the three-session series, up from just 5% on Tuesday. Combined with Monday's TZS 11.94 billion exit and Tuesday's TZS 5.39 billion, that is about TZS 20.23 billion out in three sessions. Yet the two headline indices fell less than 1% each: the DSE All Share Index slipped 0.91% to 4,454.85 and the Tanzania Share Index eased 0.27% to 9,907.07. Turnover rose from Tuesday's TZS 7.57 billion to TZS 9.57 billion — but three large pre-arranged block trades still supplied 66% of it: 1,310,500 NMB shares, 465,660 CRDB shares and 250,000 TBL shares. The normal trading board, where everyday investors buy and sell, traded only about TZS 3.24 billion — the third session in a row at roughly TZS 3.3 billion. For the everyday investor, Wednesday's lesson is the new one inside the familiar pattern: the market's real daily run-rate is steady, the blocks still sit on top of it, and this time the blocks crossed at prices above the market average — a buyer paying up, not a seller conceding.

The two headline indices fell together again, but modestly — DSEI by 0.91%, TSI by 0.27%. This time the gap between total market value (down TZS 355.76 billion) and domestic market value (down TZS 74.26 billion) runs the other way from Tuesday: the headline fell further than the domestic market. The reason is the cross-listed companies — KCB fell 2.74% on a TZS 6.33 trillion market value, and the exchange trimmed the reference prices of EABL (5,470→5,380) and KA (115→110) without any trades. When the headline and domestic figures disagree this sharply, follow the domestic one — it tells you what Tanzanian companies actually did, and on Wednesday Tanzanian companies fell only modestly.

Foreign participation is still one-way, but less so: foreign buying of TZS 2,515.96 million against foreign selling of TZS 5,421.44 million — a net outflow of about TZS 2.91 billion in a single session, roughly half Tuesday's, and a third of Monday's. The pace of the exit is halving each day (TZS 11.94 billion, then 5.39 billion, then 2.91 billion). When heavy selling fades session by session and still fails to push prices down hard, it tells you domestic demand is deep — one of the healthiest signals a frontier market can send, even on a day when the selling itself deserves attention.

3. In Focus: Three Days of Blocks, and the Third Day Breaks the Pattern

Monday's and Tuesday's wraps explained how to work out a block trade's implied price: take the counter's total reported value, subtract the normal board's volume multiplied by its average price, and divide what is left by the block's shares. Wednesday gives us a third data point for the same exercise, and this time the answer changes.

The NMB block of 1,310,500 shares implies a price of roughly 1,902 shillings — about 3% above the day's average market price of 1,850, and essentially the day's high of 1,900. Monday's NMB block implied roughly 1,800 shillings against an average price of 1,920 — a 6% discount. Tuesday's implied roughly 1,799 against 1,880 — a 4% discount. So the discount has not just narrowed, it has reversed: a buyer paying above the market price to move size, after two days of sellers conceding price to move size, is a different kind of trade. The CRDB block of 465,660 shares tells the same story in miniature: it implies roughly 2,865 shillings, about 1% above the day's average price of 2,850. Only the TBL block of 250,000 shares crossed below the market, implying roughly 10,000 shillings against an average price of 10,230 — a 2% discount, a routine transfer. The practical lesson, three days in: do the arithmetic on every block, because the same calculation produced an exit at a concession twice and a premium once — and telling those apart is the difference between reading the market and reading the headline.

4. Bond Market: TZS 7.6 Billion Day as the Benchmark Holds Its Yield

Government bond turnover fell sharply from Tuesday's TZS 23.52 billion to TZS 7.55 billion in face value across 24 deals (government bonds: face value TZS 7.55 billion, transaction value TZS 9.07 billion; no corporate bond trades). A much quieter day — and still a solid one, carried almost entirely by a single bond. The 25-year bond issued on 16 October, now in its third week as the market's long-term benchmark, traded TZS 6.16 billion in 10 deals at prices between 116% and 120% of face value, with yields settling around 10.5–10.9%. When a new bond trades actively for a third straight week and its yield stops wandering, it means the market has accepted it as the reference for long-term Tanzanian government debt. The rest of the session was smaller prints: the 25-year bond at 12.56% interest moved TZS 0.95 billion at prices between 115% and 121% of par (yields about 10.7%), and the 20-year bond at 14.00% interest moved TZS 0.21 billion at about 128% of par (yield about 10.7%). The message for savers: high-quality, tax-free income of around 10–11% a year remains available for those willing to lend to the government for the long term, while the benchmark's steady yield gives every other long-term investment in Tanzania a reference rate to be measured against.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (this week):

  • Watch whether the foreign selling keeps halving or flattens out: three days, TZS 20.23 billion out, but the pace has fallen from 11.94 billion to 5.39 billion to 2.91 billion — and the NMB block crossing at a premium is the first sign the pressure may be turning.
  • NMB closed at 1,850 with the block crossing above the market price — the premium, not the queue, is the counter's near-term story.
  • VODA rose 0.8% to 1,260 on light volume with no block — a quiet session after two block-heavy ones, and buyers still queuing well below the price at the close.
  • CRDB held flat at 2,850 with sellers still queuing heavily above the price — the offer queue eased from Tuesday but remains the counter's near-term overhang.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • The new 25-year government bond is now a settled, heavily-traded benchmark — its yield, near 10.5%, becomes the reference rate against which every long-term investment in Tanzania is measured.
  • Dividend season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.60% net) and TCC (7.00% net) tend to attract attention.

Long-Term:

A market that absorbs TZS 20.23 billion of foreign selling in three sessions while its domestic index falls under 1% on the third day is a market standing on its own feet. Add a functioning long-term bond benchmark being priced in the open market, and you have the two pillars of a maturing capital market: equity depth and a real yield curve. For the patient, long-term investor, that self-reliance — tested, not assumed, three days in a row — is the most constructive backdrop possible, even when the exit itself is the headline. ---

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Wednesday's session will be remembered as the day the exit started to fade — and the blocks changed sides. The numbers on the normal board were ordinary again, the headline turnover was extraordinary again, and the gap between them was three pre-arranged trades worth two-thirds of the market. A market where foreign selling halves for a second straight session, where a brand-new 25-year bond holds its yield for a third week, and where one block crosses at a premium while two others cross near or below the market price, is a market in the middle of a genuine deepening.
