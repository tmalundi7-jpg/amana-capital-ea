Daily DSE Wrap | Thursday, 29th October 2026

TZS 6.7 Billion Day — Foreign Buying Dried Up Completely, and Two Blocks Now Carry Three-Quarters of the Market

Thursday was the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange's fourth straight session of foreign selling — and the first in which nobody from abroad bought at all. Foreign investors sold TZS 4.03 billion of shares, 60% of the day's equity turnover, while foreign buying registered exactly 0.00%. Combined with the three previous sessions, that is about TZS 24.26 billion out in four sessions (TZS 11.94 billion, then 5.39 billion, then 2.91 billion, then 4.03 billion). Yet the two headline indices barely moved: the DSE All Share Index slipped 0.30% to 4,441.63 and the Tanzania Share Index eased 0.28% to 9,879.68. Turnover fell from Wednesday's TZS 9.57 billion to TZS 6.72 billion — but two large pre-arranged block trades still supplied 75% of it: 2,131,323 NMB shares and 1,000,000 VODA shares. The normal trading board, where everyday investors buy and sell, traded only about TZS 1.66 billion — the thinnest session of the four-day series. For the everyday investor, Thursday's lesson is the one inside the numbers: the sellers and buyers have moved their biggest trades off the public board, the negotiated prices tell you who was in a hurry, and the market underneath keeps absorbing it all.

The two headline indices fell together again, but gently — DSEI by 0.30%, TSI by 0.28%. This time the gap between total market value (down TZS 115.24 billion) and domestic market value (down TZS 148.74 billion) runs the other way from the last two days: the domestic market fell further than the headline. The reason is domestic: CRDB slipped 0.35% on a TZS 7.42 trillion market value and VODA fell 2.38% on a TZS 2.76 trillion market value — the two heaviest Tanzanian companies both closed lower, and that did most of the day's falling. When the headline and domestic figures disagree, follow the domestic one — it tells you what Tanzanian companies actually did, and on Thursday Tanzanian companies fell while the cross-listed names cushioned the headline.

Foreign participation is now one-way without a counterweight: foreign selling of TZS 4,026.42 million against foreign buying of TZS 0.00 million — a net outflow of about TZS 4.03 billion in a single session, the heaviest of the series since Monday's TZS 11.94 billion. The pace of the exit has risen again after two days of easing. Yet the domestic index fell only 0.55%. When a market absorbs four straight days of foreign selling totalling TZS 24.26 billion and its domestic index is down less than 1% across the last two of them, it tells you domestic demand is deep — even on a day when the selling itself was the heaviest in three sessions.

3. In Focus: Two Blocks, Two Opposite Stories

Wednesday's wrap explained how to work out a block trade's implied price: take the counter's total reported value, subtract the normal board's volume multiplied by its average price, and divide what is left by the block's shares. Thursday gives us two more data points, and they tell opposite stories — which is exactly why the arithmetic matters.

The NMB block of 2,131,323 shares implies a price of roughly 1,787 shillings — about 3.4% below the day's average market price of 1,850. After Wednesday's surprise premium, the discount is back. The series now reads: 6.3% discount, 4.3% discount, 2.8% premium, 3.4% discount. The pattern is a seller conceding price to move size — consistent with the day's 59.88% foreign selling share. The VODA block of 1,000,000 shares, the first of the series, implies roughly 1,250 shillings — about 1.6% above the day's average price of 1,230. A buyer paying above the market price to build a million-share position in a retail-heavy name, on a day when foreigners sold everything, is accumulation language — the opposite of the NMB print. Same calculation, opposite conclusions: one negotiated transfer at a concession to exit, one negotiated purchase at a premium to enter. The practical lesson, four days in: the blocks are no longer a sideshow — at 75% of the day's turnover they are the market's real venue, and reading their prices is the difference between watching the market and understanding it.

4. Bond Market: TZS 18.9 Billion Day as the Long End Moves in Size

Government bond turnover rebounded from Wednesday's TZS 7.55 billion to TZS 18.80 billion in face value across 22 deals (government bonds: face value TZS 18.80 billion, transaction value TZS 24.68 billion) — and for the first time in the series, the corporate bond board printed too: ITRUST-26/30, a 4-year bond paying 13.00% interest, traded TZS 98.00 million in face value at 107.03% of par (yield 11.71%). A small print, but proof the corporate board is not entirely quiet.

The long end finally moved in real size. The 25-year bond at 15.75% interest traded TZS 5.56 billion in face value at about 156% of par (yield 10.00%) — the session's largest single print — followed by the 20-year bond at 15.25% interest with TZS 5.00 billion at about 141% of par (yield 10.57%), and the 2-year bond at 9.50% interest with TZS 5.50 billion at about 106% of par (yield 7.20%) — the short end pricing cleanly in size. The 25-year bond issued on 16 October, now in its fourth week as the market's long-term benchmark, traded TZS 0.83 billion in 8 deals at prices between 110% and 120% of face value, with yields between 10.5% and 11.6% — wider dispersion than the last two weeks, worth watching. The message for savers: long-term government bonds are printing real size at around 10–10.6% tax-free, and the 2-year bond shows the short end offers about 7.2% — the full spectrum of Tanzanian government debt is pricing in the open market.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (this week):

  • Watch whether foreign buying returns at all: four days, TZS 24.26 billion out, and Thursday's buying ledger read 0.00% — the exit no longer has a counterweight.
  • NMB held flat at 1,850 while its block crossed at a 3.4% discount — the discount pattern, not Wednesday's premium, is the counter's near-term story.
  • VODA fell 2.4% to 1,230 but its first-ever block crossed at a 1.6% premium — a premium on 1 million shares is accumulation language, whatever the close says.
  • CRDB slipped 0.35% to 2,840 on the normal board alone — with no block to hide behind, this was the day's cleanest read on a banking heavyweight.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • The new 25-year government bond is now in its fourth week — its yield, near 10.5–11%, is becoming the reference rate against which every long-term investment in Tanzania is measured.
  • Dividend season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.55% net) and TCC (7.00% net) tend to attract attention.

Long-Term:

A market that absorbs TZS 24.26 billion of foreign selling in four sessions — with the buying leg disappearing entirely on the fourth day — while its domestic index falls less than 1% across the last two of them, is a market standing on its own feet. Add a functioning long-term bond market printing TZS 18.9 billion in a session, and you have the two pillars of a maturing capital market: equity depth and a real yield curve. For the patient, long-term investor, that self-reliance — tested, not assumed, four days in a row — is the most constructive backdrop possible, even when the exit itself is the headline. ---
