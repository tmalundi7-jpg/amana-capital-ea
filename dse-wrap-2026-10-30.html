Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 30th October 2026

TZS 3.8 Billion Day — The Exit Eases, But the NMB Block Paid the Deepest Discount of the Week

Friday was the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange's fifth straight session of foreign selling — and the first in which the fever genuinely broke. Foreign investors sold TZS 1.41 billion of shares, 37% of the day's equity turnover, down from Thursday's 60%, while foreign buying flickered back to life at TZS 1.82 million — up from zero, but still essentially nothing against a TZS 3.77 billion turnover. Combined with the four previous sessions, that is about TZS 25.66 billion out in five sessions (TZS 11.94 billion, then 5.39 billion, then 2.91 billion, then 4.03 billion, then 1.41 billion). The two headline indices barely moved: the DSE All Share Index slipped 0.39% to 4,424.33 and the Tanzania Share Index eased 0.44% to 9,836.17. Turnover fell from Thursday's TZS 6.72 billion to TZS 3.77 billion — but the blocks supplied only 32% of it this time: the normal trading board, where everyday investors buy and sell, traded about TZS 2.55 billion — its busiest session of the five-day series. For the everyday investor, Friday's lesson is the one inside the block prices: one negotiated transfer paid the widest discount of the week to exit, another crossed exactly at the market price, and the public board quietly absorbed it all.

The two headline indices fell together for the fifth session, but gently — DSEI by 0.39%, TSI by 0.44%. Total market value fell TZS 150.82 billion to TZS 38,567.91 billion; the domestic market value fell TZS 118.28 billion to TZS 26,742.63 billion. This time the headline fell further than the domestic book — the cross-listed names leaned the headline down with it. When the two disagree, follow the domestic one: it tells you what Tanzanian companies actually did, and on Friday Tanzanian companies fell only about half a percent.

Foreign participation is still one-way, but the fever has broken: foreign selling of TZS 1,409.91 million against foreign buying of TZS 1.82 million — a net outflow of about TZS 1.41 billion in a single session, the mildest of the five. The pace of the exit has more than halved twice over. Yet the domestic index has now fallen five sessions running. When a market absorbs five straight days of foreign selling totalling TZS 25.66 billion and its domestic index is still within half a percent on the fifth day, it tells you domestic demand is deep — even on a day when the selling itself paid the widest discount of the week to get out.

3. In Focus: Three Blocks, Three Different Temperatures

Thursday's wrap explained how to work out a block trade's implied price: take the counter's total reported value, subtract the normal board's volume multiplied by its average price, and divide what is left by the block's shares. Friday gives us three data points, and they sit at three different temperatures — which is exactly why the arithmetic matters.

The NMB block of 348,000 shares implies a price of roughly 1,690 shillings — about 7.1% below the day's average market price of 1,820. The series now reads: 6.3% discount, 4.3% discount, 2.8% premium, 3.4% discount, 7.1% discount. Yesterday's seller conceded 3.4% to move 2.13 million shares; today's seller conceded twice that to move 348,000. A seller paying the widest concession of the week for a fraction of yesterday's size is not a patient seller — that is urgency, priced. The VODA block of 500,000 shares implies roughly 1,250 shillings — exactly at the day's average price. No concession, no premium. After Thursday's 1.6% premium on a million shares suggested accumulation, today's flat print is neutral — an agreed transfer with neither side in a hurry. The IEACLC-ETF block of 462,662 shares implies roughly 1,341 shillings — about 3.5% below the 1,390 average, and the reason the ETF board posted TZS 964 million (+419% on Thursday). Same calculation, three readings: one exit at the widest concession of the week, one neutral transfer at the market price, one discounted exit in the ETF corner. The practical lesson, five days in: the blocks are no longer the whole market — at 32% of turnover they are a shrinking share of it — but their prices still tell you who was in a hurry, and on Friday the hurry was all on one side.

4. Bond Market: TZS 31.3 Billion Day as Two Long Bonds Take 85% of the Trade

Government bond turnover jumped from Thursday's TZS 18.88 billion to TZS 31.26 billion in face value across 40 deals (face value TZS 31.26 billion, transaction value TZS 39.57 billion) — the heaviest bond session of the series. Two long-dated prints dominated everything: the 20-year bond at 15.25% interest traded TZS 14.50 billion in face value at about 135% of par — the session's largest single print — and the 20-year bond at 12.25% interest traded TZS 12.02 billion at between 116% and 118% of par. Together they were TZS 26.52 billion: 85% of the day's face. The 25-year bond at 12.75% interest printed TZS 2.57 billion at 118–120% of par across 9 deals, while the 25-year bond at 12.56% interest traded TZS 0.60 billion in 12 deals with prices ranging from 102% to 122% of par — the widest price dispersion of any bond this week, a sign the long end is still searching for its clearing level. The corporate bond board printed for the second session running: a 5-year bond paying 12.00% interest traded TZS 10.50 million at 92% and 100% of par — different bond from Thursday's 4-year print, and still small, but the board is now alive two days in a row. The message for savers: the long end of Tanzania's government debt is trading real size in the open market, and the full spectrum of it is pricing where everyone can see.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (next week):

  • Watch whether foreign selling keeps easing: five days, TZS 25.66 billion out, but Friday's TZS 1.41 billion was the mildest of the series and buying — barely — has returned.
  • NMB fell 1.6% to 1,820 while its block crossed at a 7.1% discount — the widest concession of the week says the exit still has its sharpest edge in this counter.
  • VODA rose 1.6% to 1,250 and its block printed flat at the market price — no urgency either way, which is itself a stabilising signal.
  • CRDB held flat at 2,840 on the week's heaviest normal-board volume — with no block to hide behind, this was the week's cleanest read on a banking heavyweight.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • The 25-year bonds are trading real size — TZS 2.57 billion and TZS 0.60 billion prints on Friday alone — with yields around 10.5–12%, becoming the reference rate against which every long-term investment in Tanzania is measured.
  • Dividend season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.57% net) and TCC (7.00% net) tend to attract attention.

Long-Term:

A market that absorbs TZS 25.66 billion of foreign selling in five sessions — with the selling easing to its mildest pace on the fifth day — while its domestic index falls less than half a percent on that day, is a market standing on its own feet. Add a functioning long-term bond market printing TZS 31.3 billion in a session, and you have the two pillars of a maturing capital market: equity depth and a real yield curve. For the patient, long-term investor, that self-reliance — tested, not assumed, five days in a row — is the most constructive backdrop possible, even when the exit itself is the headline. ---

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Friday's session will be remembered as the day the exit showed its cost — and the market held anyway. The numbers on the normal board were the week's busiest, the headline turnover was its most concentrated in reverse, and the gap between them was three pre-arranged trades worth a third of the market. A market where foreign selling eases to its mildest pace, where the biggest block crosses at the widest discount of the week, where one transfer prints flat and another exits at a concession, and where two 20-year bonds trade TZS 26.5 billion in the open market, is a market in the middle of a genuine deepening.
