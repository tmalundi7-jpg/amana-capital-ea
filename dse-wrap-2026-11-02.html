Daily DSE Wrap | Monday, 2nd November 2026

Foreigners Sold TZS 1.97 Billion — and the Market Went Up Anyway

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange opened the week with a quiet show of strength. Equity turnover rose 9% to TZS 4.10 billion, the All-Share Index added 8 points to 4,432.36, and the Tanzania Share Index edged up to 9,843.75. The headline number underneath it all is striking: foreign investors accounted for 48% of all selling — nearly TZS 1.97 billion worth of shares — and the market still closed higher. For everyday investors, Monday was a lesson in what domestic demand can do: when local buyers absorb heavy foreign selling without the index flinching, it tells you the market is standing on its own feet.

Look at the pattern: deals jumped 64% while share volume rose only 14%. That means many more small transactions — the footprint of broad participation rather than a few large institutional tickets. The one large ticket was the 1,000,000-share block trade in Vodacom Tanzania on the pre-arranged board, which accounts for nearly half the day's reported share volume. A block trade is simply a large, pre-arranged transaction between two parties; it adds to turnover without necessarily moving the price — Vodacom still closed up 0.80% at 1,260.

TBL and TCC both slipped about 1.7%, which dragged the Industrial & Allied index down 1.17% even as the banks index rose 0.42%.

3. In Focus: The Daily Price Bands

Every share on the DSE trades inside a daily price band — a ceiling and a floor set from the previous session's closing price. The bands protect all investors from extreme single-day swings, and they come in three tiers: most counters move within ±15%, large companies within ±5%, and the very largest companies with fewer shares in issue (such as TBL and TCC) within a tighter ±2%.

Because CRDB closed at 2,870 on Monday, it cannot trade on Tuesday, 3 November, outside this band:

  • Upper limit: 2,870 × 1.05 = 3,013.50
  • Lower limit: 2,870 × 0.95 = 2,688.50

4. Bond Market: One Bond Does the Heavy Lifting

Government bond turnover reached TZS 45.46 billion across 59 deals — and a single instrument did almost all the work. The 25-year 12.75% bond saw TZS 43.75 billion in face value change hands in 19 deals at 116–123% of par — 96% of the day's total. When investors pay 116 to 123 shillings for every 100 shillings of face value on a 25-year bond, they are making a deliberate choice: accept a lower running yield in exchange for locking in tax-free income for a quarter of a century. That is a strong vote of confidence in long-term rates.

The corporate bond board printed for the third session running — a 5-year 12.00% bond traded TZS 122.5 million in face value at 94% and 100% of par in 4 deals. Small, but a corporate board that trades three days in a row is a corporate board coming to life.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • Foreign selling hit 48% of turnover — the heaviest share of the recent run. Watch whether this continues: sustained foreign distribution tests how deep domestic demand really is.
  • CRDB at 2,870 is at the top of its recent range on the heaviest normal-board volume of the day. A push above 2,900 with volume would extend the move; a slip back toward 2,800 would suggest the buyers are pausing.
  • NMB held 1,820 dead flat on 1,670 deals — the market's most-traded counter by ticket count. That kind of equilibrium rarely lasts; the break, when it comes, usually has direction.
  • DCB at 385 is the session's weakest large mover. Oversold bounces are common after a −3.75% day, but a weak counter can always get weaker first.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.7% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention.
  • The bond market's appetite for 25-year paper at 116–123% of par suggests institutions expect rates to stay contained — a supportive backdrop for equities.

Long-Term:

A market that absorbs TZS 1.97 billion of foreign selling and still closes higher is a market whose price is set by its own investors, not by visitors. That self-reliance — visible in both the equity tape and the TZS 45 billion bond session — is the foundation on which long-term compounding is built. ---

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

When a market can take nearly two billion shillings of foreign selling in its stride and still close the day higher, it has acquired something more valuable than a rally: independence. The DSE's deepening domestic participation — visible in the 64% jump in deal count and the record absorption of foreign supply — means prices increasingly reflect the convictions of local capital rather than the flows of distant funds.
