Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 3rd November 2026

Foreigners Sold TZS 2.04 Billion — and This Time the Market Couldn't Absorb It

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange had a red day. Equity turnover eased 4.7% to TZS 3.91 billion, the All-Share Index fell 46.66 points to 4,385.70 (−1.05%), and the Tanzania Share Index dropped 1.27% to 9,718.68. The story underneath is a reversal of Monday's: foreign investors sold TZS 2.04 billion of shares — 52.29% of all turnover, the heaviest selling share of the recent run — and this time domestic buyers could not take it all without conceding ground. Every sector index finished lower: banks −1.50%, commercial services −1.44%, industrials −0.59%. For everyday investors, Tuesday was the boundary condition on Monday's lesson: domestic demand is deep, but a foreign outflow of this size on a quiet retail day still moves the index.

Look at the pattern: deal count collapsed 35% while share volume fell only 1.8%. Monday's flood of small retail tickets vanished, and the tape was carried by size — two block trades on the pre-arranged board: a 200,000-share block in NMB and a 1,000,000-share block in Vodacom Tanzania (together ≈TZS 1.59 billion of implied value, over 40% of headline turnover). A block trade is simply a large, pre-arranged transaction between two parties; it adds to turnover without necessarily moving the price — Vodacom still closed down only 1.59% at 1,240 despite the million-share print.

3. In Focus: The Daily Price Bands

Every share on the DSE trades inside a daily price band — a ceiling and a floor set from the previous session's closing price. The bands protect all investors from extreme single-day swings, and they come in three tiers: most counters move within ±15%, large companies within ±5%, and the very largest companies with fewer shares in issue (such as TBL and TCC) within a tighter ±2%.

Because CRDB closed at 2,810 on Tuesday, it cannot trade on Wednesday, 4 November, outside this band:

  • Upper limit: 2,810 × 1.05 = 2,950.50
  • Lower limit: 2,810 × 0.95 = 2,669.50

4. Bond Market: The Corporate Board Arrives

Bond turnover rose to a narrative TZS 72.08 billion across 57 deals — and the government board kept its long-end habit: the 25-year 12.75% bond saw TZS 48.73 billion in face value change hands in 28 deals at 100.0001–125.7055% of par, and the 20-year 12.25% bond traded TZS 17.25 billion in face value in 8 deals at 117.2490–120.9620% of par. When investors pay up to 125 shillings for every 100 shillings of face value on a 25-year bond, they are making a deliberate choice: accept a lower running yield in exchange for locking in tax-free income for a quarter of a century. That is a strong vote of confidence in long-term rates.

But Tuesday's real development is on the corporate board, which printed its biggest day of the series: a 5-year 13.00% bond traded TZS 4,737.68 million in face value at 103.3600% and 108.0380% of par in 7 deals, alongside a 5-year 14.00% bond — TZS 7.50 million at 100.0000% (1 deal). (Note: the report prints the first bond's face value as "4737.68 bln" — a typo; the report's own table shows 4,745.18 million TZS, and transaction value of 4,913.03 million TZS.) Corporate transaction value is roughly forty times Monday's. When investors pay 103 to 108 shillings for every 100 shillings of face on 5-year corporate paper, the domestic credit market is saying it believes in the issuers. A corporate board that trades TZS 4.9 billion in a day is no longer a corporate board coming to life — it is alive.

Data note: the report's bond-value tables (government transaction value 82.93bn + corporate 4.91bn) do not reconcile to the narrative's TZS 72.08 billion as printed. The headline uses the official narrative figure; the inconsistency is flagged, not resolved.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • Foreign selling has now hit two straight sessions above 48% of turnover — and today's 52.29% finally moved the index. Watch whether a third session follows: three heavy foreign-selling days in a row would test how deep domestic demand really is.
  • CRDB at 2,810 is off 2.09% on the heaviest volume of the day. The sellers are visible; whether buyers step in toward the 2,669.50 floor will set the tone for the banks.
  • NMB held 1,800 with a 200,000-share block crossing the pre-arranged board. Blocks that size are usually institutional-to-institutional — the counter's direction from here depends on whether the seller has more to distribute.
  • The corporate bond board just printed its biggest day ever. If corporate prints continue at this scale, the DSE is building a genuine secondary market in corporate credit — worth watching alongside the equity tape.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.8% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention.
  • The bond market's appetite for 25-year paper at up to 125% of par suggests institutions expect rates to stay contained — a supportive backdrop for equities.

Long-Term:

A market that absorbs TZS 1.97 billion of foreign selling one day and then concedes 47 points on TZS 2.04 billion the next has not lost its footing — it has shown its honest price for the flow. That self-reliance — visible in both the equity tape and the TZS 72 billion bond session — is the foundation on which long-term compounding is built. ---

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If block trades or price bands are new concepts, read Stocks, Bonds, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and Understanding Market Data.
  • Maintain a diversified portfolio. Spread capital across banks, industrials, consumer goods, and bonds rather than concentrating in a single counter.
  • Think in years, not days. Two million-share blocks crossing the tape are noise for the long-term investor; the steady compounding of well-managed companies is the signal.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

A market's depth is measured twice: once when it absorbs heavy selling and rises, and again when it concedes ground honestly to the same flow. The DSE has now done both in two sessions. What matters for the multi-year investor is not which day the index went up — it is that prices are increasingly set by the convictions of local capital, in equities and, now, in corporate credit, rather than by the flows of distant funds.
