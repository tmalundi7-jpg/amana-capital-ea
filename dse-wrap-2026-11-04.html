Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 4th November 2026

Foreigners Sold TZS 2.04 Billion Again — and This Time the Market Barely Moved

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange did something instructive on Wednesday. Foreign investors sold TZS 2.04 billion of shares for the third session running — 47.52% of all turnover, the same outflow that knocked 47 points off the All-Share Index on Tuesday. This time the DSEI fell just 4.01 points to 4,381.69 (−0.09%), and the banks index actually finished higher. Same supply, different answer. Equity turnover rose 10.0% to TZS 4.30 billion, and once again most of it crossed in blocks: three pre-arranged block trades — 170,000 shares in CRDB, 428,571 in NMB and 1,000,000 in Vodacom Tanzania — worth roughly TZS 2.49 billion, nearly 58% of the day's turnover. For everyday investors, Wednesday was the lesson Monday and Tuesday were building toward: heavy foreign selling no longer guarantees a falling index, because domestic demand is meeting it at the bid.

Look at the pattern: share volume rose 11.3% while the number of deals fell 2.1%. Fewer trades, bigger trades — the hallmark of institutional crossing. Three block trades on the pre-arranged board did the heavy lifting: CRDB 170,000 shares, NMB 428,571 shares and Vodacom Tanzania 1,000,000 shares. A block trade is simply a large, pre-arranged transaction between two parties; it adds to turnover without necessarily moving the price — NMB still closed unchanged at 1,800 despite its 428,571-share print, and CRDB was flat at 2,810.

3. In Focus: Reading the Tape — Normal Board, Blocks and VWAP

Wednesday's tape is a good day to learn three terms that appear in every DSE report: the normal board, block trades, and VWAP.

Most trading happens on the normal board — the continuous market where buy and sell orders meet through the day. A block trade, by contrast, is a large transaction arranged in advance between two parties and reported through the pre-arranged market; it appears in the day's totals but does not go through the normal order book. On Wednesday, blocks accounted for nearly 58% of equity turnover — which is why headline turnover (TZS 4.30 billion) looks large while most counters' prices barely moved.

The VWAP (volume-weighted average price) is the average price at which a counter actually traded during the session, weighted by how many shares changed hands at each price. Today's report publishes VWAP alongside volume for every traded counter — it is the fairest single number for "what did this counter trade at today," better than any one print. CRDB's VWAP of 2,810 on 249,996 shares, for instance, tells you the day's heavy flow settled at exactly the closing price: buyers and sellers agreed.

One more concept worth knowing: every share on the DSE trades inside a daily price band — a ceiling and a floor set from the previous session's closing price. The bands come in three tiers: most counters move within ±15%, large companies within ±5%, and the very largest companies with fewer shares in issue (such as TBL and TCC) within a tighter ±2%.

4. Bond Market: The Long End Still Rules

Bond turnover cooled to a narrative TZS 23.95 billion across 41 deals — down sharply from Tuesday's TZS 72.08 billion, but the character did not change: institutions still want long-dated paper, and they still pay over par for it. The 20-year 15.25% bond saw TZS 15.05 billion in face value change hands in 4 deals at 125.0000–141.4073% of par — meaning some investors paid 141 shillings for every 100 shillings of face value to lock in a 15.25% coupon for twenty years. The 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 2.61 billion in face value in 20 deals at 115.5000–120.9988% of par. Also printed: a 5-year 10.25% — TZS 4.50 billion at 103.5606% (1 deal).

The corporate board stepped back to two small lines after Tuesday's TZS 4.9 billion spike: a 4-year 13.00% bond — TZS 65.50 million at 100.0000% of par (2 deals) and a 5-year 12.00% bond — TZS 202.00 million at 99.3800% and 100.0000% (2 deals). Whether Tuesday was the arrival of a genuine secondary market in corporate credit or a one-day anomaly is now the question to watch.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • Foreign selling has now run three straight sessions above 47% of turnover — and the market's answer has gone from −47 points (Tuesday) to −4 points (Wednesday). Watch whether a fourth heavy foreign-selling day arrives and how the index responds: the absorption story is now the trend to test.
  • MBP's +4.08% move on 3,267 shares is a thin-volume signal — worth noting, not worth chasing. Genuine small-counter breakouts announce themselves with rising volume.
  • The corporate bond board's return to TZS 266 million after Tuesday's TZS 4.9 billion spike is the open question of the week. Two similar spikes would confirm a trend; silence would file Tuesday as an anomaly.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.9% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention.
  • The bond market's continued appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par suggests institutions expect rates to stay contained — a supportive backdrop for equities.

Long-Term:

Three sessions, the same TZS 2 billion of foreign selling, and an index down just 51 points across all three. A market that used to flinch at foreign outflows now absorbs them — in equities and in long-dated bonds. That deepening domestic bid is the single most constructive development in this market's recent history, and it is the foundation on which long-term compounding is built. ---
