Daily DSE Wrap | Thursday, 5th November 2026

Thirteen Counters Fell — and the Market's Centre Barely Moved

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange had its widest red day in the recent run on Thursday: thirteen of the thirty-one listed counters closed lower, and only two rose. But look past the breadth and the picture changes. The falls were concentrated in the small banks — Maendeleo Bank gave back all of Wednesday's gain and more, down 4.90% to 1,940 — while the heavyweights barely stirred: CRDB Bank rose 0.36% to 2,820, NMB Bank eased 0.56% to 1,790, Vodacom Tanzania rose 0.81% to 1,250, and Tanzania Cigarette Company held flat at 14,000. Breadth says sellers were active; the size of the moves in the largest companies says they were not panicked. One honest caveat belongs at the top of this wrap: the DSE's website would not load at all for this session, so the full daily report — turnover, shares traded, deals, the index values, bond trading and foreign flows — could not be read. This wrap is built only on the official closing prices, which the exchange did publish. Nothing below is estimated. Where a figure is missing, it is marked missing.

Thirteen fallers against two risers looks like a rout; the actual closes say otherwise. The market's largest companies — the ones that carry the index — finished within a fraction of a percent of where they started. The damage was in the small banks, where a handful of trades can move a price several percent. That distinction matters more than the headline count.

MBP (Maendeleo Bank) was the day's biggest loser, down 4.90% to 1,940 — exactly one session after being the top gainer at +4.08%. A round trip in two days is the signature of a small counter moving on thin volume: the price can travel a long way on very little trading, and it can come straight back. TOL fell 4.73% to 1,410 and NICO 3.25% to 3,270, both small-bank names in the same pattern. At the other end, Vodacom Tanzania (+0.81% to 1,250) was the session's top gainer and CRDB Bank (+0.36% to 2,820) the only other riser — modest moves, but they are the two counters that matter most for the market's direction, and both went up on a red day. NMB Bank (−0.56% to 1,790) was the only heavyweight to soften. Sixteen counters did not move at all, including Tanzania Cigarette Company at 14,000 and Tanga Cement at 4,340.

3. In Focus: The Daily Price Band — Why No Share Can Move More Than Its Limit

Every share on the DSE trades inside a daily price band — a ceiling and a floor set from the previous session's closing price. The bands come in three tiers under the exchange's Rule 212: most counters move within ±15%, large companies within ±5%, and the very largest companies with fewer shares in issue (such as Tanzania Breweries and Tanzania Cigarette Company) within a tighter ±2%. The band is why a stock can never gap without limit on a single day, and why a limit order placed outside the band simply cannot fill.

Thursday's closes set Friday's bands. The table below shows the allowed trading range for widely held stocks on Friday 6 November:

The band is also the reason Thursday's small-bank falls look dramatic but are ordinary: a 15% band gives a small counter room to fall 4.90% in a session, while a 2% band would have stopped TBL's move at a fraction of that. The band does not cause the move — it only sets how far the move can go in one day.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The open question from Wednesday — whether foreign selling would run a fourth straight session above 47% of turnover — cannot be answered from Thursday's data, because the foreign-flow figures were not published. When the full report returns, that is the first number to check.
  • MBP's round trip (+4.08% Wednesday, −4.90% Thursday) is the week's clearest lesson in small-counter volatility: moves without visible volume reverse fast. Genuine small-counter breakouts announce themselves with rising volume — and volume is exactly what Thursday's data lacks.
  • Watch whether Friday's session restores the full report. Two thin-data days in a row would leave the market's direction genuinely unmeasured.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.9% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention. Both were flat to a fraction down on Thursday.
  • The bond market's continued appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par (Wednesday's evidence) suggests institutions expect rates to stay contained — a supportive backdrop for equities, if Thursday's flow confirms it.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb a thirteen-counter red day without moving is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling earlier this week did not disappear on Thursday — the heavyweights simply did not need it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If price bands, block trades or VWAP are new concepts, read Stocks, Bonds, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and Understanding Market Data.
