Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 6th November 2026

Turnover More Than Doubled — and Almost All of It Was Two Blocks

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 11.61 billion of equity turnover on Friday, up 170% on Wednesday — the biggest turnover day of the recent run. But read the fine print and the story changes completely. Of the 5,593,000 shares traded, 4,731,323 — nearly 85% — crossed on the pre-arranged block board: a 3,731,323-share block in NMB Bank and a 1,000,000-share block in Vodacom Tanzania. The normal board, where everyday trading happens, turned over just TZS 2.34 billion. The market's headline doubled; the market itself barely moved, with the DSE All Share Index down 0.15% and the Tanzania Share Index down 0.10%. One honest caveat belongs at the top of this wrap: Thursday's figures were not published — the full report could not be read that day — so the comparisons below run against Wednesday 4 November, the last session with published figures. Three small inconsistencies inside Friday's own report are also carried exactly as printed and flagged where they appear: a corporate-bond face value that does not match its own table, government-bond face values that do not add to their table total, and a headline bond turnover that does not match the bond tables. Nothing below is estimated.

The most important number in the table is not the turnover — it is the foreign-selling share. For three straight sessions foreign investors had sold more than 47% of turnover; on Friday they sold 11.44% (TZS 1,327.96 million) and bought 2.15% (TZS 249.78 million). The net outflow narrowed to TZS 1,078.18 million from TZS 2,036.63 million on Wednesday. Whatever was driving the foreign selling this week, it paused on Friday — and the market's centre barely noticed either way, with the two indices each within a sixth of a percent of Wednesday.

3. In Focus: The Block Trade — Why Two Deals Can Double Turnover Without Moving the Market

A block trade is a large parcel of shares that changes hands by private arrangement and is then reported through the exchange's pre-arranged board, rather than being bought and sold in the open order book. On Friday, two of them — 3,731,323 NMB Bank shares and 1,000,000 Vodacom Tanzania shares — accounted for nearly 85% of all shares traded and roughly 80% of the turnover value. Because the price is agreed between the two parties in advance, a block adds turnover without pushing the normal-board price around: NMB Bank closed flat at 1,790 and Vodacom Tanzania rose just 10 shillings, despite the enormous volumes.

That is why the indices barely moved on a record-turnover day. The DSE All Share Index and the Tanzania Share Index are built from normal-board closing prices, so a day dominated by blocks leaves them almost untouched: down 0.15% and 0.10% respectively against Wednesday. The lesson is a practical one for anyone reading market headlines — turnover answers "how much changed hands", not "how much did prices move", and when the two disagree, the blocks are usually the reason.

The band does not cause the move — it only sets how far the move can go in one day.

4. Bond Market: Long-Dated Paper Still Dominates

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 17.27 billion across 40 deals on Friday, down from TZS 23.95 billion on Wednesday — with the caveat in section 1 that the report's own tables show a higher transaction value, so the headline is quoted as printed. On the government board, 35 deals: the 25-year 12.75% bond again dominated, with TZS 10.36 billion in face value across 19 deals at 117.88–122.00% of par, followed by the 20-year 12.25% bond (TZS 1.30 billion face, 5 deals at 117.97–120.87%). The 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 0.35 billion face across a wide 100.00–140.00% range. The corporate board printed 5 deals with TZS 465.00 million in face value, led by the 5-year 12.00% bond (TZS 350.00 million face at 99.52–101.48%). One figure in the report does not add up: the corporate-bond narrative states a face value of "TZS 4590.00 billion" for the 5-year 10.25% bond, against a table total of TZS 465.00 million — evidently misprinted, and carried here as printed. The appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par continues: institutions are still paying up for duration.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The first question for Monday is whether foreign selling stays low. Three sessions above 47% of turnover, then 11.44% on Friday — if the lower level holds, the week's selling pressure has genuinely eased; if it snaps back above 40%, Friday was a pause, not a turn.
  • NICO's 13.15% jump is the week's only large move on real volume. Genuine breakouts announce themselves with follow-through — watch whether Monday holds above 3,700 or gives the move back the way MBP gave back its gain.
  • Watch whether the NMB and VODA blocks were the start of institutional repositioning or a one-off. A second week of block activity in the same two counters would say something is being built; a quiet Monday would say it was a single rebalancing.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (8.0% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention. Both moved less than 2% on Friday.
  • The bond market's continued appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par suggests institutions expect rates to stay contained — a supportive backdrop for equities, if Monday's flow confirms it.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb a TZS 11.61 billion turnover day — most of it in two blocks — without moving is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling earlier this week did not disappear on Friday — the heavyweights simply did not need it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If price bands, block trades or VWAP are new concepts, read Stocks, Bonds, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and Understanding Market Data.
  • Maintain a diversified portfolio. Spread capital across banks, industrials, consumer goods, and bonds rather than concentrating in a single counter.
  • Think in years, not days. A 13.15% jump in one counter on one day is a signal worth watching, not a reason to chase; the steady compounding of well-managed companies is the signal.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.
