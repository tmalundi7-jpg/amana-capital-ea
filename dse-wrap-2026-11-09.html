Daily DSE Wrap | Monday, 9th November 2026

A Second Block-Driven Day — and NICO Gave Back Three-Fifths of Friday's Jump

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 9.04 billion of equity turnover on Monday, down 22% on Friday — but the shape of the day was identical. Of the 4,036,735 shares traded, 3,000,000 — nearly three-quarters — crossed on the pre-arranged block board: a 2,000,000-share block in CRDB Bank and a 1,000,000-share block in Vodacom Tanzania. The normal board, where everyday trading happens, turned over just TZS 2.07 billion. The market's headline stayed large; the market itself barely moved, with the DSE All Share Index up 0.09% and the Tanzania Share Index up 0.08%. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's own website price card was out of date on Monday — it repeated Friday's closing prices — so every price below comes from the report itself, not the card. Second, three small inconsistencies inside Monday's own report are carried exactly as printed and flagged where they appear: the per-counter turnover column adds to TZS 10.22 million more than the report's own printed total, the headline bond turnover matches the bond tables' face value rather than their transaction value, and one counter's average price does not match its own turnover figure. Nothing below is estimated.

The most important number in the table is not the turnover — it is the foreign-selling share. Friday's pause did not hold: foreign investors sold 16.80% of turnover on Monday (TZS 1,518.15 million) and bought just 0.29% (TZS 25.94 million). The net outflow widened to TZS 1,492.21 million from TZS 1,078.18 million on Friday. The selling is nowhere near the 47%-plus levels of earlier last week, but the direction is the wrong way after a single quiet session — and the market's centre barely noticed either way, with the two indices each within a tenth of a percent of Friday.

NICO was the session's warning, not its star: down 7.03% to 3,440 on 30,758 shares, giving back 260 of the 430 shillings it gained on Friday — three-fifths of the jump, gone in one session. MCB was the day's biggest loser in its own right, down 7.14% to 325 on 53,511 shares, the heaviest volume it has printed in the recent run. Vodacom Tanzania (+1.59% to 1,280) and CRDB Bank (−0.71% to 2,800) were the volume story instead: CRDB traded 387,244 shares in 1,430 deals on the normal board, the heaviest volume and the most deals of the day, while NMB Bank traded 339,418 shares in 1,071 deals and closed flat at 1,790. Swissport Tanzania (+3.21% to 2,570) was the best genuine gain on a traded counter, though on just 1,459 shares. Tanzania Breweries (+1.13% to 9,810) and Tanzania Cigarette Company (+1.21% to 14,180) both edged up, the only large-cap moves of note, and both were small.

3. In Focus: The Give-Back — Why a 13% Jump Fades the Next Day

A large one-day jump feels like a breakout. Monday showed why it usually is not. NICO rose 13.15% on Friday, the largest single-counter move of the week, on real volume — and on Monday it fell 7.03%, giving back three-fifths of the gain. Nothing in the report explains either move. That is the point: a jump with no follow-through is a price finding a level, not a trend starting.

Genuine breakouts announce themselves the next session — the price holds, or builds. A give-back like Monday's says the buyers who pushed Friday's price were not still there on Monday, and the sellers who had been waiting took the other side. The same test applies to every sharp move on this market, in both directions: MCB fell 7.14% on Monday, the day's biggest loss. If Tuesday holds near 325, the fall was a repricing; if it bounces most of the way back, it was noise. One session is a headline. Two sessions are the beginning of evidence.

The band does not cause the move — it only sets how far the move can go in one day.

4. Bond Market: The Biggest Bond Day of the Run

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 49.49 billion across 27 deals on Monday, up from TZS 17.27 billion on Friday — nearly triple — with the caveat in section 1 that the headline matches the report's face-value total rather than its transaction value of TZS 59.98 billion, so the headline is quoted as printed. On the government board, 26 deals: the 20-year 12.25% bond dominated, with TZS 31.80 billion in face value across 7 deals at 118.32–120.91% of par, followed by the 25-year 12.75% bond (TZS 11.16 billion face, 8 deals at 117.88–122.00%). The 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 6.26 billion face across a wide 100.00–138.18% range. The corporate board printed a single deal: the TCB-STAWI-25 5-year 13.50% bond, TZS 15.00 million face at 100.00% of par. The appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par continues: institutions are still paying up for duration.

The ETF board had its busiest day in a while, too: TZS 267.35 million from 1,712,680 shares in 392 deals, up from TZS 50.12 million on Friday. Almost all of it was one counter — ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 1,638,129 shares at 120, about 73% of all ETF turnover — with IEACLC-ETF adding 46,519 shares at 1,360 and VERTEX-ETF 28,032 shares at 305.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The first question for Tuesday is whether foreign selling keeps climbing. One quiet session at 11.44%, then 16.80% on Monday — if it pushes back above 40%, Friday was a pause, not a turn; if it settles back under 12%, Monday was the noise.
  • NICO's give-back is the week's open question. A 13.15% jump followed by a 7.03% fall says the move had no base under it — watch whether Tuesday holds above 3,440 or the slide continues toward 3,200.
  • MCB's 7.14% fall on the heaviest volume it has printed recently deserves the same two-session test: a hold near 325 says repricing, a sharp bounce says noise.
  • Watch whether the CRDB and VODA blocks were the start of institutional repositioning or a one-off. Two straight sessions of blocks in the same two counters would say something is being built; a quiet Tuesday would say it was rebalancing.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.9% net) and TCC (7.0% net) tend to attract attention. Both moved up a little over 1% on Monday.
  • The bond market's continued appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par suggests institutions expect rates to stay contained — a supportive backdrop for equities, if Tuesday's flow confirms it.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb a TZS 9.04 billion turnover day — most of it in two blocks — without moving is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling last week did not disappear on Monday — the heavyweights simply did not need it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If price bands, block trades or VWAP are new concepts, read Stocks, Bonds, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and Understanding Market Data.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Turnover stayed large and the market did not move — because most of it was two blocks, again. And the week's biggest jump gave most of itself back the next day. Headlines measure how much changed hands; prices measure what it was worth, and follow-through measures whether it meant anything.
