Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 10th November 2026

The Blocks Left — and the Market Fell With Them

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 2.35 billion of equity turnover on Tuesday, down 74% on Monday — but the fall is mostly an illusion of the headline. Monday's TZS 9.04 billion was mostly two blocks; Tuesday had only one, a 330,000-share block in KCB Group. The normal board, where everyday trading happens, turned over TZS 1.75 billion on 845,144 shares — down just 15.5% on Monday. What did change was the direction: 18 counters fell against 3 that rose, the DSE All Share Index fell 0.67% and the Tanzania Share Index fell 0.73%, and the two sharpest falls came where almost nobody was trading at all. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's own website price card was out of date for a second straight session — it repeated Friday's closing prices — so every price below comes from the report itself, not the card. Second, several small inconsistencies inside Tuesday's own report are carried exactly as printed and flagged where they appear: the per-counter turnover column adds to TZS 39,590 more than the report's own printed total, the headline bond turnover matches the bond tables' face value rather than their transaction value, the narrative average prices are rounded so they do not exactly reproduce the table turnovers, and one counter's opening price does not match Monday's close. Nothing below is estimated.

What it did was fall: the heavyweights all slipped a little — CRDB Bank down 0.36% to 2,790 on 303,121 shares, NMB Bank down 1.12% to 1,770 on 354,350 shares (the most deals of any counter, 1,194), Vodacom Tanzania flat at 1,280 — and the indices followed, with every sector index down. Foreign selling eased to 12.00% of turnover (TZS 281.69 million) from 16.80%, buying stayed negligible at 0.31% (TZS 7.19 million), and the net outflow narrowed sharply to TZS 274.50 million from TZS 1,492.21 million on Monday.

3. In Focus: When a Counter Falls 15% on Two Deals

A 15% fall sounds like a panic. Tuesday showed why it usually is not — at least, not a market-wide one. TTP fell 14.86% and JATU fell 12.50%, the two biggest moves of the day by a wide margin, and between them they traded 11,632 shares in 21 deals. TTP's entire session happened at one price: 315, down from an open of 370. Nobody was rushing for the exit; there was barely anybody there at all. A small counter with an empty order book can fall to its daily floor on a single seller, because there is nobody on the other side to meet them halfway.

That is the difference between a fall and a rout. A rout has volume — many sellers, many buyers, prices grinding down through the day. Tuesday's two big falls had almost no volume, and the market's centre barely moved: the heavyweights slipped about 1%, the indices fell less than 1%, and deals held up. The test, as ever, is the next session: if Wednesday holds TTP near 315, the fall was a repricing — the market found a new level for a counter nobody was watching. If it bounces most of the way back, it was one seller meeting an empty book, and the price was never really 315 at all. One session is a headline. Two sessions are the beginning of evidence.

The band does not cause the move — it only sets how far the move can go in one day.

4. Bond Market: A Second Heavy Day, Led by the Same Bond

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 46.03 billion across 27 deals on Tuesday, down 7.0% on Monday's TZS 49.49 billion — a second heavy day in a row — with the caveat in section 1 that the headline matches the report's face-value total rather than its transaction value of TZS 56.21 billion, so the headline is quoted as printed. On the government board, 24 deals: the 20-year 12.25% bond dominated again, with TZS 31.30 billion in face value across 2 deals at 120.87–122.32% of par, followed by the 25-year 12.75% bond (TZS 11.47 billion face, 9 deals at 117.88–124.81%). The 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 1.02 billion face across a wide 100.00–140.39% range. A new 2-year 9.50% bond appeared — TZS 1.50 billion face at 105.35% of par, yielding 7.80%, the lowest yield printed on the board. The corporate board printed three deals: the CRDB-2023/2028 5-year 10.25% bond, TZS 90.00 million face at 95.00% of par (yield 15.99%), and the ABK-2025/30 TZ 5-year 12.00% bond, TZS 1.50 million face at 100.00% in 2 deals. The appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par continues: institutions are still paying up for duration.

The ETF board quietened after Monday's burst: TZS 169.97 million from 976,399 shares in 241 deals, down from TZS 267.35 million on Monday. Almost all of it was one counter again — ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 895,504 shares at 120, about 92% of all ETF shares — with VERTEX-ETF adding 44,215 shares at 300 and IEACLC-ETF 36,680 shares at 1,360.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The first question for Wednesday is whether foreign selling keeps easing. Two sessions of improvement — 16.80% on Monday, 12.00% on Tuesday — after the heavy selling earlier last week; if it settles back under 12%, the outflow is fading; if it pushes back above 40%, Tuesday was a pause, not a turn.
  • TTP's 14.86% fall on a single price is the week's open question. A hold near 315 on Wednesday says repricing; a sharp bounce says one seller met an empty book.
  • JATU's 12.50% fall on 2 deals deserves the same two-session test: a hold near 175 says the market found a level, a bounce says noise.
  • NICO's slide continues: down 1.45% to 3,390 after Monday's 7.03% give-back — the Friday jump is now more than two-thirds gone. Watch whether Wednesday holds above 3,390 or the slide continues toward 3,200.
  • Watch whether the KCB block was the start of institutional repositioning in a new counter or a one-off. Two straight sessions of blocks in the same counter would say something is being built; a quiet Wednesday would say it was rebalancing.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (8.0% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention. Both slipped a little over 1% on Tuesday.
  • The bond market's continued appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par suggests institutions expect rates to stay contained — a supportive backdrop for equities, if Wednesday's flow confirms it.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb a TZS 2.35 billion turnover day — most of it in one block — and fall less than 1% is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling last week did not disappear on Tuesday — the heavyweights simply did not need it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If price bands, block trades or VWAP are new concepts, read Stocks, Bonds, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and Understanding Market Data.
  • Maintain a diversified portfolio. Spread capital across banks, industrials, consumer goods, and bonds rather than concentrating in a single counter.
  • Think in years, not days. A 14.86% fall in one counter on one day is a signal worth watching, not a reason to panic; the steady compounding of well-managed companies is the signal.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Turnover fell 74% and the market fell less than 1% — because most of Monday's turnover was two blocks, and Tuesday had only one. The day's biggest moves happened where almost nobody was trading. Headlines measure how much changed hands; prices measure what it was worth, and volume measures whether it meant anything.
