Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 11th November 2026

A TZS 9.1 Billion Block — and the Market Barely Moved

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 11.60 billion of equity turnover on Wednesday, up 394% on Tuesday — but almost none of that growth came from everyday trading. Two pre-arranged blocks accounted for TZS 9.40 billion of it: 7,000,000 Vodacom Tanzania shares and 200,000 NMB Bank shares. The normal board, where everyday trading happens, turned over about TZS 2.20 billion on 1,055,852 shares — up just 25.5% on Tuesday. What did change was who was selling: foreigners sold 78.43% of all equity turnover, the highest share on record in this run, and the market absorbed it with the DSE All Share Index down just 0.09% and the Tanzania Share Index down 0.49%. Somebody wanted those shares at that price. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's own website price card showed ZAN TIMIZA ETF at 110, but the report itself shows a close of 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. Second, several small inconsistencies inside Wednesday's own report are carried exactly as printed and flagged where they appear: the per-counter turnover column adds to TZS 800 less than the report's own printed total, the headline bond turnover matches the bond tables' face value rather than their transaction value, the narrative average prices are rounded so they do not exactly reproduce the table turnovers, and one counter's printed turnover does not match its own average price. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: When One Trade Is 87% of the Market

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range, as the NMB block appears to have done at about 1,512 against a day's range of 1,730–1,760. What a block does is move the headline: Wednesday's two blocks were 87.21% of all shares traded and about 81.04% of all turnover value, and they carried three deals between them. Strip them out and the market looks ordinary — TZS 2.20 billion of normal-board turnover, up 25.5% on Tuesday, in about 2,486 deals.

The Vodacom block is the one to understand, because it explains the foreign-selling figure. Foreigners sold TZS 9,100.00 million of shares on Wednesday — 78.43% of all equity turnover — and the Vodacom block alone is worth about TZS 9,100.55 million at its implied price of about 1,300. The two numbers match to the shilling if the block priced at exactly 1,300. That is almost certainly one foreign holder selling 7,000,000 shares to a buyer who wanted them at 1,300 — above Wednesday's traded range of 1,260–1,290, and above the close of 1,270. The buyer paid a premium to the day's trading, not a discount. That is not a market losing confidence; it is a large holder changing hands at an agreed price, and the market barely noticed — the indices fell less than half a percent.

The test, as ever, is the next session: if Thursday's foreign selling falls back toward single digits, Wednesday was one holder's exit, not a foreign retreat. If it stays high, something broader is being unwound. And the two-session test from Tuesday has its answer: TTP held at 315 and JATU held at 175 on Wednesday, both unchanged. The falls were repricing — the market found new levels for two counters almost nobody was watching — not one seller meeting an empty book. One session is a headline. Two sessions are the beginning of evidence, and the evidence says the levels held.

4. Bond Market: Quiet After Two Heavy Days

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 2.45 billion across 14 deals on Wednesday, down 94.7% on Tuesday's TZS 46.03 billion — the board went quiet after two heavy days — with the caveat in section 1 that the headline matches the report's face-value total rather than its transaction value of TZS 3.19 billion, so the headline is quoted as printed. On the government board, 11 deals: the 25-year 15.95% bond traded TZS 1.55 billion in face value across 2 deals at 125.00–139.20% of par, followed by the 25-year 12.75% bond (TZS 0.37 billion face, 8 deals at 100.00–120.13%). The 20-year 12.25% bond — the last two days' leader — traded just TZS 0.50 billion face in 1 deal at 121.00% of par, yielding 10.24%. The corporate board printed three deals: the SAMIA-25/30.T1 5-year 12.00% bond, TZS 26.50 million face at 100.00% of par in 2 deals, and the EFTA-2026/2031 5-year 14.00% bond, TZS 5.00 million face at 100.00% in 1 deal (yield 14.36%). The appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par continues, but on a fraction of the recent volume: institutions are still paying up for duration, just not in size this session.

The ETF board ticked up after Tuesday's quiet session: TZS 188.32 million from 1,112,302 shares in 200 deals, up from TZS 169.97 million on Tuesday. Almost all of it was one counter again — ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 1,044,524 shares at 120, about 94% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 40,599 shares at 1,360 and VERTEX-ETF 27,179 shares at 300.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The first question for Thursday is whether foreign selling falls back toward single digits. Wednesday's 78.43% is almost entirely one Vodacom block; if it settles back under 12%, the outflow was one holder's exit; if it stays high, something broader is being unwound.
  • NMB's 200,000-share block deserves the same watch as Tuesday's KCB block did: two straight sessions of blocks in the same counter would say something is being built; a quiet Thursday would say it was rebalancing.
  • DSE's 3.56% jump on 9 deals is the week's thin-volume move to watch. A hold near 6,400 on Thursday says the market found a level; a sharp fall back says nine deals set a price nobody else would pay.
  • MCB's whipsaw continues: down 7.14%, up 3.08%, down 2.99% across three sessions. Watch whether Thursday holds above 320 or the slide continues toward 300.
  • NICO's slide continues: down 1.47% to 3,340, its third down day since Friday's jump — the jump is now more than three-quarters gone. Watch whether Thursday holds above 3,340 or the slide continues toward 3,200.
  • TTP and JATU held their levels on Wednesday, so the repricing reading stands. Watch for a drift back up over the next sessions — a slow recovery would confirm the market is comfortable at the new levels.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.9% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention. TBL rose 1.03% on Wednesday; TCC rose 0.21%.
  • The bond market's continued appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par suggests institutions expect rates to stay contained — a supportive backdrop for equities, if Thursday's flow confirms it.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb a TZS 9.1 billion foreign sale in a single trade — most of it in one block — and fall less than half a percent is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling last week did not disappear on Wednesday — it bought the block. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Turnover rose 394% and the market fell less than half a percent — because most of Wednesday's turnover was two blocks, and the biggest of them was one seller meeting one buyer at an agreed price. Headlines measure how much changed hands; prices measure what it was worth, and volume measures whether it meant anything.
