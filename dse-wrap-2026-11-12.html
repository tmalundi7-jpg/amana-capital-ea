Daily DSE Wrap | Thursday, 12th November 2026

The Blocks Came Back — and This Time Foreigners Bought Them Too

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 11.69 billion of equity turnover on Thursday, almost unchanged on Wednesday — but again almost none of that growth came from everyday trading. Two pre-arranged blocks accounted for about TZS 9.81 billion of it: 2,000,000 Vodacom Tanzania shares and 720,606 TBL shares. The normal board, where everyday trading happens, turned over about TZS 1.88 billion on 928,059 shares — down 14.3% on Wednesday. What changed was who was on both sides: foreigners sold 83.88% of all equity turnover and bought 61.80% of it. The market absorbed a second day of block trading with the DSE All Share Index down just 0.26% and the Tanzania Share Index down 0.50%. Somebody wanted those shares at that price — and this time the somebody was foreign too. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report itself did not show its usual daily prices tables in the version published on its website — so open, high, low, per-counter turnover and outstanding buy/sell orders are not available for Thursday, and nothing below estimates them. The normal-board turnover above is average price times volume, from the report's own rounded average prices, so it is approximate. Second, the exchange's own website price card again showed ZAN TIMIZA ETF at 110, but the report itself shows it traded 574,346 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: Foreigners on Both Sides

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Thursday's two blocks were 74.56% of all shares traded and about 83.9% of all turnover value, and they carried three deals between them. Strip them out and the market looks ordinary — about TZS 1.88 billion of normal-board turnover, down 14.3% on Wednesday, in 2,719 deals.

The foreign flow is the one to understand, because it explains both sides of the headline. Foreigners sold TZS 9,806.06 million of shares on Thursday — 83.88% of all equity turnover — and bought TZS 7,224.75 million — 61.80%. The two numbers line up with the blocks almost exactly: foreign selling matches the implied block value of about TZS 9,805.76 million, and local selling of about TZS 1,884.44 million matches the normal-board turnover of about TZS 1,884.32 million. The plain reading is that the blocks changed hands between foreign holders, while local investors did the everyday selling. Wednesday's story was one foreign seller meeting a local buyer; Thursday's is foreign holders rearranging among themselves. The net outflow — what foreigners sold minus what they bought — collapsed from TZS 9.10 billion to TZS 2.58 billion, and the market fell half a percent.

The test, as ever, is the next session: if Friday's foreign buying falls back toward single digits, Thursday was one holder's exit meeting another holder's entry, not a foreign retreat. If it stays high, foreign holders are actively repositioning. And the two-session test from Tuesday has a third answer: TTP held at 315 and JATU held at 175 on Thursday, both unchanged for a third session. The falls were repricing — the market found new levels for two counters almost nobody was watching — not one seller meeting an empty book. One session is a headline. Three sessions are the beginning of evidence, and the evidence says the levels held.

4. Bond Market: TZS 54 Billion — the Biggest Day in This Run

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 54.00 billion across 30 deals on Thursday, up from TZS 2.45 billion on Wednesday — the bond board's biggest session in this run by a wide margin — with the caveat in section 1 that the headline is the face value; the transaction value was higher still at TZS 64.94 billion, 20.3% above face. On the government board, the 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 36.68 billion in face value across 14 deals at 118.54–122.00% of par, followed by the 15-year 12.25% bond (TZS 15.00 billion face, 2 deals at 119.33% of par). The 20-year 12.25% bond traded TZS 1.03 billion face in 3 deals at 114.21–120.00% of par, and the 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 1.06 billion face in 2 deals at 133.45–138.95% of par. There was no activity in the corporate bonds segment. The report's bond price table did not appear in the published version, so no yields are stated for this session. The appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par continues at full strength: institutions are paying up for duration, and on Thursday they did it in size.

The ETF board rose after Wednesday's quiet session: TZS 353.33 million from 801,696 shares in 262 deals, up from TZS 188.32 million on Wednesday. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 574,346 shares at 120, about 72% of all ETF shares — down from 94% on Wednesday — with IEACLC-ETF adding 204,277 shares at 1,360 and VERTEX-ETF 23,073 shares at 300.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The first question for Friday is whether foreign buying falls back toward single digits. Thursday's 61.80% is almost entirely the blocks; if it settles back under 12%, the buying was one holder's entry; if it stays high, foreign holders are actively repositioning.
  • TBL's 720,606-share block deserves the same watch as the Vodacom and NMB blocks did: two straight sessions of blocks in the same counter would say something is being built; a quiet Friday would say it was rebalancing.
  • MCB's whipsaw continues: down 7.14%, up 3.08%, down 2.99%, up 4.62% across four sessions. Watch whether Friday holds above 340 or the slide resumes toward 300.
  • NICO's slide continues: down 0.90% to 3,310, its fourth down day since Friday's jump — the jump is now almost entirely gone. Watch whether Friday holds above 3,310 or the slide continues toward 3,200.
  • KA and NMG both moved with no trade printed for a third straight session. Watch for a printed trade in either — a trade would say the market has found a level it will transact at; continued silence would say the prices are being set without buyers or sellers meeting.
  • TTP and JATU held their levels for a third session, so the repricing reading stands. Watch for a drift back up over the next sessions — a slow recovery would confirm the market is comfortable at the new levels.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (8.0% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention. TBL fell 0.71% on Thursday despite its block; TCC rose 0.57%.
  • The bond market's continued appetite for 20- and 25-year paper at well above par suggests institutions expect rates to stay contained — a supportive backdrop for equities, if Friday's flow confirms it.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb a second straight day of block trading — most of it foreign holders trading among themselves — and fall half a percent is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling last week did not disappear on Thursday — it kept the everyday board trading in 2,719 deals while the blocks passed overhead. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---

6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence

  • Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If price bands, block trades or VWAP are new concepts, read Stocks, Bonds, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and Understanding Market Data.

  • Think in years, not days. An 83.88% foreign-selling figure sounds like an exodus until you learn foreigners bought 61.80% of the same market on the same day. The steady compounding of well-managed companies is the signal.
  • Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Turnover was flat and the market fell half a percent — because most of Thursday's turnover was two blocks, and the biggest story in them was who was on both sides. Headlines measure how much changed hands; prices measure what it was worth, and volume measures whether it meant anything.
