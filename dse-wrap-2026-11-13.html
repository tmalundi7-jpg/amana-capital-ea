Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 13th November 2026

The Blocks Left — and the Market Shrunk to Its Real Size

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 1.35 billion of equity turnover on Friday, down 88.5% on Thursday — but Thursday's TZS 11.69 billion was two pre-arranged blocks, and Friday had none. Strip the blocks out of both days and the comparison is gentler but still down: Thursday's everyday board was about TZS 1.88 billion; Friday's whole market was TZS 1.35 billion, on 680,747 shares in 2,389 deals. What left with the blocks was the foreign flow: foreigners bought 0.67% of turnover (TZS 8.98 million) and sold nothing at all — the first session in this run with zero foreign selling. The market absorbed the quiet day with the DSE All Share Index down just 0.17% and the Tanzania Share Index down 0.22%. The blocks were never the market. Friday shows what the market is when they are gone. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report itself did not show its usual daily prices tables on Wednesday and Thursday — but on Friday they are back, so open, high, low, per-counter turnover and outstanding buy/sell orders are available again, and nothing below estimates them. Second, the exchange's own website price card again showed ZAN TIMIZA ETF at 110, but the report itself shows it traded 1,984,290 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. One small discrepancy is carried as printed: the equity prices table's rows add to TZS 1,347,507,975 against the printed total of TZS 1,347,506,875, a difference of TZS 1,100 on a TZS 1,347.51 million total. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: What the Market Is Without the Blocks

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Wednesday's and Thursday's blocks were about 84% of all turnover value on those days, and they carried the foreign flow with them. Strip them out and the market looks ordinary — about TZS 1.35 billion of everyday trading on Friday, down from about TZS 1.88 billion on Thursday, in 2,389 deals.

The foreign flow is the one to understand, because it explains both sides of the headline. Foreigners sold nothing on Friday — 0.00% of turnover — and bought TZS 8.98 million — 0.67%. Compare Thursday: foreigners sold 83.88% and bought 61.80%. The foreign presence in this market, on the evidence of this week, is the block market: when the blocks are there, foreigners are on both sides of them; when the blocks are gone, foreigners are barely there at all. The net outflow — what foreigners sold minus what they bought — went from TZS 2.58 billion on Thursday to a net inflow of TZS 8.98 million on Friday, and the market fell two-tenths of a percent. That is not a market foreigners are leaving; it is a market whose foreign flow only exists inside pre-arranged trades.

The other thing Friday brings back is visibility. With the prices tables published again, the market's plumbing is readable: at the close, Vodacom Tanzania had 79,143 shares waiting to buy against only 1,559 waiting to sell — buyers outnumbering sellers fifty to one — while NMB Bank had 87,644 waiting to buy against 303,955 waiting to sell. ZAN TIMIZA ETF had 671,888 shares waiting to sell and none waiting to buy. Those are the orders that did not trade, and they say more about Monday than Friday's closes do.

4. Bond Market: Back to Normal Size — and Yields Are Visible Again

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 6.73 billion across 5 deals on Friday, down from TZS 54.00 billion on Thursday — back to normal size after the biggest bond session in this run — with the caveat in section 1 that the headline is the face value; the transaction value was higher still at TZS 7.34 billion, 9.2% above face. On the government board, the 5-year 10.25% bond traded TZS 5.00 billion in face value in 1 deal at 105.0006% of par — 74.3% of the session's face value — followed by the 20-year 12.25% bond (TZS 1.72 billion face, 2 deals at 119.1272% and 122.0000% of par) and the 20-year 15.25% bond (TZS 0.01 billion face, 1 deal at 109.0000% of par). Corporate bonds saw activity for the first time in this run: the SAMIA 5-year 12.00% bond traded TZS 10.00 million in face value in 1 deal at 100.0000% of par. The report's bond price table was visible again, so yields are stated for the first time since the redesign: the 5-year 10.25% bond yielded 9.91%, the 20-year 12.25% bond yielded 10.45% and 10.15% on its two trades, the 20-year 15.25% bond yielded 14.44%, and the corporate bond yielded 12.06%. Institutions are still paying above par for long-dated paper, but on Friday they did it in normal size.

The ETF board fell to TZS 266.14 million from 353.33 million on Thursday, but the share count rose sharply: 2,041,168 shares in 213 deals, up from 801,696. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 1,984,290 shares at 120 — 97.2% of all ETF shares and 89.1% of ETF turnover — with IEACLC-ETF adding 11,350 shares at 1,360 and VERTEX-ETF 45,528 shares at 300.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The first question for Monday is whether the foreign flow comes back with the blocks or stays absent. Friday's 0.67% buying and 0.00% selling says the foreign presence lives inside pre-arranged trades; a block on Monday would test that reading at once.
  • MUCOBA's 11.11% jump deserves the same watch as the banks' moves did: every trade printed at 400, so the question is whether Monday trades above 400 or the price was one session's print.
  • MCB's whipsaw continues: down 7.14%, up 3.08%, down 2.99%, up 4.62%, down 2.94% across five sessions. Watch whether Monday holds above 330 or the slide resumes toward 300.
  • TTP's fall to 305 breaks the three-session hold at 315. Watch whether Monday steadies above 305 or the slide continues — the repricing reading from Tuesday no longer holds.
  • KA and NMG both held flat with no trade printed for a fourth straight session. Watch for a printed trade in either — a trade would say the market has found a level it will transact at; continued silence would say the prices are being set without buyers or sellers meeting.
  • JATU held at 175 for a fourth session, so the repricing reading stands there. Watch for a drift back up over the next sessions — a slow recovery would confirm the market is comfortable at the new level.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.9% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention. TBL rose 0.92% on Friday despite the quiet market; TCC slipped 0.07%.
  • The bond market's yields are visible again — 9.91% on the 5-year, about 10.1–10.5% on the 20-year — and institutions are still paying above par for long-dated paper, a supportive backdrop for equities if Monday's flow confirms it.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb two straight days of block trading — most of it foreign holders trading among themselves — and fall half a percent, then print a quiet TZS 1.35 billion day and fall two-tenths of a percent, is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling last week did not disappear on Friday — it kept the everyday board trading in 2,389 deals while the blocks stayed away. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---

7. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

Headlines measure how much changed hands; prices measure what it was worth, and volume measures whether it meant anything.
