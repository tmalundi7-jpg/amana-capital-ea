Daily DSE Wrap | Monday, 16th November 2026

Volume Doubled, Prices Stood Still — and the Block Came Back

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 2.78 billion of equity turnover on Monday, up 106.6% on Friday — but the headline needs unpacking before it means anything. Inside it sits a block trade: NMB Bank, 200,000 shares on the pre-arranged board, its price and value not shown in the report. Strip the block out and the everyday board is about TZS 2.47 billion on 1,166,336 shares in 3,993 deals — still up 83.3% in value on Friday, and the busiest everyday session since 6 November. The foreign flow came back with the block: foreigners bought 7.97% of turnover (TZS 221.84 million) and sold 2.66% (TZS 74.17 million), a net inflow of TZS 147.67 million after Friday's TZS 8.98 million. And yet the market barely moved: the DSE All Share Index rose 0.16% and the Tanzania Share Index 0.06%. Volume doubled, prices stood still. That is a market absorbing flow, not chasing it. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report did not show its usual daily prices tables on Monday — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders and bond yields are not published this session, and nothing below estimates them. Per-counter turnovers are volume multiplied by the report's printed average price, which is rounded to whole shillings. Second, the exchange's own website price card again showed ZAN TIMIZA ETF at 110, but the report itself shows it traded 394,210 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. One further discrepancy is carried as printed: the everyday board's lines add to TZS 2,469.44 million against the headline total of TZS 2,784.48 million, a difference of TZS 315.04 million, which is the block's value — but the block's price is not shown, so the difference is reported, not reconciled. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: A Block, a Busy Board, and Prices That Did Not Move

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Monday's headline of TZS 2.78 billion includes the NMB block of 200,000 shares, and the foreign flow came back with it — foreigners bought TZS 221.84 million and sold TZS 74.17 million, after buying TZS 8.98 million and selling nothing on Friday. The foreign presence in this market, on the evidence of the last two sessions, lives inside pre-arranged trades.

But Monday is not only a block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Strip the block out and the everyday board still rose 83.3% in value on Friday, to about TZS 2.47 billion, with deals up to 3,993 from 2,389. Friday's lesson was that the market without blocks is about TZS 1.35 billion a day; Monday says the everyday market can be nearly twice that when the banks are busy. CRDB Bank traded 482,207 shares in 1,436 deals and NMB Bank 453,815 in 1,428 — the two banks alone were three-quarters of the headline. And yet the indices barely moved: DSEI +0.16%, TSI +0.06%. The market absorbed twice Friday's flow and stood still.

The day's sharpest moves were at the edges, not the centre. TTP's 14.75% jump to 350 sits just inside its 15% daily cap ceiling of 350.75 — a near-cap move on 4,446 shares. MCB's 9.09% fall to 300 is its sixth sharp move in six sessions, and the pattern has changed: five sessions of whipsaw, and now a slide, with volume nearly trebling. At the centre, the banks held: CRDB and NMB both flat, Vodacom up 1.56%.

4. Bond Market: The Biggest Session in This Run — One Bond Did Almost All of It

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 47.13 billion across 45 deals on Monday, up from TZS 6.73 billion on Friday — the biggest bond session in this run — with the caveat in section 1 that the headline is the face value; the transaction value on the government board was higher still at TZS 56.48 billion, 20.5% above the TZS 46.88 billion face value. One bond did almost all of it: the 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 45.96 billion in face value in 32 deals at 117.8830% to 121.5398% of par — 98.0% of the session's government face value. The rest of the government board was small: the 20-year 15.49% bond (TZS 0.44 billion face, 4 deals at 100.0000% and 133.5627% of par), the 25-year 15.95% bond (TZS 0.22 billion face, 3 deals at 100.0000% and 127.4262% of par), the 20-year 12.25% bond (TZS 0.20 billion face, 1 deal at 100.0000% of par), the 15-year 13.50% bond (TZS 0.03 billion face, 1 deal at 106.6668% of par) and the 25-year 12.56% bond (TZS 0.03 billion face, 1 deal at 100.0000% of par). Corporate bonds traded three small lines totalling TZS 264.77 million in face value — a 4-year 13.00% bond, a 5-year 12.00% bond and a 5-year 13.00% bond — with a transaction value of TZS 265.97 million. No bond price table was visible in the report, so no yields are stated for this session — institutions are still paying above par for long-dated paper, but Monday's report does not say what yield they accepted.

The ETF board halved to TZS 136.45 million from 266.14 million on Friday, on 487,477 shares in 380 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 394,210 shares at 120 — 80.9% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 58,211 shares at 1,360 and VERTEX-ETF 35,056 shares at 295.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The first question for Tuesday is whether the foreign flow stays now that it has returned with a block. Monday's 7.97% buying and 2.66% selling says the foreign presence lives inside pre-arranged trades; another block on Tuesday would test that reading at once.
  • TTP's 14.75% jump to 350 sits just inside its daily cap. Watch whether Tuesday trades above 350 or the price was one session's print on 4,446 shares.
  • MCB's whipsaw has become a slide: down 9.09% to 300 after down 2.94%, with volume nearly trebling. Watch whether Tuesday holds above 300 or the slide continues toward the levels it traded at in October.
  • MKCB rose 4.00% to 3,640 after Friday's 5.11% rise. Watch whether the two-session climb holds or gives back.
  • KA, JATU and NMG all sat without a printed trade for a fifth straight session. Watch for a printed trade in any of them — a trade would say the market has found a level it will transact at; continued silence would say the prices are being set without buyers or sellers meeting.
  • MUCOBA held at 400 after Friday's 11.11% jump. Watch whether Tuesday trades above 400 or the price was one session's print.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (8.0% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention. TBL slipped 0.81% on Monday despite the busy market; TCC held flat.
  • The bond market's yields are not visible this session — the price table was not published — so Monday says institutions are still paying above par for long-dated paper, but not what yield they accepted. A session with the table back would say more.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb twice Friday's everyday flow — most of it in two banks — and rise two-tenths of a percent is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling last week did not disappear on Monday — it kept the everyday board trading in 3,993 deals while the block came back. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---
