Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 17th November 2026

One Block, One Foreign Exit — and the Everyday Market Shrank

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 11.54 billion of equity turnover on Tuesday, up 314.5% on Monday — but the headline needs unpacking before it means anything, because almost all of it is one trade. Inside it sits a block: Vodacom Tanzania, 7,067,478 shares on the pre-arranged board, its price and value not shown in the report. The Vodacom row of the exchange's own prices table carries the block inside it — 7,275,696 shares for TZS 9,610.26 million, or 83.3% of the day's entire turnover. Strip the block out and the everyday board is about TZS 2.21 billion on 1,113,249 shares in 2,768 deals — down 10.7% in value on Monday, with deals down 30.7%. The foreign flow tells the same story from the other side: foreigners bought nothing at all (0.00%) and sold 84.70% of turnover (TZS 9,776.50 million), a net outflow of TZS 9,776.50 million after Monday's net inflow of TZS 147.67 million. And yet the market barely moved: the DSE All Share Index rose 0.08% and the Tanzania Share Index 0.37%. One holder left through the pre-arranged board; the everyday market shrank rather than panicking. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report this session DOES show its usual daily prices tables — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders, market capitalisation and bond yields are all published, after a day missing on Monday. Second, the exchange's own website price card again showed ZAN TIMIZA ETF at 110, but the report itself shows it traded 423,608 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. One further discrepancy is carried as printed: the everyday board's lines add to about TZS 2,206.43 million, using the report's own table turnovers for every counter except Vodacom, whose normal-board turnover is its volume multiplied by the report's printed average price of 1,320, because the table row includes the block. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: A Block, a Foreign Exit, and Prices That Did Not Move

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Tuesday's headline of TZS 11.54 billion is 83.3% one Vodacom row, and the foreign flow came with it — foreigners sold TZS 9,776.50 million and bought nothing at all, after buying TZS 221.84 million and selling TZS 74.17 million on Monday. The foreign presence in this market, on the evidence of the last two sessions, lives inside pre-arranged trades — and on Tuesday it left through one.

But Tuesday is not only a block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Strip the block out and the everyday board shrank: about TZS 2.21 billion, down 10.7% in value on Monday, with deals down to 2,768 from 3,993. Monday's lesson was that the market without blocks is about TZS 2.47 billion a day; Tuesday says the everyday market can be smaller than that when the flow goes through the pre-arranged board instead. CRDB Bank traded 399,074 shares in 1,102 deals and NMB Bank 343,474 in 871 — the two banks alone were 76.9% of the everyday board. And yet the indices barely moved: DSEI +0.08%, TSI +0.37%. The market absorbed a foreign exit of nearly ten billion shillings and stood still.

The day's sharpest moves were at the edges, not the centre, and they were mirror images. TOL fell 14.52% to 1,060 on a single printed price, with no buyers waiting and 114,874 shares offered — sellers waiting, nobody bidding. MCB rose 10.00% to 330 with 100,147 buyers waiting and no sellers offering at all. At the centre, the banks held: CRDB down 0.36%, NMB up 1.17%, Vodacom up 1.54% on the normal board.

4. Bond Market: Still Heavy — and Yields Are Printed Again

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 44.26 billion across 34 deals on Tuesday, down from TZS 47.13 billion on Monday, with the transaction value on the government board higher still at TZS 50.55 billion, 14.2% above the TZS 44.26 billion face value. There was no activity in the corporate bonds segment. Unlike Monday, when one bond did almost all of it, Tuesday spread its flow: the 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 6.04 billion in face value in 8 deals at 100.0000% to 140.6339% of par, the 5-year 13.00% bond traded TZS 10.00 billion in face value in 1 deal at 114.3018% of par, the 5-year 10.25% bond traded TZS 5.00 billion in face value in 1 deal at 105.5333% of par, and a second 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 5.00 billion in face value in 1 deal at 137.3893% of par. The rest was small lines from TZS 0.02 billion to TZS 4.00 billion in face value. Yields are printed again — the bond price table is back — ranging from 8.1122% (10-year 11.44%) to 16.5172% (15-year 13.50%), with most 20-year 15.49% paper yielding about 10.5–10.8%, and the 25-year 15.95% bond trading at 157.5848% of par for a 9.3410% yield — the session's highest price and one of its lowest yields.

The ETF board fell to TZS 111.11 million from 136.45 million on Monday, on 501,667 shares in 256 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 423,608 shares at 120 — 84.4% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 35,381 shares at 1,360 and VERTEX-ETF 42,678 shares at 290.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The first question for Wednesday is whether the foreign selling was one holder leaving or the start of a run. Tuesday's 84.70% selling and 0.00% buying says the exit came through a block; another block on Wednesday would test that reading at once.
  • TOL's 14.52% fall to 1,060 sits six shillings above its daily floor, with no buyers waiting. Watch whether Wednesday trades above 1,060 or the price was one session's print on a single price.
  • MCB's whipsaw has flipped again: up 10.00% to 330 after down 9.09%, with 100,147 buyers waiting and no offers. Watch whether Wednesday holds above 330 or the whipsaw turns again.
  • PAL rose 9.09% to 300 after Monday's 1.85% rise. Watch whether the two-session climb holds or gives back.
  • JATU printed its first trade in six sessions (50 shares at 175). KA sat without a printed trade for a sixth straight session. Watch for a printed trade in KA — a trade would say the market has found a level it will transact at; continued silence would say the price is being set without buyers or sellers meeting.
  • MUCOBA held at 400 on 430 shares after Friday's 11.11% jump. Watch whether Wednesday trades above 400 or the price was one session's print.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (8.0% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention. TBL slipped 0.92% on Tuesday despite the busy market; TCC held flat.
  • The bond market's yields are visible again — 8.11% to 16.52% — after a session with the table missing. A session with the table back says institutions are still paying above par for long-dated paper, and now says what yield they accepted: mostly about 10.5–10.8% on 20-year paper.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb a foreign exit of nearly ten billion shillings — most of it in one pre-arranged trade — and rise a tenth of a percent is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling last week did not disappear on Tuesday — it kept the everyday board trading in 2,768 deals while the block went through behind it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---
