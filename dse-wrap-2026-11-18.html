Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 18th November 2026

A Smaller Block, a Balanced Foreign Flow — and a Bond Board That Did the Real Business

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 2.75 billion of equity turnover on Wednesday, down 76.2% on Tuesday — but the comparison says more about the blocks than about the market, because Tuesday's headline was a 7,067,478-share block and Wednesday's is a 1,014,000-share one. Inside Wednesday's headline sits the block again: Vodacom Tanzania, 1,014,000 shares on the pre-arranged board, its price and value not shown in the report. The Vodacom row of the exchange's own prices table carries the block inside it — 1,068,130 shares for TZS 1,410.95 million, or 51.3% of the day's entire turnover. Strip the block out and the everyday board is about TZS 1.41 billion on 715,314 shares in 2,444 deals — down 36.1% in value on Tuesday, the smallest everyday board in this run. The foreign flow tells a different story from Tuesday's: foreigners bought 48.71% of turnover (TZS 1,338.68 million) and sold 48.76% (TZS 1,340.26 million), a net outflow of just TZS 1.58 million after Tuesday's one-way exit of TZS 9,776.50 million. And the market rose on it: the DSE All Share Index rose 0.53% and the Tanzania Share Index 0.63%. The bond board did the session's real business — TZS 103.46 billion in 77 deals, up 133.8% — while the everyday equity board shrank. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report this session DOES show its usual daily prices tables — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders, market capitalisation and bond yields are all published. Second, the exchange's own website carries a separate "ZAN-ETF 110" card line, but the report itself shows ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 366,471 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. Two further discrepancies are carried as printed: the 28 equity row turnovers sum to TZS 2,748,420,490 against the printed total of TZS 2,748,420,500 (TZS 10 apart), and the 77 bond rows sum to TZS 103.46370 billion against the printed total of TZS 103.46060 billion. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: A Smaller Block, a Balanced Foreign Flow, and a Bond Board That Did the Business

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Wednesday's headline of TZS 2.75 billion is 51.3% one Vodacom row, and the block inside it is 58.6% of all shares traded. But the block is a seventh the size of Tuesday's, and the foreign flow that came with Tuesday's block did not come with this one — foreigners bought TZS 1,338.68 million and sold TZS 1,340.26 million, a net outflow of TZS 1.58 million. Tuesday's exit, on Wednesday's evidence, was one holder leaving, not a run.

But Wednesday is not only a block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Strip the block out and the everyday board shrank again: about TZS 1.41 billion, down 36.1% in value on Tuesday, with deals down to 2,444 from 2,768. Tuesday's lesson was that the market without blocks is about TZS 2.21 billion a day; Wednesday says the everyday market can be smaller than that when the flow goes through the pre-arranged board instead — and smaller still when it does not. CRDB Bank traded 226,795 shares in 856 deals and NMB Bank 305,369 in 755 — the two banks alone were 82.3% of the everyday board. And yet the indices rose: DSEI +0.53%, TSI +0.63%. The market absorbed a smaller block, a balanced foreign flow and a shrinking everyday board, and rose half a percent.

The day's sharpest moves were at the edges, not the centre, and they were the same two counters as Tuesday. TOL fell 14.15% to 910 on a single printed price, with no buyers waiting and 77,068 shares offered — sellers waiting, nobody bidding, for the second session running. MCB fell 1.52% to 325 with 76,326 buyers waiting and 29,536 offers. At the centre, the banks held: CRDB down 0.36%, NMB up 1.16%, Vodacom up 1.52% on the normal board.

4. Bond Market: The Biggest Session in This Run

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 103.46 billion across 77 deals on Wednesday, up from TZS 44.26 billion on Tuesday, with the transaction value on the government board higher still at TZS 121.69 billion, 17.6% above the TZS 103.46 billion face value. The corporate bonds segment also saw activity: the SAMIA 5-year 12.00% bond traded TZS 0.50 million in face value in 1 deal at 100.0000% of par, for a 12.0940% yield. The government board was dominated by one bond: the 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 55.73 billion in face value in 40 deals at 116.3067% to 125.0719% of par, including a single TZS 40.00 billion line at 125.0719% of par for a 10.1000% yield. The 2-year 9.50% bond traded TZS 13.50 billion in face value in 1 deal at 105.4403% of par, the 5-year 13.00% bond traded TZS 10.00 billion in face value in 1 deal at 114.5823% of par, and the 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 9.21 billion in face value in 4 deals at 100.0000% to 133.4612% of par. The rest was small lines from TZS 0.03 billion to TZS 4.00 billion in face value. Yields are printed again — the bond price table is back — ranging from 7.8500% (2-year 9.50%) to 16.9324% (20-year 15.49% at 100.0000% of par), with most 25-year 12.75% paper yielding about 10.8–11.0%.

The ETF board rose to TZS 148.22 million from 111.11 million on Tuesday, on 511,652 shares in 272 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 366,471 shares at 120 — 71.6% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 59,058 shares at 1,360 and VERTEX-ETF 86,123 shares at 280.

5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (coming week):

  • The first question for Thursday is whether the everyday board keeps shrinking. Wednesday's TZS 1.41 billion is the smallest in this run — watch whether Thursday's everyday board recovers toward TZS 2 billion or stays thin while the blocks do the volume.
  • TOL's 14.15% fall to 910 sits nine shillings above its daily floor, with no buyers waiting, for the second session running. Watch whether Thursday trades above 910 or the price was two sessions' print on a single price.
  • MCB's whipsaw has flipped again: down 1.52% to 325 after up 10.00%, its eighth sharp move in eight sessions, with 76,326 buyers waiting. Watch whether Thursday holds above 325 or the whipsaw turns again.
  • MBP rose 3.13% to 1,980 at the top of its range on only 1,479 shares. Watch whether the move holds on a busier day or gives back.
  • JATU printed its second straight trade (15 shares at 175). KA sat without a printed trade for a seventh straight session. Watch for a printed trade in KA — a trade would say the market has found a level it will transact at; continued silence would say the price is being set without buyers or sellers meeting.
  • MUCOBA held at 400 on 531 shares after Friday's 11.11% jump. Watch whether Thursday trades above 400 or the price was one session's print.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.9% net) and TCC (7.1% net) tend to attract attention. TBL rose 1.55% on Wednesday; TCC rose 0.35%.
  • The bond market's yields are visible again — 7.85% to 16.93% — and the biggest bond session in this run says institutions are still paying above par for long-dated paper, now in size: a single TZS 40.00 billion line at 125.0719% of par.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb a foreign exit of nearly ten billion shillings on Tuesday — most of it in one pre-arranged trade — and rise half a percent on Wednesday while the foreign flow balances is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling last week did not disappear on Wednesday — it kept the everyday board trading in 2,444 deals while the block went through behind it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---
