Daily DSE Wrap | Thursday, 19th November 2026

A Bigger Block, a One-Way Foreign Flow — and an Everyday Board That Grew

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 5.59 billion of equity turnover on Thursday, up 103.3% on Wednesday — but the comparison says more about the blocks than about the market, because Wednesday's headline was a 1,014,000-share block and Thursday's is two blocks totalling 2,620,000 shares. Inside Thursday's headline sit both blocks: Vodacom Tanzania, 2,520,000 shares, and Tanga Cement, 100,000 shares, on the pre-arranged board, their prices and values not shown in the report. The Vodacom row of the exchange's own prices table carries its block inside it — 2,525,324 shares for TZS 3,333.81 million, or 59.7% of the day's entire turnover. Strip both blocks out and the everyday board is about TZS 1.86 billion on 898,017 shares in 2,055 deals — up 32.0% in value on Wednesday, the first growth in the everyday board this week. The foreign flow tells a different story from Wednesday's: foreigners bought 0.01% of turnover (TZS 0.51 million) and sold 59.53% (TZS 3,326.40 million), a net outflow of TZS 3,325.89 million after Wednesday's balanced day. And the market rose on it: the DSE All Share Index rose 0.98% and the Tanzania Share Index 1.49%. The bond board quietened — TZS 32.39 billion in 18 deals, down 68.7% — while the corporate board stayed busy with a single TZS 6.00 billion line. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report this session DOES show its usual daily prices tables — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders, market capitalisation and bond yields are all published. Second, the exchange's own website carries a separate "ZAN-ETF 110" card line, but the report itself shows ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 1,225,569 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. Two further discrepancies are carried as printed: the government bond narrative's face values sum to TZS 26.31 billion against the table's TZS 26.30 billion, and the corporate bond narrative prints the TMRC 5-year 11.00% bond's face value as "6000.00 bln" while the bond table shows 6.00000 billion and the corporate face-value total of TZS 6,092.28 million confirms 6.00 billion. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: A Bigger Block, a One-Way Foreign Flow, and an Everyday Board That Grew

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Thursday's headline of TZS 5.59 billion is 59.7% one Vodacom row, and the two blocks together are 74.5% of all shares traded. But Thursday's blocks are bigger than Wednesday's, and the foreign flow that came with them went one way — foreigners bought TZS 0.51 million and sold TZS 3,326.40 million, a net outflow of TZS 3,325.89 million. The Vodacom block's implied value, about TZS 3,326.41 million, sits within TZS 11,020 of the foreign-selling total. Thursday's exit, on Thursday's evidence, was one holder leaving through the pre-arranged board, not a run.

But Thursday is not only a block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Strip the blocks out and the everyday board grew: about TZS 1.86 billion, up 32.0% in value on Wednesday, with shares up 25.5% — though deals fell to 2,055 from 2,444, so the growth came on fewer, bigger tickets. Wednesday's lesson was that the market without blocks is about TZS 1.41 billion a day; Thursday says the everyday market can grow past that when the flow stays on the open board — and grow while the blocks do the volume beside it. CRDB Bank traded 271,045 shares in 767 deals and NMB Bank 464,150 in 631 — the two banks alone were 85.4% of the everyday board. And yet the indices rose: DSEI +0.98%, TSI +1.49%. The market absorbed a bigger block, a one-way foreign flow and a growing everyday board, and rose about one percent.

The day's sharpest moves were at the edges, not the centre, and they were the same two counters as Wednesday in a new shape. TOL rose 0.55% to 915 on a 800–1,040 range, with 29,204 buyers waiting and no offers — buyers back, sellers gone, after two sessions at the floor with nobody bidding. MCB held flat at 325 with 82,440 buyers waiting and 26,065 offers, ending its eight-session whipsaw. At the centre, the banks held: CRDB up 0.72%, NMB up 2.86% through 1,800, Vodacom up 3.73% on the normal board.

4. Bond Market: A Quieter Session, a Busy Corporate Board

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 32.39 billion across 18 deals on Thursday, down from TZS 103.46 billion on Wednesday, with the transaction value on the government board at TZS 32.00 billion, 21.5% above the TZS 26.30 billion face value. The corporate bonds segment stayed busy: 7 deals, TZS 6,092.28 million in face value, TZS 6,343.00 million in transaction value — dominated by one line, the TMRC 5-year 11.00% bond, TZS 6.00 billion in face value at 104.1720% of par for an 11.1117% yield. The government board's biggest line was the 20-year 15.49% bond, TZS 9.26 billion in face value in 4 deals at 128.0000% to 142.9824% of par. The 2-year 9.50% bond traded TZS 13.50 billion in face value in 1 deal at 105.8169% of par, the 15-year 14.50% bond traded TZS 2.49 billion in face value in 1 deal at 129.9847% of par, and the 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 0.97 billion in face value in 4 deals at 118.4071% to 120.1341% of par. The rest was small lines from TZS 0.09 billion to TZS 0.50 billion in face value. Yields are printed again — the bond price table is back — ranging from 7.6271% (2-year 9.50%) to 14.3688% (4-year 13.00% at 100.0000% of par), with most 25-year 12.75% paper yielding about 10.6–10.7%.

The ETF board rose to TZS 191.07 million from 148.22 million on Wednesday, on 1,332,769 shares in 225 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 1,225,569 shares at 120 — 92.0% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 13,786 shares at 1,360 and VERTEX-ETF 93,414 shares at 285.

  • The bond market's yields are visible again — 7.63% to 14.37% — and the corporate board stayed busy, with a single TZS 6.00 billion line at 104.1720% of par.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb a foreign exit of more than three billion shillings on Thursday — most of it in one pre-arranged trade — and rise about one percent while the foreign flow goes one way is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic bid that absorbed three straight sessions of heavy foreign selling last week did not disappear on Thursday — it kept the everyday board trading in 2,055 deals while the blocks went through behind it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. ---
