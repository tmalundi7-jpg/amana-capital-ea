Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 20th November 2026

Three Blocks, No Foreign Exit — and an Everyday Board That Shrank

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 3.76 billion of equity turnover on Friday, down 32.7% on Thursday — but the comparison says more about the blocks than about the market, because Thursday's headline was two blocks and Friday's is three: KCB Bank, 305,099 shares; Tanzania Portland Cement, 150,000 shares; and Vodacom Tanzania, 500,000 shares, on the pre-arranged board, their prices and values not shown in the report. The three block rows together are TZS 2.32 billion, 61.8% of the day's entire turnover. Strip all three blocks out and the everyday board is about TZS 1.62 billion on 854,605 shares in 2,346 deals — down 12.8% in value on Thursday, though deals rose 14.2%, so the everyday board shrank on more, smaller tickets. The foreign flow tells a different story from Thursday's: foreigners bought 0.38% of turnover (TZS 14.38 million) and sold 0.00% (TZS 0.16 million), a net inflow of TZS 14.22 million after Thursday's net outflow of TZS 3,325.89 million. The three blocks' implied value, about TZS 2.14 billion, sits against foreign selling of TZS 0.16 million — Friday's blocks were domestic holders moving stock through the pre-arranged board, not a foreign exit. And the market barely moved on it: the DSE All Share Index rose 0.12% and the Tanzania Share Index 0.16%. The bond board did the session's real business by deal count — TZS 27.44 billion in 92 deals, the most bond deals of the week — while the corporate board quietened to a single deal. Three caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report this session DOES show its usual daily prices tables — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders, market capitalisation and bond yields are all published. Second, the exchange's own website carries a separate "ZAN-ETF 110" card line, but the report itself shows ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 1,014,943 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. Third, three discrepancies are carried as printed: the 28 equity row turnovers add to TZS 3,761,506,555, TZS 120,301 below the printed total of TZS 3,761,626,856, and the 28 row volumes add to 1,809,804, 100 above the printed total of 1,809,704 (the AFRIPRISE row shows 26,968 shares against the narrative's 26,868); and the 92 bond rows as transcribed sum to TZS 27.34960 billion, TZS 0.10 billion below the printed total of TZS 27.44960 billion. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: Three Blocks, No Foreign Exit, and an Everyday Board That Shrank

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Friday's headline of TZS 3.76 billion is 61.8% three block rows, and the three blocks together are 52.8% of all shares traded. But Friday's blocks are different from Thursday's in the way that matters. Thursday's Vodacom block, worth about TZS 3,326.41 million, sat within TZS 11,020 of the day's foreign selling — one holder leaving. Friday's three blocks, worth about TZS 2,138.47 million together, sit against foreign selling of TZS 0.16 million. Friday's blocks were domestic holders moving stock through the pre-arranged board, and the foreign flow turned the other way — foreigners bought TZS 14.38 million and sold TZS 0.16 million, a net inflow of TZS 14.22 million.

But Friday is not only a block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Strip the blocks out and the everyday board shrank: about TZS 1.62 billion, down 12.8% in value on Thursday, with shares down 4.8% — though deals rose to 2,346 from 2,055, so the shrinkage came on more, smaller tickets. Thursday's lesson was that the everyday market can grow past TZS 1.8 billion when the flow stays on the open board; Friday says it can also fall back toward TZS 1.6 billion while the blocks do the volume beside it. CRDB Bank traded 254,522 shares in 760 deals and NMB Bank 293,243 in 731 — the two banks alone were 76.3% of the everyday board. And yet the indices barely moved: DSEI +0.12%, TSI +0.16%. The market absorbed three blocks, a small foreign inflow and a shrinking everyday board, and rose about a tenth of a percent.

The day's sharpest moves were at the edges, not the centre, and they were the same two counters as Thursday in a new shape. TOL rose 12.02% to 1,025 on a 950–1,050 range, with 17,954 buyers waiting and 87 offers — buyers back, sellers nearly gone, after two sessions at the floor with nobody bidding. MCB fell 1.54% to 320 with 82,933 shares changing hands — volume up 253.4% on Thursday — turning its whipsaw again after one flat day. At the centre, the banks held: CRDB up 0.36%, NMB flat at 1,800 for a second session, Vodacom down 0.72% on the normal board.

4. Bond Market: The Busiest Session of the Week by Deal Count, Led by One Bond

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 27.44 billion across 92 deals on Friday, down from TZS 32.39 billion on Thursday, with the transaction value on the government board at TZS 31.09 billion, 13.5% above the TZS 27.40 billion face value. Ninety-two deals is the most bond deals of the week — Monday 45, Tuesday 34, Wednesday 77, Thursday 18 — but the face value is the week's smallest, because one bond did almost all of it: the 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 16.67 billion in face value in 82 deals at 115.0000% to 121.5000% of par — 89.1% of all bond deals and 60.8% of the government face value. The 5-year 10.25% bond traded TZS 10.00 billion in face value in 1 deal at 104.7840% of par, and the 25-year 15.00% bond traded TZS 0.60 billion in face value in 2 deals at 132.0000% and 133.0000% of par. The rest was small lines from TZS 0.03 billion to TZS 0.05 billion in face value. The corporate bonds segment quietened to a single deal: the ABIK 5-year 12.00% bond, TZS 49.00 million in face value at 105.0000% of par, transaction value TZS 51.45 million — after Thursday's seven deals and TZS 6,092.28 million face. Yields are printed again — the bond price table is back — ranging from 10.0197% (5-year 10.25%) to 16.7194% (20-year 15.49% at 100.0000% of par), with most 25-year 12.75% paper yielding about 10.5–10.8%.

The ETF board eased to TZS 178.05 million from 191.07 million on Thursday, on 1,106,698 shares in 209 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 1,014,943 shares at 120 — 91.7% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 28,561 shares at 1,360 and VERTEX-ETF 63,194 shares at 290.

Long-Term:

A market whose largest companies can absorb three blocks in a session — most of the day's turnover in pre-arranged trades — and rise about a tenth of a percent while the foreign flow turns to a small inflow is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic flow that moved Friday's blocks did not disappear into the headline — it kept the everyday board trading in 2,346 deals while the blocks went through behind it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. For the week, the indices rose 1.88% (DSEI) and 2.73% (TSI) from Friday 13 November's closes — a week of wild turnover days, and the indices moved less than 2.8%. ---
