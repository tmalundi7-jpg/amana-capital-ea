Daily DSE Wrap | Monday, 23rd November 2026

No Blocks, More Deals — and the Everyday Board Grew

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 1.71 billion of equity turnover on Monday, down 54.6% on Friday — but the comparison says more about the blocks than about the market, because Friday's headline was three blocks and Monday's is none: no block-trade paragraph appears anywhere in the report, so the whole headline is the normal board. Strip Friday's blocks out and the comparison reverses — Monday's everyday board of TZS 1.71 billion is up 5.2% on Friday's everyday TZS 1.62 billion, on 798,450 shares in 2,807 deals, with deals up 19.5% on Friday, so the board grew on more, smaller tickets again. The foreign flow turned back the other way: foreigners bought 0.08% of turnover (TZS 1.34 million) and sold 0.61% (TZS 10.46 million), a net outflow of TZS 9.12 million after Friday's net inflow of TZS 14.22 million — and on the ETF board foreigners sold TZS 9.00 million against buying of TZS 0.01 million. And the market barely moved on it: the DSE All Share Index rose 0.13% and the Tanzania Share Index 0.18%. The bond board quietened to TZS 14.93 billion in 49 deals, down 45.6% in value on Friday — but the corporate board was busy again, with TZS 6.06 billion in face value, almost all of it one 5-year bond. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report this session DOES show its usual daily prices tables — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders, market capitalisation and bond yields are all published. Second, the exchange's own website carries a separate "ZAN-ETF 110" card line, but the report itself shows ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 282,871 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. Third, one misprint is carried as printed: the corporate bond narrative says the 11.00% bond traded "TZS 6000.00 bln" in face value, where the report's own tables show 6.00000 billion, i.e. 6,000.00 million. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: No Blocks, More Deals, and an Everyday Board That Grew

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Friday's headline of TZS 3.76 billion was 61.8% three block rows. Monday's headline of TZS 1.71 billion is none of that. There is no block-trade paragraph in Monday's report at all, so the whole headline is the normal board — and the normal board grew: TZS 1,708.12 million, up 5.2% on Friday's everyday TZS 1,623.03 million, on 798,450 shares in 2,807 deals.

But Monday is not only a no-block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Deals rose 19.5% while shares fell 55.9% — because Friday's share count was three blocks, and Monday's deals are all on the open board. The everyday board grew on more, smaller tickets for the second session running. CRDB Bank traded 283,819 shares in 1,049 deals and NMB Bank 350,701 in 934 — the two banks alone were 83.2% of the day's turnover. And yet the indices barely moved: DSEI +0.13%, TSI +0.18%. The market absorbed a small foreign outflow and a shrinking share count, and rose about a tenth of a percent.

The day's sharpest moves were at the edges, not the centre, and both of them reversed. TOL fell 5.37% to 970 on a 950–1,140 range, with 11,458 buyers waiting and 27,221 offers — sellers back in force, after Friday's 17,954 buyers against 87 offers. MCB fell 4.69% to 305 with 30,266 shares changing hands — turning its whipsaw again after Friday's 1.54% fall. At the centre, the banks held: CRDB down 0.36%, NMB flat at 1,800, Vodacom up 2.17%.

4. Bond Market: Quieter by Deals, Busier on the Corporate Board

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 14.93 billion across 49 deals on Monday, down from TZS 27.44 billion on Friday, with the transaction value on the government board at TZS 10.50 billion, 18.1% above the TZS 8.89 billion face value. One bond did most of it again: the 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 5.90 billion in face value in 24 deals at 117.8830% to 120.8150% of par — 49.0% of all bond deals and 66.4% of the government face value. The 25-year 13.25% bond traded TZS 2.75 billion in face value in 9 deals at 119.0000% of par, the 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 0.13 billion in face value in 7 deals at 100.0000% and 120.0000% of par, and the rest was small lines from TZS 0.04 billion to TZS 0.07 billion in face value. The corporate bonds segment was busy again: TZS 6,055.00 million in face value in 6 deals, transaction value TZS 6,348.28 million — almost all of it the TMRC 5-year 11.00% bond, TZS 6,000.00 million in face value in 2 deals at 104.8059% of par, plus the ITRUST 4-year 13.00% bond, TZS 45.00 million in face value in 3 deals at 110.9600%, and the ABIK 5-year 12.00% bond, TZS 10.00 million in face value in 1 deal at 100.0000% — after Friday's single corporate deal of TZS 49.00 million face. Yields are printed again — the bond price table is back — ranging from 10.5193% (25-year 12.75% at 120.8150% of par) to 16.2786% (20-year 15.49% at 100.0000% of par), with most 25-year 12.75% paper yielding about 10.5–10.8%.

The ETF board eased to TZS 82.38 million from 178.05 million on Friday, on 350,301 shares in 319 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 282,871 shares at 120 — 80.7% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 27,033 shares at 1,350 and VERTEX-ETF 40,397 shares at 300.

  • The first question for Tuesday is whether the everyday board holds above TZS 1.7 billion. Monday's TZS 1.71 billion is up 5.2% on Friday's everyday board — watch whether Tuesday's everyday board holds above TZS 1.7 billion or falls back toward TZS 1.6 billion.
  • TOL's 5.37% fall to 970 came on a 950–1,140 range, with 11,458 buyers waiting and 27,221 offers — sellers back in force. Watch whether Tuesday trades above 970 or the wide range was three sessions' print.
  • MCB's whipsaw has turned again: down 4.69% to 305 on 30,266 shares after Friday's 1.54% fall, with 52,484 buyers waiting. Watch whether Tuesday holds above 305 or the whipsaw turns again.
  • JATU printed its first trade (10 shares at 175) after two straight sessions without one. KA printed no trade, after two straight sessions with one.   • NMB held at 1,800 for a third session. Watch whether Tuesday holds above 1,800 or the move gives back.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.8% net) and TCC (7.0% net) tend to attract attention. TBL rose 1.12% to 9,960 on Monday; TCC rose 1.13% to 14,310.
  • The bond market's yields are visible again — 10.52% to 16.28% — and the corporate board was busy again on Monday, with TZS 6,055.00 million in face value after Friday's single deal.

Long-Term:

A market whose everyday board can grow past TZS 1.7 billion in a session with no blocks at all — on 2,807 deals, all of them on the open board — and rise about a tenth of a percent while the foreign flow turns to a small outflow is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic flow that carried Monday's board did not need the pre-arranged board beside it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. For the week so far, the indices are up 0.13% (DSEI) and 0.18% (TSI) from Friday's closes — one session in, and the market has barely moved. ---
