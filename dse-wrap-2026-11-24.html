Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 24th November 2026

A Bigger Board, Lower Indices — and No Blocks Again

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 2.17 billion of equity turnover on Tuesday, up 27.1% on Monday — and there is no block-trade paragraph in Tuesday's report either, so the whole headline is the normal board for a second straight session: the everyday board itself grew, on 983,654 shares in 3,404 deals, with deals up 21.3% on Monday. But the market fell on it: the DSE All Share Index fell 0.55% and the Tanzania Share Index 0.77%, with all three sector indices down and breadth turning more negative, 6 counters up against 15 down. The foreign flow turned back the other way again: foreigners bought 0.01% of turnover (TZS 0.22 million) and sold nothing — a small net inflow of TZS 0.22 million after Monday's net outflow of TZS 9.12 million. And the bond board collapsed to TZS 1.80 billion in 18 deals, down 87.9% in value on Monday — after Monday's busy corporate session. Two caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report this session DOES show its usual daily prices tables — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders, market capitalisation and bond yields are all published. Second, the exchange's own website carries a separate "ZAN-ETF 110" card line, but the report itself shows ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 487,836 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. Third, one small gap is carried as printed: the 21 equity row turnovers in the report's table add to TZS 2,171,117,460, which is TZS 98,840 below the printed total of TZS 2,171,216,300, while the row volumes and deals add exactly to the printed totals — the headline below uses the printed total. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: A Bigger Board, Lower Indices, and No Blocks Again

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Friday's headline of TZS 3.76 billion was 61.8% three block rows. Tuesday's headline of TZS 2.17 billion is none of that. There is no block-trade paragraph in Tuesday's report at all, so the whole headline is the normal board — and the normal board grew: TZS 2,171.22 million, up 27.1% on Monday's TZS 1,708.12 million, on 983,654 shares in 3,404 deals.

But Tuesday is not only a no-block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Deals rose 21.3% while shares rose 23.2% — the board grew on more tickets and more shares together, for the second session running. CRDB Bank traded 458,507 shares in 1,417 deals and NMB Bank 312,917 in 1,117 — the two banks alone were 84.0% of the day's turnover. And yet the indices fell: DSEI −0.55%, TSI −0.77%. The market absorbed a bigger board, a small foreign inflow and a quieter bond session, and fell about half a percent.

The day's sharpest moves were at the edges, not the centre, and both of them reversed. TOL rose 11.86% to 1,085 on a 970–1,115 range, with 41,347 buyers waiting and 4,817 offers — buyers back in force, after Monday's 11,458 buyers against 27,221 offers. TTP fell 10.29% to 305 on a 305–340 range. MCB rose 8.20% to 330 with 21,059 shares changing hands — turning its whipsaw again after Monday's 4.69% fall. At the centre, the banks fell: CRDB down 0.36%, NMB down 1.67%, Vodacom down 0.71%.

4. Bond Market: A Quiet Day After Monday's Busy Corporate Session

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 1.80 billion across 18 deals on Tuesday, down from TZS 14.93 billion on Monday, with the transaction value on the government board at TZS 2.14 billion, 36.3% above the TZS 1.57 billion face value. One bond did most of it: the 25-year 15.00% bond traded TZS 0.70 billion in face value in 2 deals at 142.3539% and 155.7575% of par — 44.6% of the government face value. The 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 0.33 billion in face value in 4 deals at 123.0000% to 137.0000% of par, the 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 0.22 billion in face value in 1 deal at 119.9997% of par, and the rest was small lines from TZS 0.03 billion to TZS 0.05 billion in face value. The corporate bonds segment was quiet: TZS 245.00 million in face value in 7 deals, transaction value TZS 249.23 million — the ITRUST 4-year 13.00% bond, TZS 84.50 million in face value in 1 deal at 105.0000% of par, and the 5-year 12.00% bonds, TZS 160.50 million in face value in 6 deals at 100.0000% of par — after Monday's TZS 6,055.00 million face. Yields are printed again — the bond price table is back — ranging from 9.5057% (25-year 15.00% at 155.7575% of par) to 12.6851% (ITRUST 4-year 13.00% at 105.0000% of par), with most 25-year paper yielding about 10.5–10.6%.

The ETF board grew to TZS 105.39 million from 82.38 million on Monday, on 583,478 shares in 311 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 487,836 shares at 120 — 83.6% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 17,630 shares at 1,350 and VERTEX-ETF 78,012 shares at 300.

  • TOL's 11.86% rise to 1,085 came on a 970–1,115 range, with 41,347 buyers waiting and 4,817 offers — buyers back in force. Watch whether Wednesday trades above 1,085 or the wide range was two sessions' print.
  • MCB's whipsaw has turned again: up 8.20% to 330 on 21,059 shares after Monday's 4.69% fall, with 25,002 buyers waiting. Watch whether Wednesday holds above 330 or the whipsaw turns again.
  • JATU printed no trade for a second straight session, with 25,000 buyers waiting and no offers. KA printed no trade, after two straight sessions with one. Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.8% net) and TCC (7.0% net) tend to attract attention. TBL fell 0.10% to 9,950 on Tuesday; TCC held at 14,310.
  • The bond market's yields are visible again — 9.51% to 12.69% — and the corporate board was quiet on Tuesday, with TZS 245.00 million in face value after Monday's TZS 6,055.00 million.

Long-Term:

A market whose everyday board can grow past TZS 2.1 billion in a session with no blocks at all — on 3,404 deals, all of them on the open board — and fall about half a percent while the foreign flow turns to a small inflow is a market with depth at its centre. The domestic flow that carried Tuesday's board did not need the pre-arranged board beside it. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. For the week so far, the indices are down 0.43% (DSEI) and 0.59% (TSI) from Friday's closes — two sessions in, and the market has given back a little more than Monday gained. ---
