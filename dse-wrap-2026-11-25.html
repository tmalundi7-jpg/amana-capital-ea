Daily DSE Wrap | Wednesday, 25th November 2026

A Block Returns, the Headline Doubles — and the Everyday Board Barely Moves

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 4.08 billion of equity turnover on Wednesday, up 87.9% on Tuesday — but TZS 1.77 billion of it, 43.3%, is a single NMB Bank block of 981,215 shares on the pre-arranged board, price and value not shown. Strip the block out and the everyday board is TZS 2.31 billion, up only 6.4% on Tuesday, on 1,102,092 shares in 2,936 deals — shares up 12.0%, deals down 13.7%, so the everyday board grew on fewer, larger tickets. And the market fell on it: the DSE All Share Index fell 0.48% and the Tanzania Share Index 0.17%, though the sectors split — banks down 0.56% while industrials rose 0.16% and commercial services rose 1.24% — and breadth improved to 10 counters up against 11 down. The foreign flow turned to a net outflow: foreigners bought 0.01% of turnover (TZS 0.33 million) and sold 0.10% (TZS 4.13 million) — a net outflow of TZS 3.80 million after Tuesday's net inflow of TZS 0.22 million. And the bond board did the session's real business: TZS 18.36 billion in 33 deals, 10.2 times Tuesday, almost all of it one 20-year bond. Three caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report this session DOES show its usual daily prices tables — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders, market capitalisation and bond yields are all published. Second, the exchange's own website carries a separate "ZAN-ETF 110" card line, but the report itself shows ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 244,500 shares at 120, unchanged — every price below comes from the report, not the card. Third, two small gaps are carried as printed: the 20 equity row turnovers in the report's table add to TZS 4,079,479,275, which is TZS 9,001 below the printed total of TZS 4,079,488,276, while the row volumes add to 2,083,307, 100 above the printed total of 2,083,207 — the headline below uses the printed total; and the report's narrative says NMB had 947 deals on the normal board while its own table shows 948, and 948 is what adds to the printed total of 2,937. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: A Block Returns, and the Everyday Board Barely Moves

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Wednesday's headline of TZS 4.08 billion is 43.3% one NMB block row. Strip the block out and the everyday board is TZS 2.31 billion — up only 6.4% on Tuesday's TZS 2.17 billion, on 1,102,092 shares in 2,936 deals. And foreigners sold only TZS 4.13 million all session, so the block was domestic holders moving stock between themselves, not an exit — the same shape as Friday's three blocks, which were also domestic.

But Wednesday is not only a block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Deals fell 13.7% while shares rose 12.0% on the everyday board — the board grew on fewer, larger tickets. CRDB Bank traded 418,952 shares in 1,281 deals and NMB Bank 525,268 on the normal board in 947 deals — the two banks alone were 89.8% of everyday turnover. And yet the indices fell: DSEI −0.48%, TSI −0.17%. The market absorbed a bigger headline, a small foreign outflow and the week's busiest bond session, and fell about half a percent.

The day's sharpest moves were at the edges, not the centre, and both of them reversed. TTP rose 11.48% to 340 — traded only at 340, no range at all — after falling 10.29% on Tuesday. PAL fell 5.17% to 275 on 5,064 shares. MKCB rose 8.51% to 3,570 with 1,146 shares changing hands — on a 3,500–3,770 range, the widest on the board in points. TOL rose 2.30% to 1,110 with 14,003 shares changing hands — on a 1,090–1,150 range, with 38,007 buyers waiting. At the centre, the banks fell: CRDB down 0.72%, NMB down 0.56%, Vodacom up 1.43%.

4. Bond Market: One Bond Does Almost All of It

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 18.36 billion across 33 deals on Wednesday, up from TZS 1.80 billion on Tuesday — 10.2 times Tuesday's value — with the transaction value on the government board at TZS 25.53 billion, 39.4% above the TZS 18.31 billion face value. One bond did almost all of it: the 20-year 15.25% bond traded TZS 15.00 billion in face value in 1 deal at 141.4475% of par — 81.9% of the government face value. The 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 1.22 billion in face value in 8 deals at 132.1651% to 139.9074% of par, the 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 1.26 billion in face value in 6 deals at 120.6119% to 122.0000% of par, and the 25-year 15.95% bond traded TZS 0.68 billion in face value in 6 deals at 135.0000% to 145.4022% of par — and the rest was TZS 0.16 billion across three small lines. The corporate bonds segment was quiet: TZS 53.90 million in face value in 7 deals, transaction value TZS 53.90 million — the 5-year 10.25% bond, TZS 25.00 million in face value in 1 deal at 100.0000% of par, and the 5-year 12.00% bonds, TZS 28.90 million in face value in 6 deals at 100.0000% of par as printed in the narrative and the corporate table, though the bond annex shows TZS 33.40 million for the 12.00% bonds — carried as printed. Yields are printed again — the bond price table is back — ranging from 10.3944% (20-year 15.49% at 139.9005% of par) to 14.4736% (15-year 13.50% at 100.0000% of par), with most 25-year paper yielding about 10.4–10.6%.

The ETF board grew to TZS 128.54 million from 105.39 million on Tuesday, on 352,285 shares in 247 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 244,500 shares at 120 — 69.4% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 63,660 shares at 1,350 and VERTEX-ETF 44,125 shares at 305.

  • TTP's 11.48% rise to 340 came with no range at all — every trade at 340. Watch whether Thursday trades away from 340 or the price holds at the level the whole session printed.
  • MKCB's 8.51% rise to 3,570 came on a 3,500–3,770 range, the second-widest on the board in points after DSE's 450-point range, on only 1,146 shares. Watch whether Thursday holds above 3,570 or the wide range was one session's print.
  • TOL's 2.30% rise to 1,110 came on a 1,090–1,150 range, with 38,007 buyers waiting and 26,777 offers. Watch whether Thursday trades above 1,110 or the range narrows.

  • NMB fell 0.56% to 1,760 after falling 1.67% on Tuesday. Watch whether Thursday holds above 1,760 or the move gives back further.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.8% net) and TCC (7.0% net) tend to attract attention. TBL rose 0.10% to 9,960 on Wednesday; TCC rose 0.28% to 14,350.
  • The bond market's yields are visible again — 10.39% to 14.47% — and the corporate board was quiet on Wednesday, with TZS 53.90 million in face value after Tuesday's TZS 245.00 million.

Long-Term:

A market whose headline can double on one domestic block — on 2,937 deals, 981,215 of them pre-arranged — and fall about half a percent while the foreign flow turns to a small outflow is a market whose everyday board is steadier than its headline. The domestic flow that carried Wednesday's everyday board did not need the pre-arranged board beside it to grow 6.4%. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. For the week so far, the indices are down 0.90% (DSEI) and 0.75% (TSI) from Friday's closes — three sessions in, and the market has given back a little more than Monday gained. ---
