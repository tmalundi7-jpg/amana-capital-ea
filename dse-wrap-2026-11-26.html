Daily DSE Wrap | Thursday, 26th November 2026

Two Blocks, a Smaller Headline — and the Indices Turned Up

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 2.59 billion of equity turnover on Thursday, down 36.4% on Wednesday — but Wednesday's headline carried one block worth TZS 1.77 billion and Thursday's carries two blocks worth about TZS 511.00 million together, 19.7% of the headline. Strip the blocks out and the everyday board is TZS 2.08 billion, down only 9.9% on Wednesday's everyday board, on 961,417 shares in 2,716 deals — shares down 12.8%, deals down 7.5%, so the everyday board shrank on fewer, smaller tickets. And the market rose on it: the DSE All Share Index rose 0.45% and the Tanzania Share Index 0.65%, though the sectors split — commercial services up 4.75% while banks rose 0.14% and industrials fell 0.16% — and breadth narrowed to 7 counters up against 8 down. The foreign flow turned hard to a net inflow: foreigners bought 9.94% of turnover (TZS 257.86 million) and sold 2.09% (TZS 54.15 million) — a net inflow of TZS 203.71 million after Wednesday's net outflow of TZS 3.80 million. And the bond board did the session's real business again: TZS 32.74 billion in 18 deals, 78.3% above Wednesday, almost all of it two bonds. Three caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report this session DOES show its usual daily prices tables — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders, market capitalisation and bond yields are all published. Second, the exchange's own website price card disagrees with the report itself on three counters — EABL (card 5,840 vs report 5,760), USL (card 30 vs report 25) and ZAN-ETF (card 110 vs report 120) — and the card's change column is unusable this session, so every price and change below comes from the report, not the card. Third, two small gaps are carried as printed: the 28 equity row turnovers in the report's table add to TZS 2,593,473,660, which is TZS 10 below the printed total of TZS 2,593,473,670, while the row volumes and deals add exactly to the printed totals of 1,105,417 and 2,718 — the headline below uses the printed total; and the report's narrative says NMB's VWAP is 1,750 while its own table turnover divided by its volume gives 1,755.47. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: Two Blocks, a Smaller Headline — and the Indices Turned Up

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Thursday's headline of TZS 2.59 billion is 19.7% two block rows — CRDB's 94,000 shares and TPCC's 50,000 shares. Strip the blocks out and the everyday board is TZS 2.08 billion — down only 9.9% on Wednesday's everyday board of TZS 2.31 billion, on 961,417 shares in 2,716 deals. And foreigners bought TZS 257.86 million against selling of TZS 54.15 million — a net inflow of TZS 203.71 million, the first net inflow since Tuesday — so the buying came from outside as well as inside.

But Thursday is not only a block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Deals fell 7.5% while shares fell 12.8% on the everyday board — the board shrank on fewer, smaller tickets. CRDB Bank traded 385,295 shares on the normal board in 1,115 deals and NMB Bank 356,943 shares in 924 deals — the two banks alone were 81.2% of everyday turnover. And yet the indices rose: DSEI +0.45%, TSI +0.65%. The market absorbed a smaller headline, a large foreign inflow and the week's biggest bond session, and rose about half a percent.

The day's sharpest move was at the centre, not the edges. Vodacom rose 4.93% to 1,490 — the session's biggest gainer — closing at its high with 97,795 buyers waiting and no offers outstanding. TOL rose 3.60% to 1,150 with 7,753 shares changing hands — on a 1,100–1,170 range, with 38,845 buyers waiting. JATU traded again — 25,000 shares in one deal at 175, clearing its whole offer book — after two sessions of silence. At the edges, NICO fell 1.17% on the board's widest range, 300 shillings from 3,300 to 3,600, and MUCOBA fell 2.56% to 380, the day's biggest faller. The ETF board exploded to TZS 557.71 million, 4.3 times Wednesday, on ZAN TIMIZA's 2,433,141 shares at 120.

4. Bond Market: Two Bonds Do Almost All of It

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 32.74 billion across 18 deals on Thursday, up from TZS 18.36 billion on Wednesday — 78.3% more value in 45.5% fewer deals — with the transaction value on the government board at TZS 41.84 billion, 27.8% above the TZS 32.75 billion face value. Two bonds did almost all of it: the 20-year 15.25% bond traded TZS 15.33 billion in face value in 2 deals at 131.8183% and 147.6862% of par — 46.8% of the face value — and the 5-year 10.25% bond traded TZS 10.00 billion in face value in 1 deal at 106.5544% of par — 30.5% of the face value. The 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 5.63 billion in face value in 3 deals at 111.6189% to 139.2338% of par, the 20-year 12.25% bond traded TZS 0.90 billion in face value in 3 deals at 120.0000% of par, and the 25-year 12.75% bond traded TZS 0.49 billion in face value in 5 deals at 120.9997% of par — and the rest was TZS 0.41 billion across three small lines. The corporate bonds segment was silent: no activity at all — after Wednesday's TZS 53.90 million in face value in 7 deals. Yields are printed again — the bond price table is back — ranging from 9.5716% (5-year 10.25% at 106.5544% of par) to 15.7166% (25-year 15.00% at 100.0000% of par), with most 20- and 25-year paper yielding about 10.4–11.6%.

The ETF board exploded to TZS 557.71 million from TZS 128.54 million on Wednesday, on 2,654,719 shares in 279 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 2,433,141 shares at 120 — 91.7% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 194,366 shares at 1,340 and VERTEX-ETF 27,212 shares at 310.

  • VODA's 4.93% rise to 1,490 came with 97,795 buyers waiting and no offers outstanding, closing at its high. Watch whether Monday trades above 1,490 or the buyer queue thins.
  • TOL's 3.60% rise to 1,150 came on a 1,100–1,170 range, with 38,845 buyers waiting and 34,401 offers. Watch whether Monday trades above 1,150 or the range narrows.
  • JATU traded again — 25,000 shares in 1 deal at 175, clearing its whole offer book. KA printed no trade for a fourth straight session.   • The foreign flow turned to a net inflow of TZS 203.71 million after Wednesday's net outflow of TZS 3.80 million. Watch whether Monday's foreign buying holds near 10% of turnover or falls back toward Wednesday's 0.01%.

Medium-Term (Q4 2026 — the fourth quarter of 2026):

  • Dividend-season positioning typically begins in the final quarter — high-yield counters like TBL (7.8% net) and TCC (7.0% net) tend to attract attention. TBL was flat at 9,960 on Thursday; TCC fell 0.77% to 14,240.
  • The bond market's yields are visible again — 9.57% to 15.72% — and the corporate board was silent on Thursday, with no activity at all after Wednesday's TZS 53.90 million.

Long-Term:

A market whose headline can shrink by more than a third on two smaller blocks — on 2,718 deals, 144,000 of them pre-arranged — and rise about half a percent while the foreign flow turns to a net inflow of TZS 203.71 million is a market whose everyday board is steadier than its headline. The domestic flow that carried Thursday's everyday board did not need the pre-arranged board beside it to stay above TZS 2.0 billion. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. For the week so far, the indices are down 0.46% (DSEI) and 0.11% (TSI) from Friday's closes — four sessions in, and the market has given back a little of what Monday gained. ---
