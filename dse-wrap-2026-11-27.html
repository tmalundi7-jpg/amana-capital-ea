Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 27th November 2026

Two Giant Blocks, a Quiet Everyday Board — and Foreigners Sold TZS 6.80 Billion

The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange printed TZS 10.73 billion of equity turnover on Friday, up 313.7% on Thursday — but 83.9% of the headline is two blocks: 1,500,000 CRDB Bank shares and 910,000 Tanzania Portland Cement shares, worth about TZS 9.00 billion together. Strip the blocks out and the everyday board is TZS 1.73 billion, down 17.2% on Thursday's everyday board, on 844,399 shares in 2,229 deals — shares down 12.2%, deals down 17.9%, so the everyday board shrank on fewer, smaller tickets for the second session running. And the market stood still on it: the DSE All Share Index rose 0.02% and the Tanzania Share Index fell 0.20%, though the sectors split — commercial services up 1.30% while banks fell 0.48% and industrials fell 0.28% — and breadth narrowed to 6 counters up against 10 down. The foreign flow turned violently to a net outflow: foreigners bought 0.05% of turnover (TZS 5.34 million) and sold 63.34% (TZS 6,797.00 million) — a net outflow of TZS 6,791.66 million after Thursday's net inflow of TZS 203.71 million. And the bond board cooled hard: TZS 10.85 billion in 38 deals, 66.9% below Thursday, though the corporate board traded again after Thursday's silence. Three caveats belong at the top of this wrap. First, the exchange's report this session DOES show its usual daily prices tables — open, high, low, per-counter turnover, outstanding buy/sell orders, market capitalisation and bond yields are all published. Second, the exchange's own website price card disagrees with the report itself on one counter — ZAN-ETF (card 110 vs report 120), the second straight session the card line is wrong on ZAN — and the card's change column is unusable this session, so every price and change below comes from the report, not the card. Third, two small gaps are carried as printed: the 28 equity row turnovers in the report's table add to TZS 10,730,217,215, which is TZS 1,000 below the printed total of TZS 10,730,218,215, while the row volumes and deals add exactly to the printed totals of 3,254,399 and 2,233 — the headline below uses the printed total; and the report's narrative says bonds totalled TZS 10.85 billion while its own bond table total is TZS 10.86 billion. Nothing below is estimated.

3. In Focus: Two Giant Blocks, a Quiet Everyday Board — and Foreigners Sold

A block trade is a deal agreed in advance between two parties and reported through the exchange, rather than struck on the open order book. It does not move the price the way an open-market trade does, because the price is settled beforehand — and it can sit outside the day's traded range. What a block does is move the headline: Friday's headline of TZS 10.73 billion is 83.9% two block rows — CRDB's 1,500,000 shares and TPCC's 910,000 shares. Strip the blocks out and the everyday board is TZS 1.73 billion — down 17.2% on Thursday's everyday board of TZS 2.08 billion, on 844,399 shares in 2,229 deals. And foreigners sold TZS 6,797.00 million against buying of TZS 5.34 million — a net outflow of TZS 6,791.66 million, the largest foreign selling of the week by a wide margin — so the selling came from outside as well as inside.

But Friday is not only a block story, and that is what makes it interesting. Deals fell 17.8% while shares rose 194.4% on the headline — the board's shape changed completely, because two pre-arranged deals carried 2,410,000 of the 3,254,399 shares. CRDB Bank traded 317,937 shares on the normal board in 822 deals and NMB Bank 205,733 shares in 600 deals — the two banks alone were 71.6% of everyday turnover. And yet the indices stood still: DSEI +0.02%, TSI −0.20%. The market absorbed the week's biggest headline, the week's biggest foreign outflow and the week's second-quietest everyday board, and moved about nothing.

The day's sharpest move was at the edges, not the centre. MUCOBA rose 5.26% to 400 — the session's biggest gainer — a day after being the biggest faller. TOL rose 3.48% to 1,190 with 7,777 shares changing hands — on a 1,170–1,200 range, with its order book flipped to 11,279 buyers against 38,986 offers. MCB traded 36,250 shares in 60 deals on a 305–325 range — its busiest session of the week. At the centre, the banks drifted — CRDB down 0.36% to 2,750, NMB down 0.57% to 1,750 — and Vodacom rose 1.34% to 1,510. The ETF board fell back to TZS 139.91 million, 74.9% below Thursday, on 264,406 shares in 177 deals.

4. Bond Market: Two Bonds Do Most of It Again — and the Corporate Board Trades

Bond trading printed a headline TZS 10.85 billion across 38 deals on Friday, down from TZS 32.74 billion on Thursday — 66.9% less value in more than twice as many deals — with the transaction value on the government board at TZS 13.71 billion, 35.5% above the TZS 10.12 billion face value. Two bonds did most of it: the 20-year 15.49% bond traded TZS 6.07 billion in face value in 7 deals at 116.5559% to 150.6017% of par — 55.9% of the face value — and the 25-year 13.25% bond traded TZS 1.93 billion in face value in 11 deals at 119.0000% of par — 17.8% of the face value. The 25-year 15.00% bond traded TZS 0.68 billion in face value in 2 deals at 140.2275% and 142.4740% of par, the 20-year 14.00% bond traded TZS 0.51 billion in face value in 3 deals at 128.2127% and 130.6366% of par, and the 20-year 12.10% bond traded TZS 0.50 billion in face value in 1 deal at 110.8802% of par — and the rest was TZS 0.67 billion across six small lines. The corporate bonds segment traded again: TZS 736.00 million in face value in 6 deals, transaction value TZS 743.05 million — a 5-year 12.00% bond (TZS 706.00 million in 4 deals) and a 7-year 10.50% bond (TZS 30.00 million in 2 deals) — after Thursday's silence and Wednesday's TZS 53.90 million. Yields are printed again — the bond price table is back — ranging from 9.5115% (20-year 15.49% at 146.6018% of par) to 14.4615% (5-year 12.75% at 100.0000% of par), with most 20- and 25-year paper yielding about 10.0–11.5%.

The ETF board fell back to TZS 139.91 million from TZS 557.71 million on Thursday, on 264,406 shares in 177 deals. ZAN TIMIZA ETF traded 117,072 shares at 120 — 44.3% of all ETF shares — with IEACLC-ETF adding 77,647 shares at 1,340 and VERTEX-ETF 69,687 shares at 315.

Long-Term:

A market whose headline can more than quadruple on two pre-arranged deals — on 2,233 deals, 2,410,000 of them pre-arranged — and move about nothing while foreigners sell TZS 6.80 billion is a market whose everyday board is quieter than its headline. The domestic flow that carried Friday's everyday board did not need the pre-arranged board beside it to stay above TZS 1.7 billion. That steadiness at the centre, not the noise at the edges, is what long-term compounding is built on. For the week as a whole, the indices are down 0.44% (DSEI) and 0.31% (TSI) from Friday's closes — five sessions in, and the market has given back a little of what Monday gained. ---
