[0] Daily DSE Wrap | Friday, 25th September 2026
[1] Equities Reclaim High Ground as Bond Turnover Contracts for Third Straight Session
[2] The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange closed the week on a strong note, with both indices reaching new post-split highs and equity turnover recovering above the TZS 3 billion mark. Yet the most significant development was not in the equity market at all — it was in the bond market, where turnover contracted for the third consecutive session. This is the pattern that experienced investors watch for: when institutional money steps away from fixed income while equities attract fresh capital, a rotation may be quietly underway. For everyday investors, this is a moment to pay close attention.
[4] 1. Market Snapshot
[5] The session moved the market back into the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 3.78 billion, after Thursday's brief dip into the NORMAL range. The deal count rose 7% to 3,719, confirming that everyday investors remained engaged. The NMB block trade of 429,000 shares was absorbed without difficulty.
[6] The DSEI closed at a new post-split high of 4,682.13, up 24 points, while the TSI climbed 57 points to 10,419.49. Both indices have now risen for three consecutive sessions.
[7] Foreign participation remains negligible. Foreign buying was zero for the second consecutive session, while foreign selling was 11.31% of turnover (TZS 428 million), concentrated in NMB and CRDB profit-taking. Local investors bought 100% and sold 88.69% of turnover. The market is entirely domestically driven.
[8] Bond market turnover fell 9.4% to TZS 18.91 billion — the third consecutive contraction (TZS 39.48 bn → TZS 20.87 bn → TZS 18.91 bn). This is a pattern worth watching.

Losers: DCB (–4.5%), NICO (–3.5%), TCCL (–2.8%), MCB (–1.3%), VODA (–0.8%).

[13] MBP added 2.9% to 2,150, while MUCOBA surged 11.1% to 450 on very light volume. These smaller counters can be volatile; investors should be cautious.
[14] DCB was the session's worst performer, falling 4.5% to 420. The order book is heavily offer-weighted: 265,768 offers against just 19,600 bids — a ratio of 13.6 to 1 in favour of sellers. This is a strong signal of selling pressure.
[15] NICO fell 3.5% to 3,610, while TCCL dropped 2.8% to 3,810.
[16] VODA closed at 1,280, down 0.8%. The order book is offer-heavy: 146,116 offers against 46,608 bids — a ratio of 3.1 to 1 in favour of sellers.
[18] 3. In Focus: The Bond-to-Equity Rotation Signal — What It Means for Everyday Investors
[19] Friday's session delivered the clearest signal yet of a potential rotation from bonds to equities. Bond market turnover has now contracted for three consecutive sessions:
[20] While bond turnover has fallen 52% over three sessions, equity turnover has recovered from TZS 2.73 billion to TZS 3.78 billion. This is the pattern that experienced investors watch for: when institutional money steps away from fixed income while equities attract fresh capital, a rotation may be underway.
[21] Why does this matter?
[22] When institutions buy government bonds, they are parking cash in a safe, predictable asset. When they sell bonds or stop buying, that cash has to go somewhere. Often, it moves into equities — particularly high-quality, dividend-paying stocks like CRDB, NMB, and TCC.
[23] The signal is not yet fully confirmed. For it to be confirmed, we would need to see bond turnover remain below TZS 10 billion for at least two more sessions while equity turnover stays above TZS 5 billion. But the direction of travel is clear.
[24] What should everyday investors do?
[25] The lesson is not to rush into the market. It is to pay attention to the signals and position accordingly. If the rotation is real, quality stocks like CRDB, TBL, and TCC may benefit. If it is a false start, the daily price bands and disciplined limit orders will protect you from overpaying.
[26] The most important thing is to avoid chasing sharp moves. Instead, identify the stocks you want to own, set your limit prices, and wait for the market to come to you.
[28] 4. Bond Market: Distressed Seller Exits at 19% Yield
[29] Government bond turnover fell 9.4% to TZS 18.91 billion on Friday, the third consecutive contraction. The most active instruments were:
[30] 10-year 11.44% bond (Nov 2019): TZS 16.03 billion at 90.38% to 111.65%, yielding 8.17% to 19.08%
[31] 25-year 15.75% bond (Jan 2025): TZS 0.78 billion at 140.00% to 147.52%, yielding 10.60% to 11.27%
[32] 20-year 12.25% bond (Jul 2026): TZS 0.82 billion at 105.00%, yielding 11.93%
[33] 25-year 15.00% bond (Aug 2025): TZS 0.40 billion at 136.72%, yielding 10.89%
[34] The most striking development was the 10-year 11.44% bond trading at 90.38%, yielding 19.08%. This is a distressed sale — an institution exiting at a significant loss. Such trades often occur when an institution needs to raise cash quickly, and they can create opportunities for patient buyers.
[35] What does this mean for everyday investors?
[36] The bond market continues to offer attractive yields for those seeking predictable, tax-free income. The 25-year 15.75% bond at 10.60% to 11.27% tax-free remains an attractive option for long-term savers. However, the distressed sale in the 10-year bond suggests that some institutions are under pressure. This is not a reason to panic — it is a reason to be selective.
[37] For those considering bonds, the key is to focus on quality and yield. The 25-year bonds at over 10% tax-free are attractive. The 10-year bond at 19% is a distressed sale and should be treated with caution.
