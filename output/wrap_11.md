Daily DSE Wrap | Tuesday, 11th August 2026

Bond Market Heats Up While Equities Consolidate — A Calm, Locally Anchored Session

Tuesday’s session on the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange was steady rather than spectacular. Equity turnover eased to just under TZS 4 billion, while activity in the government bond market surged — a clear sign that local institutions are balancing their portfolios between shares and fixed income. The All-Share Index slipped modestly, but the market’s underlying character remains firmly domestic and orderly.

## 1. Market Snapshot

Equity activity returned to a more normal pace after Monday’s block-trade-driven spike. The number of deals remained healthy at nearly 3,000, confirming that retail and local institutional investors are still active across the market. Bond turnover, however, jumped sharply to TZS 41.55 billion — the highest in recent weeks — as institutions locked in long-dated government paper.

Foreign participation was negligible once again. International buyers accounted for just 0.51% of equity turnover, and sellers for 1.21%. The market’s centre of gravity is now overwhelmingly domestic, with local pension funds and investors setting the tone.

## 2. Top Movers

Gainers: MKCB (+3.5%), MUCOBA (+2.2%), MBP (+1.2%), TBL (+0.2%).
Losers: SWIS (–10.7%), MCB (–9.5%), DSE (–5.6%), TCCL (–4.9%).

The sharpest moves were in smaller or less liquid counters. SWIS fell over 10% on modest volume, while MCB gave back much of Monday’s gain on heavy trading of more than 232,000 shares. Such swings are a reminder that low-liquidity stocks can move rapidly and that price changes in these names should be interpreted with caution.

Among the larger, widely held stocks, CRDB was completely unchanged at 2,600, holding the level it has defended for several sessions. NMB also finished flat at 17,600, consolidating near its all-time high. VODA dipped to 1,010 after Monday’s strong showing, while TBL added a few shillings to close at 9,970.

## 3. In Focus: What a Quiet Equity Market and a Busy Bond Market Tell Us

Tuesday’s most important signal was not any single share price move but the contrast between the two main markets. Equities were calm, while government bonds saw TZS 41.55 billion in turnover — more than double the previous session.

This is not a sign that investors are abandoning shares. Rather, it reflects the natural, healthy behaviour of a maturing financial system. Large local institutions — pension funds, insurance companies, and collective investment schemes — have ongoing obligations that require them to hold both equities for growth and bonds for stable, tax-free income. After a busy period in the equity market, some of that institutional cash is now rotating into fixed income, locking in attractive long-term yields.

For the everyday investor, this is reassuring. It means the market is not dependent on a single source of demand. When equity trading slows, bond activity picks up, and vice versa. This balance between the two asset classes is one of the reasons the DSE has remained stable even during periods of quieter trading.

The standout bond was the 25-year government bond with a 13.25% coupon, which traded heavily at prices ranging from 100% to over 123% of face value, translating into yields of roughly 10.6% to 13.3%. For an investor seeking predictable, long-term income that is free of withholding tax, such instruments remain highly attractive.

## 4. Today’s Concept: The 5% Daily Price Band — Your Safety Net

On Tuesday, CRDB closed at 2,600. On Wednesday, the stock cannot trade at any price outside a band of ±5% around that closing level. The limits are:

Upper limit: 2,600 × 1.05 = 2,730

Lower limit: 2,600 × 0.95 = 2,470

Any order to buy above 2,730 or to sell below 2,470 will be automatically rejected by the DSE’s trading system. This mechanism prevents sudden, uncontrolled price swings and ensures orderly trading for everyone, whether you are buying ten shares or ten thousand.

## 5. Bond Market: Institutions Lock In Attractive Long-Term Yields

The government bond market was Tuesday’s busiest arena. The 25-year 13.25% bond alone traded face value of over TZS 39 billion, with prices ranging from par to a significant premium. Buyers at these levels are primarily pension funds and insurance companies seeking to match long-term liabilities with reliable, tax-free income.

Yields on the most actively traded maturities ranged from approximately 10.6% to 13.3%, depending on the specific bond and price. For an investor who can hold to maturity, these returns remain substantially higher than most bank deposit rates and comfortably above the rate of inflation.

## 6. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (August):

The equity market is consolidating after several weeks of block-driven activity. Trading volumes may remain moderate, with the large banking counters — CRDB and NMB — continuing to anchor the market.

NMB is at its all-time high. The ex-dividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 dividend has not yet been published. Historically, share prices can adjust downward once the books close and the entitlement to the dividend is removed.

VODA has eased back to the 1,010 area after a strong rally in late July. The stock appears to be finding a new range as investors digest the company’s results and dividend outlook.

Medium-Term (Q3 2026):

The Bank of Tanzania’s steady policy stance remains supportive of both equities and bonds. Any change in the benchmark interest rate would be the most significant macroeconomic event for the market.

The implementation of the government’s infrastructure spending will directly affect cement, construction, and logistics companies.

Long-Term:
The market’s ability to absorb large block trades, maintain stable prices, and simultaneously support a deep bond market is a sign of genuine maturity. For the patient, long-term investor, this quiet resilience is the foundation on which lasting wealth is built.

## 8. Considerations for a Multi-Year Framework

A quiet equity market alongside a busy bond market is not a warning sign; it is the mark of a financial system that is working exactly as it should. The DSE now has the depth to support both a liquid equity market and a thriving fixed-income market, all driven by local capital. For the multi-year investor, this balance is the real story — a stable, self-reinforcing market that can compound wealth patiently over time, without depending on the whims of foreign capital.
