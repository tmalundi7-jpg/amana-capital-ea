P: 3. In Focus: The 2.3-Million-Share ETF Block Trade — What It Teaches Us
P: Wednesday’s session saw a trade of 2,330,000 shares of the IEACLC-ETF, valued at roughly TZS 3.15 billion. That is an enormous transaction, larger than the total daily equity turnover in many sessions. Yet the price of the ETF barely moved, and the prices of the individual stocks held inside the ETF were not disturbed at all. How is that possible?
P: The answer lies in two important features of how the DSE operates. The first is the pre-arranged block trade. This is a privately negotiated transaction between two large institutions — perhaps a pension fund selling a large position to another institution, or a fund manager rebalancing its portfolio. By agreeing on a price and quantity privately, and then reporting the trade to the exchange through the pre-arranged board, the parties avoid flooding the open market with a huge order that could push prices sharply up or down.
P: The second is the nature of an ETF itself. An Exchange-Traded Fund is a basket of securities — typically shares or bonds — that trades on the stock exchange like a single stock. The IEACLC-ETF holds a diversified portfolio of Tanzanian equities. When an institution buys or sells a large block of the ETF, it is effectively buying or selling a tiny slice of every company in the fund. The individual companies are not affected because the trade happens at the ETF level, not in the shares of CRDB, NMB, or VODA directly.
P: For the everyday investor, a block trade like this is a signal of deep institutional activity, but not a reason to change course. It tells you that professional money is moving, and that the market’s infrastructure is robust enough to handle transactions worth billions of shillings without fuss. That quiet capability is a sign of a maturing exchange.

P: 4. Today’s Concept: The 5% Daily Price Band — Your Safety Net
P: On Wednesday, CRDB closed at 2,580. On Thursday, the stock cannot trade at any price outside a band of ±5% around that closing level. The limits are:
P: Upper limit: 2,580 × 1.05 = 2,709
P: Lower limit: 2,580 × 0.95 = 2,451
P: Any order to buy above 2,709 or to sell below 2,451 will be rejected by the exchange’s automated trading system. This rule is not a restriction designed to frustrate — it is a safety net that prevents extreme, panic-driven moves and ensures orderly price discovery, even on days when a giant block trade is taking place elsewhere.

P: 5. Bond Market: Institutions Pause After a Busy Start to August
P: Government bond turnover fell sharply to just TZS 3.5 billion, the quietest day in weeks. Activity was concentrated in the 20-year maturity, where the 12.25% bond and high-coupon 15.49% bonds changed hands in modest amounts.
