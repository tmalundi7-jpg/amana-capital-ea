P: 3. In Focus: Block Trades in a Calm Market — What They Tell Us
P: Thursday’s two large block trades — 400,600 CRDB shares and 395,000 VODA shares — might sound dramatic, but for the everyday investor they were barely noticeable. The price of CRDB moved only 10 shillings, and VODA closed just 1% higher. How is it possible for hundreds of thousands of shares to change hands without causing a stir?
P: The answer lies in the pre-arranged block trade mechanism. When a large institution wants to sell a big position, it does not simply dump the shares onto the open market — that would crash the price. Instead, it negotiates privately with a buyer (often another institution) and agrees on a price and quantity. The transaction is then reported to the exchange through the pre-arranged board, but the actual exchange of shares happens away from the public order book. The rest of the market sees the trade only after it is complete.
P: This is a sign of a healthy, maturing market. It means that even when large investors want to reposition their portfolios, they can do so without disrupting the prices that ordinary investors see. The fact that CRDB’s block was absorbed and the stock actually rose slightly suggests that there is steady buying interest at these levels, even if it has not yet been enough to push the price decisively higher.
P: For the everyday investor, a block trade is a piece of information, not a signal to act. It tells you that professional money is active, but it does not tell you whether that money is buying or selling the stock’s long-term prospects. Your own decisions should be based on your research, your goals, and your plan — not on what large institutions are doing behind closed doors.

P: 4. Today’s Concept: The 5% Daily Price Band — A Safety Net in Action
P: Thursday’s calm trading was also a direct result of a rule that protects every DSE investor: the 5% daily price band. Because CRDB closed at 2,590, on Friday the stock cannot trade at any price outside a range of ±5% around that level. The limits are:
P: Upper limit: 2,590 × 1.05 = 2,719.50
P: Lower limit: 2,590 × 0.95 = 2,460.50
P: Any order to buy above 2,719.50 or to sell below 2,460.50 will be automatically rejected by the DSE’s trading system. This rule is not a restriction designed to frustrate — it is a safety net that prevents panic-driven swings and ensures orderly price discovery, even when large block trades are taking place.

P: 5. Bond Market: Institutional Interest Returns to Government Paper
P: After a very quiet Wednesday, government bond turnover rebounded to TZS 12.47 billion. The busiest instrument was a 10-year bond with an 11.75% coupon, while the 15-year 12.25% bond and the 20-year 15.49% high-coupon paper also attracted steady demand. Yields on these long-dated bonds remain attractive at around 10.0–12.1%, entirely free of withholding tax. The bond market continues to provide a deep, liquid alternative for institutions seeking predictable, long-term income alongside their equity holdings.
P: 6. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate
P: Near-Term (August):
P: The market is consolidating after July’s extraordinary activity. Trading is likely to remain concentrated in the large banking and telecom counters, with CRDB, NMB, and VODA continuing to dominate volumes.
P: NMB remains at its all-time high. The ex-dividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 dividend has still not been published but is expected at any time. Historically, share prices can adjust downward once the books close and the entitlement to the dividend is removed.
P: VODA touched 1,030 intraday, showing that there is still buying interest above the 1,000 level. The company’s full-year results have now been absorbed by the market, and the stock may trade in a range as investors assess the longer-term growth outlook.
P: Medium-Term (Q3 2026):
P: The Bank of Tanzania’s steady policy stance continues to support both equities and bonds. Any change in the benchmark interest rate would be the most significant macroeconomic event.
P: The implementation of the government’s infrastructure spending will directly benefit cement, construction, and logistics companies.

The quiet absorption of large block trades, the near-complete absence of foreign selling, and the steady local participation are all signs of a market that has matured significantly in recent months. For the patient, long-term investor, this stability is the foundation on which sustainable wealth is built.
