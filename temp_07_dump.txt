P: 3. In Focus: Block Trades – Why They Matter and What They Don’t Tell You
P: Friday’s session included two pre-arranged block trades – 296,000 shares of CRDB and 27,700 shares of NMB. These were privately negotiated transactions between large institutions, reported on the exchange’s pre-arranged board but executed away from the open market. For the everyday investor, the price of CRDB did not flinch, and NMB barely moved.
P: A block trade is a signal of institutional activity, not a buy or sell recommendation. It tells you that professional money is on the move, but it does not tell you why, or whether the seller believes the stock is overvalued or the buyer sees long-term value. Often, block trades are simply portfolio rebalancing – a pension fund adjusting its weightings, or an insurance company shifting capital between asset classes.
P: What is important is that the market can absorb trades of this size without disruption. That is a sign of depth and maturity. For you as an investor, it reinforces the principle that you should make decisions based on your own research, your own financial goals, and a plan you have thought through in advance – not on what large institutions are doing in private.

P: 4. Today’s Concept: The 5% Daily Price Band – A Practical Tool
P: On Friday, CRDB closed at 2,590. That means on Monday, the stock cannot be traded at any price outside a band of ±5% around that closing level. The limits are:
P: Upper limit: 2,590 × 1.05 = 2,719.50
P: Lower limit: 2,590 × 0.95 = 2,460.50
P: Any order to buy above 2,719.50 or to sell below 2,460.50 will be automatically rejected by the DSE’s trading system. This rule protects investors by preventing extreme, panic-driven price moves and ensuring that trading is orderly, even on days when large block trades take place.

Before placing any trade, check the previous day’s closing price, calculate the 5% range, and ensure your price falls within it. This small habit can save you from placing orders that the exchange will reject, and it helps you set targets that the market can actually reach.

P: 6. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate
P: Near-Term (August):
P: The market appears to have found a calm, post-July rhythm. Trading remains concentrated in the large banking and telecom counters.
P: NMB is at an all-time high, with the ex-dividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 dividend still pending. When the books close, the share price may adjust downward by an amount roughly equal to the dividend – a normal, mechanical process that investors should understand in advance.
