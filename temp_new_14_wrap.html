3. In Focus: Block Trades and the Power of Local Demand

Friday’s three large block trades — in NMB, TCCL, and TPCC — highlight a key feature of the modern DSE: when large institutions need to move shares, they do so privately, away from the open market, and the local investor base absorbs the impact.

A block trade is a privately negotiated transaction between two institutions, typically executed on the exchange’s prearranged board. It allows a seller to offload a large position without flooding the order book and crashing the price. The fact that these trades occurred at prices close to the prevailing market level, and that the broader index still rose, indicates that demand from Tanzanian pension funds, insurance companies, and retail investors is deep enough to absorb institutional selling.

This is a dramatic change from the first half of 2026, when foreign investors were consistently net sellers and local buying was insufficient to prevent sharp declines. The market has since absorbed the largest ownership transfers in its history, and the results are now visible: a stable, locally anchored exchange that can weather external pressure without panic.

For the everyday investor, a block trade is not a signal to buy or sell. It is simply one part of the market’s daily mechanics. The more important lesson is that a market with deep domestic demand is fundamentally safer and more predictable than one that depends on international capital flows.

4. Today’s Concept: The 5% Daily Price Band — Your Safety Net

On Friday, CRDB closed at 2,700. That means on Monday, 17 August, the stock cannot trade at any price outside a band of ±5% around that closing level. The limits are:

Upper limit: 2,700 × 1.05 = 2,835

Lower limit: 2,700 × 0.95 = 2,565

Any order to buy above 2,835 or to sell below 2,565 will be automatically rejected by the DSE’s trading system. This rule applies to every actively traded stock, with a wider 15% band for a few smaller counters.

This mechanism ensures that even the largest trades occur in an orderly fashion. For you as an investor, it is a practical tool: before placing any order, check the previous day’s close, calculate the band, and set your limit within it. This prevents the frustration of submitting orders that the exchange will automatically reject.

5. Bond Market: A Quiet Week for Fixed Income

Government bond turnover fell to TZS 5.22 billion, the lowest of the week, as institutions took a breather after several active sessions. The 10year 11.44% bond and the 20year 12.25% bond were the most traded instruments, with yields ranging from about 9.3% to 13.6%, all free of withholding tax.

The bond market remains a deep, liquid alternative for investors seeking predictable, longterm income. Even on quieter days, the underlying demand from pension funds and insurance companies remains intact, providing a stable floor for the fixedincome market.

6. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate

Near-Term (August):

  • The market has built a solid base above 4,200. With local buying absorbing foreign sales comfortably, further gradual gains are possible, though volumes may remain uneven.
  • NMB is at an alltime high. The exdividend date for the approved TZS 610.15 dividend is overdue and could be announced at any time. When it is, the share price may adjust downward by an amount roughly equal to the dividend — a normal, mechanical process.
  • CRDB held the 2,700 level after Thursday’s rally, confirming that buying interest remains at these prices. A sustained move above 2,720 would signal a stronger recovery.
  • VODA continues to trade near 1,020, with support visible around 1,000. The stock appears to be stabilising after its lateJuly surge.

Medium-Term (Q3 2026):

  • The Bank of Tanzania’s steady policy stance remains supportive of both equities and bonds. Any change in the benchmark interest rate would be the most significant macroeconomic event.
  • The implementation of the government’s infrastructure spending will directly benefit cement, construction, and logistics companies, including TPCC and TCCL.

Long-Term:
The DSE’s ability to advance despite foreign selling in four of the last five sessions is a powerful indicator of structural change. A market that can rely on its own domestic capital is far more resilient than one that must wait for international investors to return. For the patient, longterm investor, this is the foundation on which sustainable wealth is built.
