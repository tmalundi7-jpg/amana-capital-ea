Daily DSE Wrap | Monday, 14th September 2026
Indices Hit New Post-Split Highs as Market Consolidates After Record Week
The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange opened the new week with a session that told two stories at once. On the surface, equity turnover fell sharply to TZS 6.33 billion — a 62% decline from Friday's block-heavy TZS 16.64 billion. Yet beneath that headline, both the All-Share Index and the Tanzania Share Index closed at new post-split highs, and the number of individual deals actually rose. The market is not running out of steam. It is catching its breath after an extraordinary week, and the underlying demand remains firmly intact.

The session remained in the HIGH turnover regime at TZS 6.33 billion, well above the TZS 3 billion threshold. The volume decline is a natural consequence of Friday's exceptional block activity — NMB alone traded 4.9 million shares in a single block. On Monday, there was no such block, so the volume figure returned to a more normal level.
The deal count tells the real story: 5,098 individual transactions, up 36% from Friday. This means more everyday investors were buying and selling, even as the total value traded fell. Broad participation remains healthy.
Indices at new highs: The DSEI closed at 4,689.98 — a new post-split high — while the TSI crossed 10,446.48. Both indices have now risen for five consecutive sessions. Market capitalisation reached a record TZS 40.88 trillion.
Foreign participation remains minimal: Foreign selling accounted for 13.70% of turnover (TZS 867 million), while foreign buying was just 0.46% (TZS 29 million). The net outflow of approximately TZS 838 million is a routine flow, not a structural exit. Local investors bought 99.54% and sold 86.30% of turnover — the market is entirely domestically anchored.

3. In Focus: New Highs, Balanced Order Books, and the 5% Daily Price Band
Monday's session provided a useful lesson in how markets behave after a period of intense activity. Last week was dominated by block trades — NMB absorbed 4.9 million shares on Friday alone, VODA 600,000, and TCC 114,000. This week opened with no major blocks, and turnover naturally fell.
But here's what matters: the indices still rose. The DSEI gained 31 points. The TSI gained 46 points. And the deal count increased by 36%. This tells us that the buying pressure is broad-based, not dependent on a handful of institutional transactions.
The order books also tell a story. CRDB shows 318,892 bids versus 269,938 offers — nearly balanced, with a slight edge to buyers. VODA shows 44,345 bids versus 18,789 offers — a 2.4x ratio favouring buyers. These are healthy, liquid markets where both sides can transact without difficulty.
For the daily price band, Monday's closing prices determine Tuesday's limits. Because CRDB closed at 2,980, it cannot trade on Tuesday, 15 September, outside a band of ±5% around that level:
Upper limit: 2,980 × 1.05 = 3,129.00
Lower limit: 2,980 × 0.95 = 2,831.00

4. Bond Market: Still Attracting Duration Demand
Government bond turnover was TZS 17.47 billion on Monday, slightly below Friday's TZS 19.43 billion but still elevated. The most liquid instruments were:
20-year 12.25% bond (Jul 2026): TZS 6.30 billion at 107.90% to 114.50%, yielding 10.73% to 11.51%
10-year 11.44% bond (Sep 2021): TZS 5.00 billion at 114.10%, yielding 9.21%
25-year 15.00% bond (Aug 2025): TZS 3.10 billion at 114.15% to 138.39%, yielding 10.69% to 13.24%
25-year 13.25% bond (Feb 2026): TZS 1.11 billion at 137.43%, yielding 9.50%
The message is consistent: institutions are still locking in long-dated, tax-free yields. The 20-year 12.25% bond offers over 11% tax-free at the higher end of its range. The 10-year 11.44% bond at 9.21% is the tightest yield on record for that maturity.
What does this mean for everyday investors?
The bond market and equity market are both attracting capital simultaneously. This is not a one-way rotation; it is a two-way liquidity environment. For those seeking predictable income, the 20-year bond at 11% tax-free remains attractive. For those seeking growth, the equity market is offering new highs and liquid order books.
A balanced approach — some capital in bonds for stability, some in quality equities for growth — can work well in this environment.
5. Strategic Outlook: What to Anticipate
Near-Term (Mid-September):
CRDB closed at 2,980, just below the 3,000 mark. The order book is balanced, and the stock has risen for five consecutive sessions. A pullback to 2,950 would represent a natural pause.
NMB closed at 2,180 with an offer-heavy order book (417,461 offers vs. 115,242 bids). The ex-dividend date for the TZS 61.015 per share dividend remains pending. Caution is warranted.
VODA closed flat at 1,110 with a 2.4x bid/offer ratio. The expected FY2025 dividend announcement remains the key catalyst.
TCC continues to rally into its ex-dividend window, closing at 13,500.
TOL rebounded 5.7% after last week's decline. This counter remains volatile; avoid chasing.
Medium-Term (Q3 2026):
The bond market and equity market are both attracting liquidity. Watch for a contraction in bond turnover below TZS 10 billion for two consecutive sessions while equity turnover remains elevated — that would signal a clearer rotation.
The Bank of Tanzania's monetary policy stance remains supportive. Any rate cuts would further compress bond yields and support equity valuations.
Long-Term:The events of the past two weeks — the NMB share split, the CRDB block trades, the bond yield compression, and the foreign flow reversal — all point in the same direction: the DSE has entered a new era. Local capital is firmly in control, institutional participation is growing, and the market is demonstrating depth and resilience that would have been unthinkable a year ago. For the patient, long-term investor, this is a market that rewards discipline, education, and the willingness to let compounding work.
6. Professional Approach: How to Use This Intelligence
Combine the Daily Wrap with our education series. If the price-band or order-book concepts are new to you, read Stocks, Bonds, and ETFs and Understanding Market Data.
Understand what falling turnover means. A decline in turnover after a block-heavy session is normal. Look at the deal count — if it remains high, broad participation is intact.
Respect the daily price bands. They are designed to keep trading orderly and prevent panic-driven moves. Always calculate the allowed range before placing an order.
Watch the order books. When offers exceed bids (as in NMB), the buying pressure is easing. When bids far exceed offers (as in VODA), demand is strong.
Don't chase volatile counters. TOL's 5.7% rebound after an 11.6% decline is a reminder that speculative moves can reverse quickly.
Think in years, not days. Daily rallies and pullbacks are part of normal market behaviour. The steady, compounding growth of well-managed companies over many years is what builds real wealth.
Remember that all investments carry risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and always do your own research.
